# PHYS 2022 agent instructions

- A tutorial session works through one or more questions. Record each question in the session's `tutorial.md` as a question block (see `academic-ingest` for the cloze format).
- A recorded tutorial question may be a recovery rather than the original wording. The concept each question demonstrates is what has to match; its phrasing may differ.
- A weekly project is a PhET-simulation investigation submitted on Canvas before its due date. Each lives under `assignments/project N/` with the prompt PDF in `attachments/`, Canvas metadata in `submission.yml` and `index.md`, and the physical relationships the project develops in the `project N.md` child note.

## what to record from any material

- Record every experiment a textbook chapter, lecture, tutorial, or mini-project develops: the photoelectric effect, Compton scattering, Franck–Hertz, Rutherford scattering, Davisson–Germer, the black-body cavity, spectral series, and the PhET mini-project investigations.
- Record the technique each recorded experiment stands for, named and placed among its family: emission or absorption spectroscopy, photoemission, electron diffraction, backscattering spectrometry. One family holds many types, and the type is what generalizes beyond the single apparatus. A technique with its own Wikipedia page becomes a standalone topic note; any other technique is recorded inside the experiment's note.
- Record the picture a reader should hold: a potential well as walls, the alpha swinging past the nucleus, the wave packet as the particle.
- Record the definitional diagrams: the drawings that carry a setup or fix a convention.
- Record the major results and what each of them means.
- Record how d, λ, T, V₀, n, or the charges shift the measured result.
- Record the qualitative results: trends without their numbers.
- Keep history brief: dates, names, and discovery narratives get one clause when they date a result. Notes and cards ask about the physics, not who found it when.

## decisions that carry across rounds

- When sources disagree, look up the best current value first, and tell an accurate value (a constant, a measurement) from a historical one (what a source reported at the time): the current value goes in the prose, the historical value stays beside it labeled historical, no source chosen silently.
- Before creating a note or splitting one, write the proposal first: the name, what it would hold, what moves, which sections cross-link. Build only what was approved.
- Link a section born outside a session from a week entry when that week's syllabus topic covers it.
