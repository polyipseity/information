# electron energy loss spectroscopy

_Electron energy loss spectroscopy_ (EELS) exposes a material to a beam of electrons with a known, narrow range of kinetic energies and reads the spectrum of energies they leave with. Most of the electrons scatter elastically and keep their energy. Some lose fixed amounts in inelastic scattering, and those losses name the excitations the material supports.

The technique belongs to electron spectroscopy, the group of methods that analyse the energies of electrons. The [Franck–Hertz experiment](Franck%E2%80%93Hertz%20experiment.md) measures the same thing in simple dress: drifting electrons surrendering one fixed $4.9\ \text{eV}$ at a time inside mercury vapour. EELS runs that measurement on arbitrary materials, with an electron spectrometer recording the whole loss spectrum instead of one current drop.

## energy-loss features

The inelastic losses come at fixed energies, one per excitation: phonon excitations, inter- and intra-band transitions, plasmon excitations, and inner-shell ionisations. A peak in the loss spectrum at a known energy is read off as the excitation that took it.

Inner-shell ionisations identify elements. An electron arriving $285\ \text{eV}$ down carries about the energy it takes to remove a carbon atom's inner-shell electron, so a larger-than-expected count of electrons at that loss is evidence of significant carbon in the sample. Surveying a wide range of energy losses with care determines the types of atoms in the sample and the numbers of each type the beam strikes.

## scattering angle

The deflection of each electron can be measured along with its energy loss. The scattering angle against the loss energy gives the dispersion relation of whatever excitation took the energy, so one measurement reads both what the material excites and how that excitation propagates.
