# spectroscopy

Spectroscopy spreads radiation out after it has met matter and records how much arrives at each wavelength, frequency, or energy. The positions of the features identify what is there: a line sits where the transitions of one particular substance can put it. The intensities say how much is present, since a substance emits or absorbs at a wavelength in proportion to how much of it there is. Families divide the field by what goes into the measurement and what comes out of it. One family holds many types, and the type is what still holds when the apparatus changes.

## type families

- [Emission spectroscopy](emission%20spectrum.md) reads a substance from the wavelengths it gives off when excited.
- [Absorption spectroscopy](absorption%20spectrum.md) reads a substance from the wavelengths a beam loses passing through it.
- [X-ray spectroscopy](X-ray.md) reads an excited target from the characteristic line energies it emits.
- [Photoemission spectroscopy](photoemission%20spectroscopy.md) reads the binding energies of a material's electrons from the kinetic energies of the electrons light frees from it.
- Mass spectrometry is outside the light-reading families: it sorts ionized fragments by mass-to-charge ratio rather than reading a spectrum of radiation.
