# index

- name: Modern Physics
- credits: 3

PHYS 2022 is an undergraduate course in modern physics at HKUST covering relativity, quantum theory (wave–particle duality and the Schrödinger equation), atoms and molecules, and statistical physics (Maxwell, Bose, and Fermi distributions).

## overview

- official course outline
    - the birth of modern physics
    - the experimental basis of quantum physics
    - the structure of the atom
    - wave properties of matter and quantum mechanics
    - quantum mechanics
    - the hydrogen atom
    - atomic physics
    - molecules, lasers, and solids
    - semiconductor theory and devices
    - the atomic nucleus
    - particle physics
    - the theory of special relativity
- topic-to-file mapping
    - foundations
        - [physics](physics.md)
        - [conservation law](conservation%20law.md)
    - pre-1900 physics
        - [classical physics](classical%20physics.md)
        - [fundamental interaction](fundamental%20interaction.md)
- intended learning outcomes
    - describe the experimental evidence for quantum physics, including the photoelectric effect, atomic spectra, and electron diffraction
    - explain wave–particle duality, the Bohr atomic model, the uncertainty principle, and solutions to the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation
    - explain the structure and development of atomic, molecular, solid-state, and nuclear models
    - apply Lorentz transformations and the concepts of special relativity (reference frames, space-time interval, relativistic energy, and relativistic momentum) to solve physical problems
    - discuss the fundamental concepts of modern astrophysics and general relativity
    - apply the principles of modern physics to analyse and solve practical problems
- notes
    - the instructor's study advice: self-study the textbook chapter before class to work out what you do and do not understand; in class, ask and answer questions, discuss with classmates, and do exercises instead of just listening; build a physics mindset by linking abstract concepts and formulas to real-world phenomena through virtual experiments
