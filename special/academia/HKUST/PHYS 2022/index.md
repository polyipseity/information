# index

- HKUST PHYS 2022
- name: Modern Physics
- credits: 3

---

PHYS 2022 is an undergraduate course in modern physics. It covers relativity, quantum theory, atoms and molecules, and statistical physics. In quantum theory it treats wave–particle duality and the Schrödinger equation; in statistical physics, the Maxwell, Bose, and Fermi distributions.

## logistics

- prerequisites: one of PHYS 1114 or PHYS 1314
- cross-campus course equivalence: AMAT 2050
- attributes: blended learning
- textbook: _Modern Physics for Scientists and Engineers_ (Stephen T. Thornton, Andrew Rex, and Carol Hood; CENGAGE; fifth edition)
- grading
    - homework: 20%; five textbook end-of-chapter problems per submission; nine of the eleven submissions count
    - mini projects: 20%; five of the ten projects count; whether project 1 counts is the student's choice, and projects submitted after it count automatically
    - tutorials: 20%; eight of the ten submissions count; each submission is worth 10 points for the answer and 10 for evaluating a peer
    - mid-term examination: 20%; open book
    - final examination: 20%; open book
- sections:
    - lecture: L1
        - L1: Lecture Theater G; TuesdayT13:30:00/TuesdayT14:50:00, ThursdayT13:30:00/ThursdayT14:50:00
    - tutorials: T1
        - T1: G009B, CYT Bldg; FridayT11:30:00/FridayT12:20:00
        - T2: Rm 1410, Lift 25-26; MondayT14:00:00/MondayT14:50:00
        - T3: Rm 1410, Lift 25-26; MondayT12:00:00/MondayT12:50:00
- note: homework follows the format of [`HW sample.pdf`](attachments/HW%20sample.pdf); mini projects follow [`Project sample.pdf`](attachments/Project%20sample.pdf)
- note: a mini project with more than one simulation needs only one of them, chosen freely, and its textbook formula may be compared against any standard reference
- note: a mini project report sums its data in one equation with "=" fitted from the data alone, compares that equation quantitatively with the textbook one, fits by physical sense rather than software precision, and attaches the captured screens as the original data
- note: both examinations take printed hardcopies; electronic versions are not allowed
- note: marks earned during the add-drop period may be included in the final mark

- intended learning outcomes
    - describe the experimental evidence for quantum physics, including the photoelectric effect, atomic spectra, and electron diffraction
    - explain wave–particle duality, the Bohr atomic model, the uncertainty principle, and solutions to the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation
    - explain the structure and development of atomic, molecular, solid-state, and nuclear models
    - apply Lorentz transformations and the concepts of special relativity (reference frames, space-time interval, relativistic energy, and relativistic momentum) to solve physical problems
    - discuss the fundamental concepts of modern astrophysics and general relativity
    - apply the principles of modern physics to analyse and solve practical problems
- notes
    - the instructor's study advice: work out before class what the textbook chapter does and does not make clear; in class, ask and answer questions, discuss with classmates, and work the exercises rather than listen; tie abstract concepts and formulas to real phenomena through virtual experiments
