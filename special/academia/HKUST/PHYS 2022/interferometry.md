# interferometry

_Interferometry_ is an interference technique: it splits a wave into two beams, sends them along different paths, and recombines them, and the fringe pattern the recombination leaves reports the difference between the paths. Where the beams recombine in step the result is bright, and where they arrive opposed it is dark, so a path difference of one wavelength moves the pattern by one fringe. A difference too small to see directly then appears as a fraction of a fringe, which carries length measurement down to a fraction of a wavelength. The wave may be any that interferes; light is the usual beam.

## Michelson interferometer

A Michelson interferometer splits the light from one source with a half-silvered mirror, sends the two beams down paths at right angles, and reflects each back so the two recombine. Whichever arm the light takes longer in returns with its fringes displaced, and rotating the apparatus exchanges the two arms. The 1887 Michelson–Morley experiment used that exchange to look for the ether: the Earth's motion through it was expected to change which arm the round trip took longer in, so the fringes would move as the apparatus turned. No shift appeared, although the experiment was sensitive enough to reveal the ether's effects, and the [search for the ether](electromagnetic%20radiation.md#medium%20and%20the%20aether) continued with other methods.

## measurements

An interferometer measures whatever changes one path against the other. A mirror displaced by $\delta$ changes its arm's path by $2\delta$ on the way out and back, so a displacement of $\lambda/2$ shifts the fringes once and a shift of a tenth of a fringe is a twentieth of a wavelength. Filling one arm with a gas of refractive index $n$ lengthens that arm's optical path from $L$ to $nL$ without moving anything, and the fringe shift gives $n$. Height variations along a surface carried on one arm come back as fringes.
