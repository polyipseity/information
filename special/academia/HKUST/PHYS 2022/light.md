# light

_Light_ is electromagnetic radiation, the part of the electromagnetic spectrum that the eye can see. Whether it travels as a wave or as a shower of particles was argued for a century: Newton's particle view gave way in 1801, when the double-slit experiment produced behaviour the particle model cannot account for, and Maxwell later identified light with the [electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic%20radiation.md) of his theory.

## wave and particle views

The two views differ over what light does at an obstruction, and they were not judged equal at first.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- which view was accepted first ::@:: Newton's particle view.

### the wave view

The wave view, associated with Christiaan Huygens (1629–1695), has light travelling through space like the ripples spreading across the surface of a still pond after a dropped rock. Waves bend into a shadow, so light should spread past an obstacle instead of leaving a hard edge behind it.

- the wave view of light: what it predicts at an obstacle ::@:: Waves bend into the shadow.

### the particle view

The particle view, associated with Isaac Newton (1642–1727), has light travelling as a shower of particles that go in a straight line until an obstacle deflects, reflects, or absorbs them. Particles produce straight shadows.

## Young's double-slit experiment

Thomas Young passed light through two narrow slits in 1801. A shower of particles would leave two bright lines, but the light formed a pattern of bright bands, which is interference between waves arriving from the two slits. The wave view was accepted from then on, and Maxwell later showed that the wave is the electromagnetic wave predicted by his equations.
