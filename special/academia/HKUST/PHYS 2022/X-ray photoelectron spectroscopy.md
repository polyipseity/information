# X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) is the type of [photoemission spectroscopy](photoemission%20spectroscopy.md) that works with X-ray photons. It irradiates a material with X-rays, frees electrons by the [photoelectric effect](photoelectric%20effect.md), and measures their kinetic energies. X-ray photons carry the energy to reach the tightly bound core levels, and each element holds its own set of them. Their binding energies place the peaks where no other element puts them, so a spectrum names the elements present. Hydrogen and helium are the exception: neither has an inner shell below its outermost electrons. The reading reaches only the top 5–10 nm of the surface.

## chemical shift

A core level does not sit at exactly one binding energy: the atom's chemical state moves it. An atom bonded to electronegative neighbours holds its electrons tighter, so its peak slides to higher binding energy. The size of the slide says what the element is bonded to.
