# inertial frame

An _inertial frame_ is a reference frame in which a body keeps a constant velocity unless a net external force acts on it. Newton's first law of motion holds there, and that law defines the inertial frames: a body at rest is the case of zero velocity. A frame accelerating relative to an inertial frame is non-inertial, and its motion obeys Newton's second law only once fictitious forces join the real ones.

## Galilean transformation

Two inertial frames at constant relative velocity $v$ along the $x$ axis convert each other's measurements through the Galilean transformation: $x' = x - vt$, $y' = y$, $z' = z$, and $t' = t$. The frames share one time, and a velocity $v_x$ along the $x$ axis reads $v_x - v$ in the frame moving at $v$.

Every law of mechanics keeps its form on both sides of the transformation. That is Galilean invariance. The two frames stand alike under it, so no experiment confined to one inertial frame detects its uniform motion through space. No inertial frame is absolute, and no state of motion through space is marked out as the one at rest.

Galilean invariance fails where the relative speed of the frames approaches the speed of light. Maxwell's equations do not keep their form under a Galilean transformation. Special relativity replaces it with the Lorentz transformation, whose invariance leaves every inertial frame equal again.
