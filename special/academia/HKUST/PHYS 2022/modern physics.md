# modern physics

_Modern physics_ is the physics that grew out of the discoveries of the 1890s and the questions they left open.

## unresolved questions of 1895

The controversy over [atomic theory](history%20of%20atomic%20theory.md) raised questions of its own: it was not universally accepted, the constituents (if any) of atoms became a significant question, and the structure of matter remained unknown with certainty.

Three further problems were left open. The first was the existence of an electromagnetic medium, the "ether". The second was the observed difference in the electric and magnetic field between stationary and moving reference systems, which is the problem of the speed of light. The third was the failure of classical physics to explain blackbody radiation.

## discoveries of 1895 to 1898

Wilhelm Röntgen, a German physicist, discovered X-rays on 8 November 1895, and received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1901. Pieter Zeeman, a Dutch physicist, found the Zeeman effect in 1896 and received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1902. J. J. Thomson, a British physicist, studied cathode rays using an improved evacuated tube and showed they were negatively charged particles by deflecting them in electric and magnetic fields. He measured their charge-to-mass ratio and found it was independent of cathode material, leading to the discovery of the electron. By 1899 he showed that the same negatively charged particles were produced by radioactive materials, heated materials, and illuminated materials. Henri Becquerel, a French physicist, discovered radioactivity in 1896, and Marie and Pierre Curie took it further in 1898; the three shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1903. Robert Millikan determined the electron charge through the oil drop experiment around 1909, measuring $e = 1.592 \times 10^{-19}$ C.

Thomson's experiment used a Crookes tube: a glass envelope evacuated to low pressure with a cathode at one end and an anode at the other. A high voltage across the terminals produced cathode rays (a visible glow). Thomson showed these rays travelled in straight lines by placing a Maltese cross in their path, which cast a sharp shadow on the phosphorescent screen behind it. He then applied electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to the ray direction. The electric field deflected the rays toward the positive plate, showing they carried negative charge. The magnetic field deflected them in the opposite direction. By adjusting the two fields until the net deflection was zero, he could determine the velocity $v = E/B$ and from the electric deflection alone the charge-to-mass ratio $q/m$. The result was the same regardless of the cathode material or the gas in the tube, establishing the electron as a universal constituent of atoms.

## revision of the classical foundations

The discoveries of the decade and the complications that followed required a revision of the fundamental physical assumptions that had made [classical physics](classical%20physics.md) succeed. The revision starts with two theories, the theory of relativity and quantum mechanics.

## open problems

The map of physics divides into classical physics, relativity, and quantum physics. Beyond them the questions are still open: quantum gravity, dark matter, and dark energy are unexplained, and the proposed routes to quantum gravity include string theory and loop quantum gravity.
