## problems

> Consider a classical hydrogen atom model consisting of an electron (mass $m = 9.109 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$, charge $-e$ where $e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$) in a circular orbit of radius $r$ (the hydrogen atom radius, $r = 0.53\ \text{Å}$) around a stationary proton (charge $+e$), using vacuum permittivity $\epsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\text{ F/m}$ and speed of light $c = 2.998 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$: use electrostatic and centripetal force balance to determine the orbital speed $v$, calculate the system's total mechanical energy $E$, find the emitted electromagnetic wave frequency from the orbital acceleration, and compute the time to collapse by linearly dividing the total energy magnitude by the Larmor radiation power loss $P = \frac{2}{3}\frac{e^2 a^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 c^3}$.
>
> - solution: the orbital speed is {@{$v = \sqrt{e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 m r)} = 2.19 \times 10^6\ \text{m/s}$ at $r = 0.53\ \text{Å}$}@}; the total mechanical energy is {@{$E = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} = -\frac{e^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0 r} = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J} = -13.6\ \text{eV}$}@}; the emitted electromagnetic wave frequency is {@{the orbital frequency $f = v/(2\pi r) = 6.56 \times 10^{15}\ \text{Hz}$}@}; the collapse time is {@{$t = |E|/P = 12\pi^2\epsilon_0^2 m^2 r^3 c^3/e^4 = 4.7 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{s}$}@}
> - explanation: {@{The Coulomb attraction}@} supplies {@{the centripetal force}@}, so {@{$mv^2/r = e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2)$}@} fixes {@{the speed at $v = \sqrt{e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 m r)}$ once the radius is given}@}. The kinetic energy is {@{$\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = e^2/(8\pi\epsilon_0 r)$}@}, and the potential is {@{$-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$, twice as large and negative}@}, so the total energy is {@{half the potential, $E = -e^2/(8\pi\epsilon_0 r)$}@}. A circular orbit {@{accelerates at $a = v^2/r$ and radiates at the orbital frequency $v/(2\pi r)$}@}. Putting $a$ into $P$ gives {@{$P = e^6/(96\pi^3\epsilon_0^3 m^2 r^4 c^3)$}@}, which {@{grows as the orbit shrinks}@}.
