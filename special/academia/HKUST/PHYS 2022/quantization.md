# quantization

Quantization is discreteness where a classical account would allow a continuum: the quantity sits on one allowed value or the next, and nothing lies between the steps. Electric charge comes in units of one basic charge, an atom holds a fixed ladder of energy levels, and an oscillator of frequency $f$ inside a cavity holds only the energies $E = nhf$.

## electric charge

Millikan's oil-drop experiment showed electric charge to be quantized: the droplets carried whole multiples of one basic charge, so every observed charge is $q = ne$ for an integer $n$, with the electron carrying $-e$ and $e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ C taken positive. Modern theories put quarks at $\pm e/3$ and $\pm 2e/3$. No free quark has ever been seen alone, so the charges of observed hadrons and other directly observed particles stay whole multiples of $e$. See [modern physics](modern%20physics.md) for Millikan's measurement of $e$.

## line spectra

An excited gas emits at a fixed set of wavelengths rather than a continuous band, and the discreteness comes from the atom's energy being quantized. A photon carries away one gap of that ladder, $hf = E_u - E_l$ with $E_u$ and $E_l$ the two level energies, so the emitted frequencies are the gaps the atom has and nothing between them. Hydrogen's lines sit where the [Balmer formula](Balmer%20series.md) puts them, and the [Bohr model](Bohr%20model.md) reproduced those wavelengths from an atom confined to a ladder of levels. [emission spectrum](emission%20spectrum.md) explains why an atom shows only those lines.

## energy quanta in a cavity

The radiation inside a cavity is emitted and absorbed by oscillators in the walls, and classical physics lets an oscillator of frequency $f$ carry any energy at all. Planck quantizes that energy: the oscillator holds only $E = nhf$ for an integer $n$, in steps of $hf$, and it exchanges energy in units of $hf$ as well. The ladder and what it fixes for the black-body spectrum sit in [Planck's law](Planck%27s%20law.md#two%20modifications), and [Max Planck](Max%20Planck.md) records how the quantum came about.
