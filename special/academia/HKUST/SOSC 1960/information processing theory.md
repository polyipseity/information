# information processing theory

Information processing theory explains cognitive development by the mental processes that produce thinking at any one time, and by the transition processes that lead to growth in that thinking. It is one of the main families of cognitive development theory, alongside [stage theories](developmental%20stage%20theories.md) and [sociocultural theories](sociocultural%20theory.md), and grew out of the American experimental tradition. David Klahr's theory is one instance of the family.

- whose theory is an instance of information processing theory ::@:: David Klahr's.

## processes of thinking

### mental processes

The mental processes produce the thinking a child has at any one time. A child's answer is explained by the processes that produced it.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what the mental processes of information processing theory produce ::@:: The thinking a child has at any one time.
- what explains a child's answer in information processing theory ::@:: The mental processes that produced the answer.

### transition processes

Transition processes lead to growth in that thinking. The family treats the growth as maturational change in the basic components of a child's mind. Development is a change in the processes themselves.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what transition processes lead to ::@:: Growth in the thinking that the mental processes produce.
- what information processing theory calls development ::@:: A change in the processes that produce thinking.

### continuous development

The approach emphasises continuous development, in contrast with [Jean Piaget's stages of cognitive development](Piaget's%20theory%20of%20cognitive%20development.md). Growth is a change in the components the mind already has, so it comes in small steps rather than in jumps between stages.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- how information processing theory treats development ::@:: As continuous, in contrast with the stages of Jean Piaget's theory.

## processing account

The mind works like a computer: input, then processing, then output. Input is what the senses receive, processing is what happens in between, and output is what the child does or says. Thinking is a sequence of steps rather than a single act.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- how the mind works in information processing theory ::@:: Like a computer: input, then processing, then output.

### standard model

In the standard model, attention brings information in, [working memory](memory.md#working%20memory) holds and manipulates it, and long-term memory stores it.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the three parts of the standard model of information processing ::@:: Attention, working memory, and long-term memory, in that order.

### information stores

The stores differ in capacity and duration. Sensory memory holds input briefly. Short-term memory holds about seven items, plus or minus two, for 15 to 20 seconds, George Miller's count. Long-term memory holds an unlimited amount, for years.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the capacity and duration of short-term memory ::@:: About seven items, plus or minus two, for 15 to 20 seconds.
- the capacity and duration of long-term memory ::@:: An unlimited amount, for years.

### cognitive processes

Cognitive processes move information between the stores. Perception interprets the environment. Coding writes information into long-term memory, and retrieval brings it back out. Recoding regroups information, and chunking is the clearest case.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what coding and retrieval do with long-term memory ::@:: Coding writes information into long-term memory, and retrieval brings it back out.
- what recoding does in information processing theory ::@:: It regroups information, and chunking is the clearest case.

## executive cognition

Executive cognition is the person's awareness of how they process information, close to metacognition. It covers their strengths and weaknesses.

---
