# motivated skepticism

Motivated skepticism is doubt about evidence that contradicts what a person wants to believe, however strong the evidence (Ditto & Lopez, 1992). Hot cognition is thinking shaped by desires and feelings, not by evidence alone. A directional goal, the motivation to reach a particular outcome or judgment, produces motivated skepticism. People trusted a medical test that reported a deficiency less than one that reported health.
