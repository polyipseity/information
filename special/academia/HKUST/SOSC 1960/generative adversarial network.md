# generative adversarial network

A generative adversarial network (GAN) is a deep learning model that produces new images, such as faces. Two networks compete during training, one making the images and the other judging them. Its output carries its training set's statistics. People struggle to tell that output from a photograph.

## generation and detection

### two networks

The generator makes new images and the discriminator tries to spot them as fake (Goodfellow et al., 2014). The generator aims at an image the discriminator misses, and training improves both by turns. Its output is new stimuli with its training set's statistics, so the network generates rather than classifies.

### telling fakes

The fakes the network generates are hard to tell from a real photograph (Miller et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2021). Because they look convincing, they matter to societies as much as to research.

## research use

A generative network holds face identity fixed while varying one perceptual trait, so a face trait theory becomes something a researcher can manipulate. Sobieszek, Siemiątkowski and Imbir (2024) generated faces along each dimension of a computationally defined face space for people to rate. They tested whether dominance and trustworthiness, the two primary traits of [first impression](first%20impression.md), are independent.
