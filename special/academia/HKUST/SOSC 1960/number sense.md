# number sense

Number sense is knowledge of numerical magnitudes, the sizes that numbers refer to. It is closely related to mathematics achievement test scores.

Children from low-income families enter kindergarten knowing less about numbers than children from wealthier families. The proposed explanation is a difference in numerical activity at home: middle- and upper-income families play numerical board games such as Chutes and Ladders more often.

## numerical board-game intervention

### numbered board

Every square of Chutes and Ladders carries a number. A player starts on square one and spins a spinner or throws a die, which sets how many squares the token moves.

The game teaches magnitude because a higher square agrees with several other quantities at once. The higher the square a token reaches, the farther it has travelled from the start, the more physical moves the child made to get there, the more number words the child has said and heard, and the longer the game has been running. These spatial, kinesthetic, verbal, and time cues point the same way, so the board gives a multisensory foundation for knowing how large a number is.

### gains from play

About an hour of play, spread over two weeks, raised low-income children's knowledge of numerical magnitudes, their reading of printed numbers, and their skill at learning novel arithmetic problems. The gains were still present months later. The intervention costs almost nothing, since a parent can draw the game on a sheet of paper.
