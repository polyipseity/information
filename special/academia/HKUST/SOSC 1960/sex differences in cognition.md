# sex differences in cognition

Sex differences in cognition are differences between the sexes in scores on cognitive tests. They are small, and they show in the averages rather than in every person. The spread within one sex is wider than the gap between the two, so a group average says nothing about an individual.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- where the sex differences in cognition appear ::@:: In group averages, not in every individual of either sex.
- what a group average says about one person ::@:: Nothing, since the spread within a group is wider than the gap between groups.

## shape of the difference

### variability

Males have the larger variance in scores, which puts more of them at both ends of the distribution: more of the highest scores and more of the lowest. A larger spread at the top alone produces a lopsided picture of who is capable, even where the two averages match.

Brain size explains little. A larger brain is not a faster one, and brain size does not track the difference in scores.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- which sex shows the larger variance in scores ::@:: Males, which puts more males at both the highest and lowest scores.
- what a larger spread at the top produces ::@:: A lopsided picture of who is capable, even where the two averages match.
- why brain size explains little ::@:: A larger brain is not a faster one, and brain size does not track the difference in scores.

### verbal and visual-spatial ability

The difference runs in opposite directions across two abilities: women score higher on verbal tests, and men score higher on visual-spatial ones. Each gap is small next to the variation within one sex, so neither predicts what a given man or woman will do.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- which sex scores higher on each ability ::@:: Women on verbal tests, and men on visual-spatial ones.
- how much a sex difference predicts about one person ::@:: Little, since each gap is small next to the variation within one sex.

## stereotype threat

Stereotype threat is the drop in performance that a stereotype about one's group can cause. A woman told that women do worse on the task carries a doubt she did not arrive with. The doubt costs her the performance the stereotype predicted, so the belief about the group helps produce the difference it describes.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what stereotype threat is ::@:: The drop in performance that a stereotype about one's own group can cause.
- how a stereotype about women's performance works ::@:: The woman carries a doubt she did not arrive with, and it costs her performance.
- why stereotype threat matters for group differences ::@:: The belief about the group helps produce the group difference it describes.
