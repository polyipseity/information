# electroencephalography

An electroencephalogram records the brain's own electrical activity. The scalp electrodes do not produce that activity. They measure a voltage difference between two points on the head. That difference is made of the summed postsynaptic activity of the neurons underneath.

The method reads the activity itself, so it pins down when something happened well. It cannot show where in the brain that activity came from, because the field reaching one electrode could have come from almost anywhere.

A raw recording is a mass of overlapping activity. An event-related potential comes from averaging segments of it, each lined up with a specific sensory, cognitive, or motor event.

Averaging cancels whatever has no fixed relationship to the event. The stereotyped response survives as a waveform with named deflections. People mix up a deflection's shape and its sign.

## reading the waveform

Time runs left to right, from the event to half a second after it. The vertical axis carries voltage. The axis runs the opposite way round from the usual: negative goes up, positive goes down. A peak above the baseline is negative.

The letter says what the deflection is, not when it happens. P is positive and N is negative. The number after the letter gives the approximate latency in tens of milliseconds.

P1 is the first positive deflection, near 80 ms. N2 is the second negative, near 230 ms. P3 is the third positive, near 400 ms, and it is the largest and the latest.
