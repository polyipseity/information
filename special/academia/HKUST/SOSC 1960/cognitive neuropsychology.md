# cognitive neuropsychology

This field works out how healthy cognition is organized by studying what brain damage takes away. One patient fails one task after a known lesion and does everything else normally. Two patients failing in opposite ways do more: one reads printed words badly but takes in spoken ones fine, and another does the reverse. That is a double dissociation, and it shows that reading and comprehension are separate parts of the mind rather than one faculty.

Nobody can order a stroke, so nobody can assign the lesions. Every patient arrives with a brain that was already itself.

## what a lesion can and cannot show

A patient sees faces badly after damage to one region and sees everything else normally. The region contributes to face recognition. The evidence does not show it doing the job alone. The region may be one station in a chain, and the damage may knock out the chain rather than the station.

Damage shows a deficit, not a signal, and a theory describes a deficit. A structure that still works says nothing about what it contributes to the function. Only a structure that is gone and takes a capacity with it shows that the capacity depended on it.

A patient who cannot recognize faces may be failing for reasons that have nothing to do with faces. Poor acuity, a lapse of attention, and the language needed to attempt the task would each produce the same score. The task sets a second limit: it has to isolate the capacity from those three.

## the two cautions

The first caution asks whether the patients were typical before the damage. A group already below average before the injury will have post-injury scores that say little about what the injury cost. Record the pre-damage level where a record survives, compare against a matched control group, or state which comparison was available.

The second caution asks whether a compensatory strategy is doing the work the missing region used to do. A patient may solve a task by a route intact people never use, because a brain that has lost a station in a chain reorganizes around the loss. The reorganization can carry the score being measured, and the score then describes a reorganized brain rather than the undamaged architecture the theory is about. Where a true loss cannot be separated from a learned substitute, state the claim with the ambiguity intact.

## necessity and sufficiency

Necessity claims the function could not be performed without the region. Sufficiency claims the region alone would perform it. Either can hold without the other, and evidence for one never settles the other. Treating them as one claim causes most of the difficulty here.

A coil sits over the parietal cortex while someone does a selective attention task. That is [transcranial magnetic stimulation](transcranial%20magnetic%20stimulation.md). Stimulation can be switched off and damage cannot, so the same person is compared across normal and disrupted conditions. Reaction time and accuracy are measured before, during, and after stimulation. A drop in parietal performance establishes necessity. The design needs no pre-injury baseline, which a patient case cannot supply.

Stimulation brings noise and scalp sensation, and a control site unrelated to attention rules both out. The drop must be absent when that site is stimulated the same way.

No patient can be asked to carry a lesion, and a [computational model](computational%20model.md) can be damaged on purpose. Damaging one component of a model of face recognition drops its accuracy on Asian faces and leaves it unchanged on Caucasian ones. The component is necessary within the model for one function and not another, a narrow role, and the claim stops there. A component that impairs everything has a broad one.

No amount of activation establishes sufficiency. A region active during an experience is responding to something, and a process that follows the experience accounts for the same signal, so the image cannot show which came first. The [three requirements for causation](correlation%20does%20not%20imply%20causation.md#the%20three%20requirements%20for%20causation) put the same problem in checklist form, where temporal precedence fails first, because nothing orders the activation and the perception. Imaging locates where something happens without showing that it began there.

A causal role needs both kinds of evidence: mapping says where a region responds, disruption what happens without it.
