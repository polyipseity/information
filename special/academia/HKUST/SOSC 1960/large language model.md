# large language model

A large language model is a deep network trained on text. It predicts what comes next, and it was one of the first deep models the public met.

---

- what a large language model predicts ::@:: What comes next in a text.
- the scale of a large language model beside earlier neural networks ::@:: A far greater one.
- which deep models the public met first ::@:: Large language models.

## architecture and prediction

### transformer

The transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) is the architecture behind a large language model. It rests on attention, which encodes long-range dependencies: an association between two distant words survives their separation in the input.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the architecture of a large language model ::@:: The transformer, built on attention.
- what attention lets a large language model encode in text ::@:: Long-range dependencies.
- what long-range dependencies connect ::@:: Words separated in the input stream.

### next-word prediction

A language model continues a text one word at a time. Elman (1991) trained a network to produce the next word of English sentences, aiming to internalise English syntax from those examples. A large language model is the same task at far greater scale and capacity. For the history of such networks, see [artificial neural network](artificial%20neural%20network.md).

- how the 1991 network was to internalise English syntax ::@:: By learning word prediction from example sentences.
- what a large language model is beside the 1991 network ::@:: The same task at far greater scale and capacity.
- how old the task of predicting the next word of a sentence is ::@:: As old as 1991.

## public reception

O'Toole and Ludvig credit the reception to three factors: replies to informal text prompts that read as human, open or free availability, and help with many daily tasks. They join object recognition, face recognition and the game of Go among the human-level feats artificial intelligence has reached, and they are now omnipresent.

- what availability large language models had when they entered public consciousness ::@:: Open or free.
- how common large language models are now ::@:: Omnipresent.

### prompt design

Prompt design is the study of the best way to ask an artificial intelligence a question (Kern et al., 2024). A prompt is judged by how well it assesses the novelty, feasibility and value of a creative idea. Asking a large language model to explain first and rate later gives assessments comparable to existing methods without training data.

---

- the prompt design whose assessments are comparable to existing methods ::@:: Explain first, rate later.
- what the explain-first, rate-later prompt design does without ::@:: Training data.
