# confounding

A confounder is tied to both the manipulated variable and the measured one, standing between a claim and its evidence. Confounding is a systematic error that distorts an estimate of a causal effect. The pattern in the data can be genuine and still say nothing about cause.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- overview ::@:: A confounder distorts a causal claim by being tied to both the variable under study and the outcome.
- what kind of error confounding is ::@:: A systematic error.
- why a confounded association is still real ::@:: The pattern in the data is genuine, but it does not show that one variable causes the other.

## what makes a variable a confounder

Three conditions make a variable a confounder.

- it predicts the outcome, the dependent variable, on its own;
- it is tied to the exposure, the independent variable under study;
- it is not on the causal pathway between the two.

The third condition rules out a mediator, the variable that carries the effect from the exposure to the outcome. A mediator sits inside the mechanism, so holding it constant blocks the effect.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- overview ::@:: A confounder independently predicts the outcome, is associated with the exposure, and is not on the pathway between them.
- the first condition a confounder meets ::@:: It independently predicts the outcome, the dependent variable.
- the second condition ::@:: It is associated with the exposure, the independent variable.
- the third condition ::@:: It is not on the causal pathway between the exposure and the outcome.
- what the third condition rules out ::@:: A mediator, the variable that carries the effect from the exposure to the outcome.
- where a confounder sits ::@:: Outside the mechanism, so hold it constant.
- where a mediator sits ::@:: Inside the mechanism, so holding it constant blocks the effect.

## a third variable behind an association

Charitable giving and reported happiness rise together. Wealth makes generous spending possible, and it makes people report more happiness. It links the two, so neither causes the other. A correlational design cannot separate the third variable from the two it moves with; [a correlation does not imply causation](correlation%20does%20not%20imply%20causation.md) generalizes the point.

The same pattern appears with no experiment. Students pick one of two professors. The class that picks the low-expectation professor gets lower grades; the class that picks the demanding one gets higher grades. Intelligence decided who chose what. The grades describe the students who chose each course, and say little about the teaching.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- overview ::@:: A third variable that moves with both of the correlated variables can produce the whole pattern.
- the third variable behind a link between charitable spending and reported happiness ::@:: It is wealth, which makes generous spending possible and also makes people report more happiness.
- what a third variable does to a causal reading ::@:: It links the two variables, so neither one causes the other.
- the third variable in a comparison of the grades in two professors' courses ::@:: It is student intelligence, because it decided which students chose which course.
- what the grades in those two courses end up measuring ::@:: The students who chose each course, and very little about the teaching.
- what a correlational design cannot do with a third variable ::@:: Separate the two variables from the third.

## confounds an experiment introduces

Random assignment balances the differences between conditions that nobody thought about. It does nothing about a difference the design itself creates, such as the expectations of both parties.

- A treated participant may report the change they expect rather than the one they got: a [placebo](placebo.md) effect.
- A participant who guesses the study's purpose may report the expected result instead: participant demand.
- An experimenter who knows the conditions may see the result they expect: the experimenter expectation effect.

A [double-blind procedure](blinded%20experiment.md) takes both parties' expectations away. A study that leaves them in place cannot claim the independent variable produced the difference.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- overview ::@:: Some confounds are created by the design, and random assignment does not touch them.
- what random assignment does handle ::@:: It handles the differences between conditions that nobody thought about.
- what random assignment does not handle ::@:: A difference the design itself creates, rather than one that already existed.
- the confound a participant creates by knowing they are being treated ::@:: A placebo effect, where a participant reports a change they expected.
- the confound a participant creates by guessing the purpose ::@:: Participant demand, where a participant reports the result the study seems to want.
- the confound an experimenter creates by knowing the conditions ::@:: The experimenter expectation effect, where the experimenter sees the result they expected.
- what removes the expectations of both parties ::@:: A double-blind procedure.
- what a study with those confounds left in cannot claim ::@:: That the independent variable produced the difference between the conditions.
