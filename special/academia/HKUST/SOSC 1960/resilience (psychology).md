# resilience

Resilience is the ability to stay healthy under stress. Health psychology names five protective factors: coping, control and self-efficacy, social relationships, dispositions and emotions, and stress management. Each predicts health on its own, and studying what protects health this way is [positive health](health%20psychology.md#prevention%20and%20technology).

## response to stress

### coping

Coping is what a person does about a stressor. It comes in two kinds. Problem-focused coping addresses the event, as studying does for an exam. Emotion-focused coping regulates the feeling the event produced, as a funny film distracts from the exam. Problem-focused coping does more for mental wellness, but only where the stressor can be changed. A death in the family leaves nothing to solve, so managing the feeling is the only route.

---

- what emotion-focused coping does ::@:: Regulates the emotions that come with the stress.
- which coping kind does more for mental wellness ::@:: Problem-focused coping, where the stressor can be changed.
- when emotion-focused coping is the better choice ::@:: When the stressor cannot be controlled, such as a death in the family.

### stress management

Some stressors cannot be removed, so the interventions aim at the response. Relaxation and meditation lower arousal through breathing exercises, muscle relaxation, and mental imagery. Exercise reduces stress and has health benefits of its own. Biofeedback shows a person a normally hidden bodily signal, such as heart rate, then teaches strategies to change it. The training has lowered the risk of heart disease and hypertension.

## protective resources

### control and self-efficacy

A control belief protects health even when the control is not real. Glass and Singer (1972) found that people told they could silence an unpleasant noise did better on a proofreading task than people told nothing. Neither group could affect the noise. Rodin and Langer (1977) and Schulz and Hanusa (1978) found that older residents of an assisted-living home given a plant to water lived longer than those who were not. The belief lowers stress hormones and helps immune function. It also decides whether a person tries to change at all.

Self-efficacy is the belief that one can finish a task and reach a goal. A person who believes they can manage the behaviour has less stress and drops negative health behaviours such as smoking.

### social relationships

Social integration is the number of social roles a person holds, and the absence of isolation. The roles are daughter, student, team member, volunteer. They encourage the habits that protect health. Isolation raises the risk of disease and death by about as much as smoking regularly does.

Social support is what the people in those roles provide when stress arrives. It comes as emotional help such as a hug, tangible help such as a loan or a place to stay, and advice. Support cuts stress directly and improves the health behaviours a person keeps.

### dispositions and emotions

Cardiologists in the 1950s noticed that heart patients tended to be competitive, impatient, hostile, and time-urgent. They called the pattern Type A behaviour. It carried about twice the heart disease risk of the calmer Type B pattern. The harmful parts were hostility and competitiveness. Repeated angry arousal damages the arteries. A hostile person also loses the supportive network that would soften the damage.

Positive emotion protects health instead. People who feel happy or excited more often live longer, develop fewer illnesses, and recover better when they fall ill. The link holds in poor countries as well as rich ones. Optimism works through coping. It predicts a faster recovery from a heart attack.
