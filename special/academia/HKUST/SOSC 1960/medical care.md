# medical care

Psychology decides three things about medical care: who seeks it, what the patient tells the doctor, and whether the recommendation is followed. Each step loses people who needed care.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the three steps psychology decides in medical care ::@:: Who seeks care, what the patient tells the doctor, and whether the recommendation is followed.
- what all three steps of medical care share ::@:: Each one loses people who needed care.

## access and communication

### seeking care

Older people, women, and people from higher socioeconomic backgrounds seek care more often. Money is one barrier, and preconceived notions about doctors or the illness are another.

The internet has added a step before the appointment. About a third of people say what they read online shaped their decision to see a doctor. They judge what they find badly. A student's article earns as much trust as a physician's once the topic feels familiar. A site's design, logos, and contact details pass for credibility.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- which people are more likely to seek medical care ::@:: Older people, women, and people from higher socioeconomic backgrounds.
- what stops a person who needs care from seeking it ::@:: Financial obstacles and preconceived notions about practitioners or the illness.
- how well people judge online health information ::@:: Badly: they rate a student's article as credible as a physician's when the topic feels familiar.
- what people use to judge a health website instead of its accuracy ::@:: Its design, logos, and the organisation's contact information.

### communication

What the patient reports decides the diagnosis. Embarrassment hides a detail the diagnosis rests on, and so does the feeling of being rushed. Treatment then proceeds on an incomplete picture.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what decides the accuracy of a diagnosis ::@:: What the patient reports in the consultation.
- what distorts what a patient reports ::@:: Embarrassment and the feeling of being rushed.

## adherence

Adherence is following a medical recommendation. It fails often: patients stop medication early, leave prescriptions unfilled, or do not change the behaviour they were told to change. In one study, over a third of diabetic patients missed care that would have prevented or slowed the blindness diabetes causes.

Technology can measure what a patient does instead of asking. A pill bottle records when it is opened. Text messages improve adherence in depression, smoking cessation, and weight loss.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what adherence is ::@:: Following a medical recommendation, such as taking a medication properly or changing a behaviour.
- how often patients fail to follow a medical recommendation ::@:: Often: patients stop medication early, leave prescriptions unfilled, or do not change the behaviour they were told to change.
- the case that shows the cost of poor adherence ::@:: Over a third of diabetic patients missed care that would have prevented or slowed diabetes-related blindness.
- how technology measures or improves adherence ::@:: Pill bottles record when they are opened, and text messages improve adherence in depression, smoking cessation, and weight loss.
