# affective forecasting

Affective forecasting is predicting how one will feel after an event or a decision. People get the direction right but the intensity and duration wrong. Because people choose between options by imagining how each will feel, a mistaken forecast can send them to the wrong one.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what affective forecasting is ::@:: Predicting how one will feel after an event or a decision.
- what affective forecasting gets right ::@:: The direction, whether the feeling will be positive or negative.
- what affective forecasting gets wrong ::@:: How intense the feeling will be and how long it lasts.

## impact bias

The impact bias is the tendency to overestimate the intensity of a future feeling. Brickman, Coates, and Janoff-Bullman (1978) compared expected with actual feeling: people expected to feel worse after losing a job, and happier after winning the lottery, than they did.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what the impact bias overestimates ::@:: The intensity of a future feeling.
- which outcomes Brickman, Coates, and Janoff-Bullman (1978) found the impact bias distorts ::@:: Losing a job and winning the lottery, each less intense than expected.

## durability bias

The durability bias is the tendency to overestimate how long a feeling will last. The bias is stronger for negative events, because people underestimate how much they will adapt and cope (Gilbert, Pinel, Wilson, Blumberg, & Wheatley, 1998).

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what the durability bias overestimates ::@:: How long a positive or negative feeling will last.
- for which events the durability bias overestimates duration more (Gilbert et al., 1998) ::@:: Negative ones, because people underestimate how much they adapt and cope.
