# gender role

A gender role is the part a society expects a person to take because of their sex, covering the work, the manner of speaking, the emotional response, and the habits assumed to belong to men or to women. Widely held gender roles are stereotypes: they describe what people expect of a sex rather than what its members are like, so a belief about men or women is a claim about a group rather than evidence about the people in it.

## stereotyped behaviour

Common stereotypes divide everyday life along sex. The household falls to women, who are pictured coming home to cooking, washing, vacuuming, and housework while men rest, watch television, and sleep. Women are thought to speak at greater length and in more meandering fashion, to cry at a romantic film that sends men to sleep, and to take hours over an appearance that men settle in seconds. Travel is imagined the same way: men move around the world with ease while women stay tied to duties at home, and a man packs a single item where a woman fills the car with luggage. A man walks straight to the item he came for while a woman works through every aisle, and a man's discussion turns into a contest while women's talk overlaps. Both sexes are imagined to want the same partner, standing on a pedestal surrounded by cooking, cleaning, laundry, and childcare.
