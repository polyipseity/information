# dark adaptation

Dark adaptation is the slow gain in sensitivity that lets you see in near-darkness. [Rods](photoreceptor.md) carry it. Their pigment bleaches away in bright light and takes minutes to rebuild, though full recovery takes up to half an hour. A move from a dark room into bright light bleaches the rods at once and blinds you for about a second, until the cones take over.

## contrast gain

Contrast gain is the retuning of visual sensitivity to the contrasts a scene offers. A night scene holds little contrast, so sensitivity rises and a face invisible a few minutes earlier separates from its background. In bright light the same gain would saturate, so sensitivity falls.

- what contrast gain does in a dim scene ::@:: Sensitivity rises to the small contrasts present, so faint shapes separate from their background.
- why high gain would fail in bright light ::@:: Contrasts are already large, so the same gain would saturate.
