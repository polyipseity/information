# misinformation effect

The misinformation effect is memory for an incident altered by the information introduced when the incident is questioned. In a classic experiment, participants watched slides of a car accident and then were asked to remember particular details about the incident (Loftus et al., 1978). The question asked, "Did another car pass the red car when the red car stopped in front of a stop sign?" The sign actually shown was a yield sign. Implanting memories through questions of this kind is a type of retroactive interference.
