# theory of mind

Theory of mind is the ability to attribute a mental state to another person: a belief, a desire, an intention, an emotion, or a piece of knowledge. It is how someone makes sense of what another person does. A person who knows Bernard feels unwelcome reads his silence as embarrassment rather than rudeness.

## false belief task

A false belief task gives a person a belief the child taking the task knows to be untrue, then asks where that person will look. Answering correctly requires setting aside what the child knows and representing what the other person believes.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what a false belief task asks ::@:: Where a person will look, when the child knows that person's belief is untrue.
- what a correct answer to a false belief task requires ::@:: Setting aside what the child knows and representing what the other person believes.

## recursive mindreading

Recursive mindreading is a belief about a belief, then a belief about that belief. At the third level, Elaine does not know that Bernard feels she does not know him well enough to date. Adults handle about seven levels of embedding. Large language models answer about as accurately as people up to four levels, then fall off.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what recursive mindreading is ::@:: A belief about a belief, nested to further levels.
- how many levels of embedding adults handle ::@:: About seven.
- how deep large language models mindread ::@:: About four levels, then they fall off.
