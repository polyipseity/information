# research design

A research design says which variables to measure, which to manipulate, who to compare against whom, and how long to follow people. Five designs answer a research question, and each buys a different claim at a different price. The question and the resources settle the choice, not a ranking.

## what each design can establish

| Design | Assignment | What it can support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Experiment](experiment.md) | random | a causal claim about the manipulated variable |
| [Quasi-experiment](quasi-experiment.md) | none, conditions already fixed | a weaker causal claim, with the confounds that come with the groups |
| [Correlational design](correlation.md) | none, both variables measured as they occur | an association, with no direction of cause |
| [Survey](survey%20(human%20research).md) | either, depending on the design around it | whatever that design supports |
| [Longitudinal study](longitudinal%20study.md) | either, depending on the design around it | change within the same people, and the order of cause and effect |

A finding is only as strong as its design. A causal claim needs a design that rules out whatever else could have produced the difference. [Random assignment](random%20assignment.md) does that, and a correlational design cannot.

An experiment pays in the participants' time and in the researcher's control over the conditions. A quasi-experiment takes a weaker claim, for conditions nobody can assign. A correlational design is cheap. A survey reaches more people than the others. On its own it only collects data, so it is not a design. A longitudinal study shows change inside one person, and costs years.

## the constraints that choose a design

Resources come first: the time and money a study can commit. A senior project cannot start a twenty-year study. Exploratory work runs the cheap design.

Ethics binds harder. War, long-term isolation, abusive parenting, prolonged drug use, and brain injury are worth studying, but none can be assigned to a healthy participant on purpose. Where a condition cannot be assigned, the researcher works with what already happened and claims less. The limit is on what may be asked, not a flaw in the method.
