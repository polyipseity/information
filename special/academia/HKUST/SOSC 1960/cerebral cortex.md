# cerebral cortex

The cerebral cortex is the outer layer of the cerebral hemispheres: grey matter, two to four millimetres thick, folded over the whole outer surface. Perception, movement planning, memory, and reasoning are carried out here. The fold packs that surface into the space the skull allows.

## gyri and sulci

The fold is not smooth. The parts of it that bulge outward are gyri, and the parts that dip inward are sulci. A region of the cortex is named for the sulcus it lies next to rather than for its distance from a landmark. Two sulci do most of the dividing. The central sulcus starts near the top edge of the hemisphere and runs down its outer surface, separating the frontal lobe from the parietal. The lateral sulcus runs from the front of that surface back toward the side of the head, separating the temporal lobe below it from the frontal and parietal lobes above it.

![A photograph of the left side of a brain, its ridges and grooves visible, with a blue line marking the central sulcus, a blue line along the lateral sulcus, and numbers pointing at the frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobes and at the frontal, temporal and occipital poles](attachments/Human%20brain%20lateral%20view%20description.JPG)

Of the two, the central sulcus is the one worth remembering. It is the fixed landmark: everything in front of it lies in the frontal lobe and everything behind it in the parietal.

## the four lobes

The frontal lobe sits in front of the central sulcus and carries reasoning, planning, speech, movement, emotion, and problem solving. It holds the motor cortex, the strip of cortex lying immediately in front of the central sulcus, which drives voluntary movement.

The parietal lobe sits behind the central sulcus and above the lateral sulcus. It carries visuospatial attention, body sensations, perception, and orientation, and it holds the somatosensory cortex, the strip of cortex lying immediately behind the central sulcus, which receives touch from the body.

The occipital lobe sits at the rear of the hemisphere and carries visual processing. The temporal lobe sits below the lateral sulcus and carries auditory processing, speech, visual recognition, and memory.
