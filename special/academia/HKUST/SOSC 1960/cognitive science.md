# cognitive science

Cognitive science studies the mind in terms of internal representations and information processing, and asks what human intelligence is. The field formed in the 1950s. Psychology and artificial intelligence share its early figures.

## cognitive revolution

In the early 1900s, psychology was behaviourist. It measured observable behaviour and set internal processes such as thought aside. The cognitive revolution of the 1950s replaced that with internal representations and information processing. The mind became a system of parts and operations, not a black box.

The computer made the shift possible, since a researcher could then specify the mechanism that produces behaviour rather than describe it from outside. Experimental psychology and cognitive neuroscience made mental activity open to objective study.

Four figures are both cognitive scientists and pioneers of artificial intelligence: Herbert Simon, Allen Newell, Noam Chomsky and George Miller. Miller's paper is _The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two_.

## birth

Cognitive science begins with [Alan Turing's question](artificial%20intelligence.md#Turing's%20question) of 1950, whether machines can think. Two meetings in 1956 followed. A summer workshop first named artificial intelligence, and a meeting that September joined psychology to computer science. George Miller dates the birth of cognitive science to that September meeting.

The Sloan Foundation chose the name cognitive science in 1976. A report it published in 1978 set out the field under that name. The Cognitive Science Society formed in 1979.

## disciplines

Six disciplines meet in cognitive science: psychology, linguistics, anthropology, neuroscience, philosophy and artificial intelligence. A hexagon drawn for the field puts one at each corner. The field aims to understand the nature of human intelligence through interdisciplinary study, and no one discipline carries it alone.

Psycholinguistics, computational linguistics, cognitive neuroscience, computational neuroscience and cognitive psychology sit in the middle ground, where several fields take up one question at once. Each is named for the two disciplines it joins. Psycholinguistics joins psychology to linguistics, and computational linguistics joins linguistics to artificial intelligence. Computational neuroscience joins artificial intelligence to neuroscience, and [cognitive neuroscience](cognitive%20neuroscience.md) joins neuroscience to psychology. Cognitive psychology joins psychology to artificial intelligence.

The two halves of a hybrid are not equally visible in its work. Cognitive psychology reconstructs a mental process from the behaviour it produces, and cognitive neuroscience adds where in the brain that process happens.

Two of the six supply each other methods. Artificial intelligence gives psychology a model to test a theory against. Psychology gives artificial intelligence [reinforcement learning](reinforcement%20learning.md) and attention mechanisms.

### mind challenge

The Cognitive Science Society runs a Mind Challenge each year. Entrants answer one question about minds in a creative video, and the Society names a first-place video.

The 2022 question was whether machines can think, [Alan Turing's question](artificial%20intelligence.md) of 1950. His imitation game scores a machine on behaviour. An evaluator reads a text conversation and decides whether the replies came from a person. A machine that passes has imitated a person, not understood one, so the game cannot settle the question. John Searle put the gap in biology. A computer only follows instructions. Thought needs a living brain. Human learning opens the same gap. A person learns a new concept from a single example, largely unsupervised, where a network needs thousands of labelled examples.

The 2023 question asked why the world holds so much [language diversity](language%20diversity.md). A single language faculty, shared by the species, underlies all of it and is expressed as more than 7,000 languages. Isolation splits the communities that carry it: mountains, oceans and distance keep groups apart, and each group's speech drifts into a lineage of its own. Where a language ends and a dialect begins, national politics draws the line rather than structure. Serbian and Bosnian are counted as separate languages, though mutually intelligible. Linguists count by mutual intelligibility instead, whether two speakers can understand one another.

The 2024 question asked why memory fades or fails. The fading comes from compression, not a broken recorder. Memory keeps a compressed version of what happens. It is not a copy. A difference is dropped so that a rule can be drawn. What serves understanding is kept. Forgetting is part of the design. A memory that kept every detail would hold each case and never extract the rule, and would never generalise.
