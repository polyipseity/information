# classical conditioning

A dog hears a bell and then gets a piece of meat. After enough pairings the bell alone brings on saliva. The bell does not make the dog salivate. It announces meat. The association is a signal, not a cause. A stimulus that reliably announces an event calls up the response the event would have caused. The signal can be acted on before the event arrives. Learning that one stimulus predicts another is classical conditioning.

Any stimulus an organism can perceive is capable of eliciting any reaction the organism is capable of making. Almost any sound, sight, or smell can shift muscle tension, mood, and attitude.

A consequence works the other way round: a reward or a punishment follows what the animal did and decides what the animal does next. [Classical and operant conditioning](operant%20conditioning.md#classical%20and%20operant%20conditioning) sets the two side by side.

Pavlov arrived at conditioning from digestion. He was a physiologist. He shared the 1904 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. He diverted a dog's saliva into test tubes, which let him measure how much the dog produced. Trial after trial the dogs began salivating before the food reached them. The sight of the dish was enough, then the footsteps of Pavlov or his assistants. Metronomes, lights, and bells were stand-ins too. What mattered was reliable signalling that food was on the way, not the kind of stimulus.

## the four components

Meat makes a dog salivate, and a bell alone makes it do nothing, until the two are paired often enough.

The __unconditioned stimulus (US)__ elicits a particular response without prior training. The __unconditioned response (UR)__ is the response that occurs to a stimulus without prior training. The __conditioned stimulus (CS)__ does not elicit a particular response at first but comes to do so through conditioning. The __conditioned response (CR)__ is the response the conditioned stimulus eventually elicits after repeated pairings with the unconditioned stimulus. A __neutral stimulus (NS)__ is one that is not yet conditioned, so it belongs to no response.

Organisms are born with the US–UR pair, and conditioning builds the CS–CR pair out of it. The separate names let you say which half was learned.

## pairing a neutral stimulus with an unconditioned one

A bell in a laboratory produces nothing. Meat produces salivation, as it has since before the dog was born. Present the bell, then the meat, over and over. The bell ends up where the meat was: alone it brings on salivation. The bell is a conditioned stimulus doing the meat's work. The neutral stimulus has to come first and the unconditioned stimulus second.

## a stimulus that predicts a temperature change

In a particular house, the shower water comes out very hot or very cold every time the toilet is flushed. The person steps back. Over time the step comes on the flush alone, before the water changes.

The cue is usually a sound rather than a sight, and this arrangement is easy to find outside the laboratory. The flush is the conditioned stimulus. The temperature change it predicts is the unconditioned stimulus. The step back is the conditioned response, to a change that has not happened yet.

## conditioned emotional responses

A word that puts someone at ease does nothing on its own. A gunshot sound produces anxiety on its own. Pair the two and the anxiety attaches to the word. A phrase that would once have calmed someone now raises anxiety. The inversion can happen after as few as five or six pairings. Emotions are conditioned like salivation, and the emotional response is the conditioned one.

- the shape of a conditioned emotional response ::@:: The same as any conditioning: a neutral cue paired with a stimulus that already produces the emotional response.
- the original neutral stimulus in the relaxation example ::@:: A word that puts someone at ease, such as "relax".
- the unconditioned stimulus in the relaxation example ::@:: A gunshot sound.
- the unconditioned response in the relaxation example ::@:: Anxiety.
- the conditioned response in the relaxation example ::@:: Anxiety elicited by the word "relax" alone.
- what the inversion means ::@:: A cue that would once have calmed now raises the response it was paired with.
- how many pairings invert the meaning of the cue ::@:: As few as five or six.

## what conditioning generates

Conditioning does more than attach one response to one cue: the same cue can end up carrying several different responses, a liking or a disliking. Conditioning can underlie clinical conditions. What it cannot do is form an association from a pairing alone.

### preferences and aversions

A person eats a food and then falls ill. The taste or smell of that food now carries an avoidance, out of all proportion to the number of pairings. This is taste aversion conditioning. A conditioned response pulls a person towards something or pushes them away. The sign of the unconditioned stimulus paired with the cue decides which way. The same process can set a preference as readily as an aversion.

### the conditions it underlies

Fear conditioning underlies panic disorder and phobias. A cue paired with something frightening comes to carry the fear on its own. The process is detailed in [phobia](phobia.md).

In drug use, conditioned compensatory responses oppose the effect of the drug. The body's response to withdrawal pain is itself conditioned, so cues associated with morphine make a person more sensitive to pain, not less.

A third application is therapeutic rather than clinical, and it motivates the study of extinction. Applied as a treatment, extinction may be helpful. It is the principle exposure therapy is built on.

### why a pairing alone is not enough

A pairing is necessary, but it is not enough on its own. Presenting a neutral stimulus before an unconditioned stimulus does not always produce an association. Something has to register that the outcome was not already predicted. A cue that adds nothing to what the organism already knows gets nothing attached to it. This is the [blocking effect](blocking%20effect.md), and it puts attention and surprise at the centre of the account.
