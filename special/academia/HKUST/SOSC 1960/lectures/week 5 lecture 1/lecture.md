# lecture

- HKUST SOSC 1960 week 5 lecture 1
- parent: [week 5 lecture 1](index.md)

Learning psychology studies how behaviour is learned. Classical conditioning works on reflexive responses. Classical conditioning explains a conditioned compensatory response, preparedness, and fear conditioning in a phobia. Instrumental conditioning works on voluntary behaviour. Habit links a stimulus to a response. A learner copies a social model. The Bobo doll study turned psychology from behaviorism toward cognitive psychology. The behaviourist programme left the internal mechanisms of behaviour unstudied. Positive punishment adds an unpleasant consequence. Blocking leaves a redundant cue unlearned. Taste aversion follows one bad meal.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the topics learning psychology covers ::@:: A conditioned compensatory response, preparedness, fear conditioning, instrumental versus classical conditioning, habit as a stimulus-response link, a social model, the shift from behaviorism to cognitive psychology, positive punishment, blocking, and taste aversion.
- the kind of behaviour classical conditioning and instrumental conditioning each work on ::@:: Reflexive responses for classical conditioning; voluntary behaviour for instrumental conditioning.
- the classical conditioning phenomenon that explains an overdose when a tolerated morphine dose is given in a new laboratory ::@:: A conditioned compensatory response.
- the natural ability to learn an association for biological reasons ::@:: Preparedness.
- why human beings may be prepared to learn an aversion to bitter tastes ::@:: Many lethal poisons have a bitter flavour.
- the conditioning that can contribute to anxiety disorders such as phobias and panic disorder ::@:: Fear conditioning.
- the association that defines a habit ::@:: A stimulus and then a response.
- the term for a person whom a learner watches and copies ::@:: A social model.
- the shift in psychology that the Bobo doll study produced ::@:: Psychology moved from behaviorism toward cognitive psychology.
- what the behaviourist programme left unstudied ::@:: The internal mechanisms of behaviour.
- the kind of punishment that adds an unpleasant consequence, such as extra housework after a child is mean to a sibling ::@:: Positive punishment.
- the phenomenon in which a redundant cue added beside a cue that already predicts the outcome stays unlearned ::@:: Blocking.
- the conditioning in which one bad meal makes a flavour hated ::@:: Taste aversion conditioning.
