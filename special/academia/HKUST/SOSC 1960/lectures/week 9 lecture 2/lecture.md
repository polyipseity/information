# lecture

- HKUST SOSC 1960 week 9 lecture 2
- parent: [week 9 lecture 2](index.md)

Sensation and perception: the dorsal pathway, dark adaptation, rods and cones, the vestibulo-ocular reflex, lateral inhibition, the primary visual cortex, synesthesia, and opponent-process color vision.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the topics in sensation and perception ::@:: The dorsal pathway, dark adaptation, rods and cones, the vestibulo-ocular reflex, lateral inhibition, the primary visual cortex, synesthesia, and opponent-process color vision.
- the information the dorsal pathway of the human visual system lets the brain compute ::@:: Object locations and motion, so a person can avoid obstacles.
- why a friend's face becomes visible after several minutes outside at night ::@:: Contrast gain: the eyes become more sensitive to the available contrast.
- the photoreceptor type whose large number partly explains a person's excellent night vision ::@:: Rods.
- the region of the human eye with the highest density of cone photoreceptors ::@:: The central region of the fovea.
- the reflex that moves the eyes opposite to a head movement so that gaze stays on the object in view ::@:: The vestibulo-ocular reflex.
- the part of the eye in which the photoreceptors are located ::@:: The retina.
- how neurons in the brain exaggerate contrast to keep it recognizable ::@:: They suppress the response of nearby neurons, a process called lateral inhibition.
- the first stop in the cortex where neural signals start to turn into interpretive information, also known as V1 ::@:: The primary visual cortex.
- the blending of senses in which a number is perceived as a color, such as "1" seen as red, and the share of people who experience it ::@:: Synesthesia, which about 1 in 200 people experience.
- the theory of color vision that explains a yellow after-image after staring at a blue dot and looking at a white wall ::@:: The opponent-process theory of color vision.
