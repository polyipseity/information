## reading a study for a question

> __Prompt.__ How would keeping a pet help elderly feel less lonely? Spot the keywords.

The question came with a study of an animatronic pet program. Most answers restated its finding that animatronic pets reduce loneliness and improve well-being, mental wellness, resilience, and sense of purpose. Several added detail from the study: the program's feasibility was tested with survey data gathered before and after the intervention, and the pets are easier to care for than live ones, which leaves out most of the drawbacks of pet ownership. One answer asked whether an elderly person can look after both themselves and a pet. A few brought in other studies, reporting that dog owners over 65 without a partner feel significantly less social isolation than non-owners and that the number of pets owned correlates negatively with loneliness. One noted that randomized trials are still needed before the evidence is definitive.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- keywords the pet question reduces to ::@:: pets, elderly, and lonely.
- finding the class's answers restated ::@:: Animatronic pets reduce loneliness and improve well-being, mental wellness, resilience, and sense of purpose.
- how the study's feasibility was tested ::@:: With survey data gathered before and after the intervention.
- why the animatronic pets are easier to care for ::@:: They leave out most of the drawbacks of pet ownership.
- dog owners over 65 without a partner ::@:: Significantly less social isolation than among non-owners.
- correlation between pets owned and loneliness ::@:: Negative: the more pets, the less loneliness.
- doubt one answer raised about the program ::@:: Whether an elderly person can look after both themselves and a pet.
- what one answer said is still needed ::@:: Randomized trials, before the evidence becomes definitive.

## paraphrasing a finding

> __Prompt.__ Rewrite the finding in your own words: "Momentary distractions by one's cellphone during time spent with a significant other likely lowers the significant other's satisfaction with their relationship, and could lead to enhanced feelings of depression and lower well-being of that individual." (Baylor University, 2015)

Most rewrites kept the writer's own framing and named the authors. A few kept the source's wording almost whole with the citation attached. Others changed the claim, either by giving a year that does not match the source's or by widening a finding about one's partner into a claim about people in general.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- a paraphrase of the cellphone finding ::@:: Momentary phone distraction while spending time with a partner lowers that partner's satisfaction with the relationship and can lead to depression and lower well-being (Baylor University, 2015).
- what keeps a rewrite a paraphrase ::@:: The wording is the writer's own, the claim still concerns the same thing, and the authors and the year are given.
- ways a rewrite stops being a paraphrase ::@:: Keeping the source's wording with the citation attached, or changing the claim's scope or its date.
