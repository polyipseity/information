> __Prompt.__ 1.4. What do you perceive when moving quickly between light and dark environments? How is it related to photoreceptor responses? (Slide 10)

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ 1.5. What is the lightness constancy effect? (Slide 11)

Light passes through the cornea and lens to the retina, where [photoreceptors](../../photoreceptor.md) turn it into a signal. A rod fires on a single photon, fills the periphery, and reports motion without color. A cone needs more light and clusters at the fovea, where it gives fine detail and color. The optic nerve carries the signal to the lateral geniculate nucleus of the thalamus, then to the primary visual cortex.

A bipolar cell's output is its input minus the inhibition from its neighbors, so what they report in common cancels and contrast survives. At an edge, [lateral inhibition](../../lateral%20inhibition.md) leaves the bright side less suppressed than the dark side.

Walking from sunlight into a dark room blinds you for minutes while bleached rods rebuild their pigment, the slow recovery called [dark adaptation](../../dark%20adaptation.md). Walking the other way bleaches them at once and blinds you for about a second, until cones take over.

Lightness constancy is seeing a surface as the same shade whatever light falls on it. The visual system estimates the illumination and discounts it, so white paper looks white in sunlight and indoors. Lightness illusions appear where that estimate goes wrong.

Beyond the primary visual cortex the signal splits into two routes, the [two-streams hypothesis](../../two-streams%20hypothesis.md). The ventral stream works out what an object is. The dorsal stream works out where it is and how to act on it.

The eyes sit apart, so each gets a slightly different view. Binocular vision turns that difference into volume and distance, and steadies apparent size.

An illusory contour is an edge that is seen but not drawn. Three notched circles leave gaps that the visual system closes into a triangle brighter than its ground, a [reconstruction](../../visual%20cortex.md#reconstruction) of the reading the gaps best support.

An anamorphic image looks correct from one viewing position only. From that spot the viewing angle compresses a stretched drawing back into proportion. The Ames room hides its shape the same way: a peephole makes a trapezoidal room look rectangular, so two people of similar height at different real distances look unequal in height. The brain assumes both stand equally far away and reads the smaller retinal image as the smaller person.

## color

> __Prompt.__ 3.5. What is the dress illusion and why does it occur? (Slide 21)

Trichromacy holds that three kinds of cone explain color vision. The cones peak at short, medium, and long wavelengths, and a hue comes from the ratio of their responses.

[Opponent process theory](../../opponent%20process.md) says color is coded as comparisons between opposites: red against green, blue against yellow, and black against white. One member of each pair suppresses the other, so a reddish green cannot be seen.

The cone types are the same everywhere, so the retina sends the same signal. Language and cultural learning set the boundaries between [color categories](../../color%20categories.md#category%20boundaries). A language that splits a region, as Russian splits blue, lets its speakers discriminate those shades faster.

Staring at one color and then at a neutral surface leaves an afterimage in the opposite color. The driven member of the channel tires in the adapted retina, and the other dominates once the stimulus is gone.

The dress is a photograph taken under ambiguous light, and viewers disagree about whether the fabric is blue and black or white and gold. Each viewer has discounted a different illuminant, so the same pixels resolve into different fabrics. That is [color constancy](../../lightness%20constancy.md#illusions) reading a scene that does not say which light it was.

> __Prompt.__ 4.5. Why is it important to learn about human vision in psychology? (Slide 2)

The [vestibulo-ocular reflex](../../vestibulo-ocular%20reflex.md) turns the eyes against the head's motion by the same amount, driven by the vestibular report of that motion. Gaze stays on its target, and a still object does not smear across the retina.

[Synesthesia](../../synesthesia.md) is one signal giving rise to another kind of sensation. Grapheme-color synesthesia, a letter or digit carrying a fixed color, is the most common form. Cross-activation explains it: extra connections let activity in one sensory area drive another.

Inattentional blindness is failing to notice something plainly in view because attention was elsewhere. A person counting basketball passes misses someone in a gorilla suit, because an unanticipated object never reaches awareness.

A [retinal prosthesis](../../visual%20neurotechnology.md#retinal%20prosthesis) is a set of photosensitive circuits on the back of the eyeball, wired to the visual areas of the brain. It bypasses a damaged retina and gives a partial visual experience, so the patient has to learn to read an artificial signal.

Vision is the most studied sensory modality in psychology, and most of what people know of the world arrives through it.
