## research design

> __Prompt.__ Use an example to illustrate the difference between experimental and quasi experimental design

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Use an example to demonstrate the difference between correlational and experimental design

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Can we use survey data in a correlational design? Provide an example to illustrate this.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Can we use survey data in an experimental design? Provide an example to illustrate this.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ can we use brain imaging data in a correlational design? Provide an example to illustrate this.

One feature separates the three designs: who decides who is in which group. An [experiment](../../experiment.md) assigns it at random, a [quasi-experiment](../../quasi-experiment.md) compares groups that already existed, and a [correlational design](../../correlation.md) only measures.

One branch of a company moves to a four-day workweek and another stays on five, and productivity is compared between the branches. The branches may already have differed in management, workload, employee experience, and workplace culture, so only part of the difference goes to the schedule. Random assignment would leave the difference going to the schedule itself.

Caffeine and exam scores draws the same contrast. The correlational version asks students how much caffeine they drank and compares the answers with their exam scores, which leaves confounders such as hours of study standing. The experimental version splits them at random into a caffeine group and a placebo group and puts both through the same exam, so a higher score in the caffeine group belongs to the caffeine.

A [survey](../../survey%20(human%20research).md) is only a way of collecting data, not a design, and it works inside a correlational design. Sleep duration against daily stress among 300 university students is the clearest case, where lower reported sleep goes with higher reported stress at $r<0$.

In an experiment the same survey becomes the instrument rather than the design. In the humorous-video study, students are randomly assigned a humorous video or a neutral video of the same length, and both groups fill in the same mood questionnaire before and after. The independent variable is the type of video and the dependent variable is the change in mood score. A larger improvement in the humorous group supports a short-term effect on self-reported mood. A survey can also expose a bias to design around, such as participants who would attribute their feelings to one cause and not to any other.

Brain imaging fits a correlational design for the same reason, with an [MRI](../../magnetic%20resonance%20imaging.md) scan measuring the size of the hippocampus and a memory test measuring performance, so correlating the two shows whether bigger goes with better. Causal brain studies are very hard to run, so the correlation is what the design yields.

## correlation and causation

> __Prompt.__ People who drank diet coke gained more weight than those who drank regular coke. Can we conclude that diet coke leads to more weight gain? Why or why not?

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Children who received piano training performed better academically at school than those who did not. Should we all send our kids to take piano lessons in order to enhance their academic performance based on this finding? Why or why not?

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ When participants are presented with face images, an area in the inferior temporal lobe is activated. Can we conclude that activation in this region leads to perception of a face? Why or why not?

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ It was found that students who have short sleep at night are more depressed. Can we conclude that lack of sleep leads to depression? Why or why not?

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Provide an example to demonstrate that when we observe a correlation between variables, it does not always result from a causation.

Four findings, and the same answer to all four: nothing was manipulated and nobody was assigned, so each is an association. [Correlation does not imply causation](../../correlation%20does%20not%20imply%20causation.md) sets out the three structures that fit one.

For diet coke, confounding comes first, since the two groups may differ in overall diet, physical activity, health conditions, initial body weight, diabetes, age, or socioeconomic status. Reverse causation runs the other way, since people already gaining weight, or already managing it, switch to diet drinks. The San Antonio Heart Study backs the second: diet coke consumption tracked with rising waist circumference, and the leading explanation was that heavier people select diet beverages in disproportionate numbers. A randomized controlled trial would settle it.

For piano training, families who can afford lessons tend to have higher incomes and more educated parents, and both independently raise achievement. The causal direction can also run backwards, since children who are already disciplined are the ones enrolled and kept at it, and selection bias applies to the sample itself. Piano need not be the route to better grades, but it may build the focus and persistence that studying also asks for.

For face activation, a region may respond to faces without being necessary for recognizing them, and none of the three requirements for causation is met. Lesions and [transcranial magnetic stimulation](../../transcranial%20magnetic%20stimulation.md) supply necessity where imaging cannot, as a lesion leading to prosopagnosia does for face recognition.

For short sleep, depression itself often brings insomnia or fragmented sleep, and academic stress, anxiety, poor health, and irregular schedules can each reduce sleep and raise depression together. The safe conclusion is that students who sleep less tend to report more depression. Going further would take a controlled experiment or a [longitudinal design](../../longitudinal%20study.md).

Hormone replacement therapy went with a significantly lower risk of coronary heart disease, and doctors concluded it prevented the disease and prescribed it widely. Later randomized trials found it increased the risk for some women, and the women taking it had better diets, more exercise, and better access to healthcare. Military engineers looked at returning bombers, found bullet holes concentrated in the wings, tail, and fuselage with almost none in the engines and cockpit, and wanted armor exactly there. Only the surviving planes had been examined, so the armor belonged where the holes were not. The jump is made for three reasons: framing, through binary thinking and a refusal to admit a multitude of causes; data selection, through a survivor filter or an affluence filter; and interpretation, through the narrative fallacy and confirmation bias.

- diet coke, the compensatory-eating explanation ::@:: Someone who saves calories on the drink eats more elsewhere, so the extra food is the cause.
- what imaging can never show about a region ::@:: Sufficiency, because the activity seen may be a consequence of the perception or of a metabolic process that follows it.
- what shows necessity instead ::@:: Lesions and transcranial magnetic stimulation: damage or interference with a region shows it is necessary for a function, as in prosopagnosia.
- the conclusion that is safe on short sleep ::@:: Students who sleep less tend to report more depression.
- two series that rose together with nothing connecting them ::@:: US spending on science, space, and technology, and suicides by hanging, strangulation, and suffocation, both climbing across 1999 to 2009.
- bomber planes and armor placement ::@:: Bullet holes clustered in the wings, tail, and fuselage, but only surviving planes were examined, so the armor belonged where the holes were not.

## designing an exercise-and-mood study

> __Prompt.__ How are you going to measure mood or emotion in this study? List some possible ways to measure mood/emotion.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Design a study to examine this relationship using an experimental design

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Design a study to examine this relationship using a correlational design

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Design a study to examine this relationship using a quasi-experimental design

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ Design a study to examine this relationship that involves a longitudinal design

The study starts from an observation about one person: every time after exercise a feeling of happiness follows, and a study is wanted on the relationship between exercise and mood.

The instruments are set out in [mood measurement](../../mood%20measurement.md): a single rating, a validated questionnaire such as PANAS or POMS, experience sampling, and the physiological routes. PANAS was the one the designs below use, and two limits carry into them: physiological arousal tracks more than happiness, and the living brain is correlational at best, not proof that a region is the mood.

The experimental design puts around 80 to 90 students at random into 30 minutes of exercise or a 30-minute lecture or nature video control. One design used three arms, no exercise against moderate walking against vigorous running, so the dose could be compared as well. Mood is measured with a validated affect scale, PANAS in most versions, immediately before and immediately after, and the change scores are compared between groups with a one-way ANOVA.

Around 100 students in the correlational design report their total exercise minutes and their average mood over the same past seven days, with mood on a scale from 1 to 10. Pearson's $r$ gives the direction and strength of the linear relationship, a positive value being the prediction. A correlation does not establish causation: happier students may exercise more, or a third variable such as academic stress may drive both. Self-report and recall bias sit on top of either.

The commonest quasi-experimental split is active members of sports clubs against students who do not exercise regularly, and both groups take the same mood questionnaire, rated 1 to 10 or 0 to 10. Without random assignment, sleep quality, family income, relationships, age, gender, health status, and socioeconomic status all remain confounds, so a difference between the groups can be observed but not attributed to exercise.

The longitudinal design tracks fifty people, 25 exercisers and 25 controls, over a year. In one version the exercise is assigned, an hour a day, and mood is surveyed every month. Its advantage is that it can ask whether the exercise level at time 1 predicts the mood at time 2.

## the methods used to study the brain

> __Prompt.__ If you want to know whether there is a brain region uniquely associated with students' word reading performance, which method/technique would you use? Why?

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ If you want to know how quickly the human brain can process the emotional expression of a face compared to its identity, which method/technique would you use? Why?

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ If you want to know whether disrupting a specific brain region (e.g., parietal region) can lead to impaired selective attention ability, which method/technique would you use? Why?

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ John had a accident and had a specific brain region damaged. Afterwards, the doctor found that he performed worse than other people in face recognition. What can we conclude from this finding?

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Prompt.__ When we damage a component of a computation model of face recognition, the model has poorer performance in recognizing Asian faces but not Caucasian faces. What can we conclude from this finding?

Which region is active is a question about space, answered by a picture of where the activity sits. How fast the processing happens is a question about time, answered by timing accurate to the millisecond. Whether a region is needed at all is a question about interruption, answered only by disrupting the region.

[fMRI](../../functional%20magnetic%20resonance%20imaging.md) answers the first. It records while the student is actually reading a passage, so the region and the behaviour are measured in the same run. The crux is the word uniquely: contrasting real words against a control dataset isolates the region tied to reading rather than to vision or attention in general. Its poor temporal resolution costs nothing here, because only the area is needed, not the moment.

[EEG](../../electroencephalography.md) answers the second, and specifically the event-related potentials derived from it, which date the response to the millisecond. ERP components such as the N170 or the P100 mark where expression decoding falls against identity decoding. Backward masking and face-evoked potentials paradigms are the ways to run it. [MEG](../../magnetoencephalography.md) was added as an alternative with good temporal and good spatial resolution.

[TMS](../../transcranial%20magnetic%20stimulation.md) answers the third, and it leaves a virtual lesion with no permanent damage. The procedure stimulates the parietal cortex while participants perform a selective attention task, and reaction time and accuracy are measured before, during, and after. The vertex is the control, a site not related to attention, and it rules out the nonspecific effects of stimulation such as the noise and the scalp sensation. A drop in parietal performance but not vertex performance makes the region necessary for selective attention, which is cause and effect rather than correlation. The alternatives fall short, since fMRI shows only correlation and lesion studies in patients involve permanent, uncontrolled damage. Lesion-symptom mapping and tDCS were named alongside it.

A single lesion case licenses only the hypothesis that the damaged region may be involved in face recognition, as [cognitive neuropsychology](../../cognitive%20neuropsychology.md) holds. The comparison is between different people, not a before-and-after on the same person, and there is no baseline for him, so he may have been worse at face recognition before the accident. The damage may also act through other regions, and the fusiform face area was named as a candidate region.

The intact model scores 90% on both Asian and Caucasian faces, and damaging the component drops Asian faces to 60% while Caucasian faces stay at 90%. Within the model the component is selectively necessary for recognizing Asian faces and not Caucasian ones, and the model appears to have race-differentiated internal representations. [Computational model](../../computational%20model.md) sets out what a damaged model does and does not predict. The first check is baseline accuracy, since Asian faces may already have been harder for the model and the deficit may be smaller than it looks. The asymmetry may instead reflect different features for the two categories, or training-data bias, including a model trained on Asian faces. The conclusion applies to the model, and human behavioural and brain research is needed before it generalizes. It does line up with the [other-race effect](../../cross-race%20effect.md) in people, and the model may be a mechanism for it.
