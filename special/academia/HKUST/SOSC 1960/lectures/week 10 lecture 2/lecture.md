# lecture

- HKUST SOSC 1960 week 10 lecture 2
- parent: [week 10 lecture 2](index.md)

Ten multiple-choice questions on developmental psychology: how development proceeds, Piaget's stages and what each adds, nature and nurture, phonemic awareness, adolescent brain development, and sex differences in intelligence.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the six areas of developmental psychology, and what each covers ::@:: How development proceeds, Jean Piaget's stages and what each adds, nature and nurture, phonemic awareness, adolescent brain development, and sex differences in intelligence.
- the four stages of Jean Piaget's theory of cognitive development in order, with the identifying behaviour of the preoperational and formal operational stages ::@:: Sensorimotor, preoperational reasoning, where a taller, thinner glass reads as holding more juice after a pour, concrete operational reasoning, then formal operational reasoning, where a child reasons deductively.
- the measure that shows an infant does not know a teddy bear still exists once it leaves the field of vision ::@:: An object permanence task.
- the two findings that recent research reports on sex differences in intelligence quotient (IQ) scores ::@:: Higher variability in males than in females in general, and no higher average score in males than in females.
- how nature and nurture combine in development ::@:: Nature and nurture interact to determine virtually every aspect of development.
- the biggest limitation of Jean Piaget's concrete operational reasoning stage ::@:: Children in the stage cannot think in systematic, scientific ways.
- the name for the awareness of the component sounds that make up a word, such as the r, ay, and n sounds in rain ::@:: Phonemic awareness.
- the brain part, important in planning and flexible problem solving, that continues to develop throughout adolescence ::@:: The prefrontal cortex.
- the pattern of development that involves a series of relatively sudden qualitative changes ::@:: Discontinuous development.

## quiz

> __Question 1.__ Davina has made great strides in her understanding of her science classes. Being able to perform deductive reasoning, Davina is in which of Piaget's developmental stages?
>
> 1. formal operational stage
> 2. concrete operational stage
> 3. deductive reasoning stage
> 4. conventional reasoning state
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{1. formal operational stage}@}

> 1. a concentration test
> 2. a reversibility task
> 3. a conservation exercise
> 4. an object permanence task
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{4. an object permanence task}@}

> __Question 3.__ Which of the following description(s) is/are correct?
>
> I. Recent research showed that there is a higher variability of IQ scores in males than in females in general.
>
> II. Recent research has shown that on average males have higher IQ scores than females.
>
> 1. I only
> 2. II only
> 3. I & II
> 4. None of the above
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{1. I only}@}

> __Question 4.__ If you were taking part in a debate with another psychologist over the relative influences of nature and nurture on development, which position do you think you'd be most likely to take if you wanted to win the debate?
>
> 1. Nature and nurture interact to determine virtually every aspect of development.
> 2. Genes can be muted or enhanced by the influences of one's environment. Therefore nurture is most important.
> 3. Research has consistently shown that we are ultimately determined by our genes, irrespective of influence of one's surroundings.
> 4. Because it is impossible to determine the different levels of influence of nature and nurture, the debate was abandoned in psychology decades ago.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{1. Nature and nurture interact to determine virtually every aspect of development.}@}

> __Question 5.__ The biggest limitation of the concrete operational reasoning stage of development, according to Piaget, is:
>
> 1. children in this stage cannot think in systematic, scientific ways.
> 2. children in this stage fail to understand conservation tasks.
> 3. children in this stage ask too many abstract and hypothetical questions.
> 4. object permanence ability has usually failed to occur by these years.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{1. children in this stage cannot think in systematic, scientific ways.}@}
