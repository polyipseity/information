# lecture

- HKUST SOSC 1960 week 13 lecture 2
- parent: [week 13 lecture 2](index.md)

Ten multiple-choice questions on cognitive science and artificial intelligence: the cognitive revolution, how the field was named, its six disciplines, connectionist models, first impressions, explainable artificial intelligence, and the recent advance.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- which discipline the Sloan Foundation did not name in 1976 ::@:: Artificial intelligence. That year names cognitive science, and artificial intelligence was named at the 1956 Dartmouth workshop.
- which of the six disciplines of cognitive science the 1978 report excluded ::@:: Education, which is not one of the six.
- the two drivers of the recent advance, one supplying the arithmetic and one the data ::@:: The computational power of machines, through graphics processing units, and the internet.

## quiz

> __Question 1.__ ________ studies machine using experimental methods from cognitive psychology originally designed to reveal the underlying mechanisms in the human brain.
>
> 1. Machine learning
> 2. Human computer interaction
> 3. Artificial Cognition
> 4. Large language model
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{3. Artificial Cognition}@}
> - explanation: Artificial Cognition studies {@{machine behaviour, not people}@}. It borrows {@{the experimental methods of cognitive psychology}@}, designed to reveal {@{the mechanisms in the human brain}@}.

> __Question 2.__ ________ is an intellectual movement in the history of psychology that leads to an interdisciplinary study of the mind known as cognitive science.
>
> 1. Behaviorism
> 2. Cognitive revolution
> 3. Cognitive development
> 4. Social Cognition
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{2. Cognitive revolution}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Question 3.__ Which of the following description(s) is/are correct?
>
> I. The inception of AI as a field and the first use of the term was by Sloan Foundation in 1976.
>
> II. Early attempts of studying cognition as information processing adopted a rule-based approach.
>
> 1. I only
> 2. II only
> 3. I & II
> 4. None of the above
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{2. II only}@}
> - explanation: {@{The Sloan Foundation named cognitive science in 1976, not artificial intelligence. Early work was rule-based: an algorithm manipulating symbols.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Question 4.__ According to Sloan foundation's report on the state of the art in cognitive science in 1978, which of the following was NOT one of the disciplines included in the field of cognitive science?
>
> 1. Education
> 2. Philosophy
> 3. Anthropology
> 4. Neuroscience
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{1. Education}@}
> - explanation: {@{Cognitive science takes in psychology, linguistics, anthropology, neuroscience, philosophy and artificial intelligence, but not education.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Question 5.__ Which of the following description(s) is/are correct?
>
> I. Connectionist models are neural network models.
>
> II. Small-scaled connectionist models could not be used to account for effects in human cognition.
>
> 1. I only
> 2. II only
> 3. I & II
> 4. None of the above
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{1. I only}@}
> - explanation: Only I holds. Small networks did account for cognition: {@{NetTalk}@} mapped {@{English text to phonetics}@}, and {@{other networks}@} made {@{a child's confusions between the regular and irregular past tenses}@}.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Question 6.__ Which of the following statements about neural networks does not hold true?
>
> 1. Activation functions are typically threshold functions.
> 2. Error is calculated at each layer of the neural network.
> 3. Both forward and back propagation take place during the training process of a neural network.
> 4. Most of the data processing is carried out in the hidden layers.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{2. Error is calculated at each layer of the neural network.}@}
> - explanation: Error is computed {@{once, at the output layer}@}, by comparing {@{the prediction with the label}@}. {@{Backpropagation}@} then sends the error {@{back through the chain rule}@}.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Question 7.__ Social psychology research has suggested that the two primary trait dimensions used to form instant first impressions are ________
>
> 1. dominance and empathy
> 2. kindness and empathy
> 3. trustworthiness and kindness
> 4. dominance and trustworthiness
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{4. dominance and trustworthiness}@}
> - explanation: {@{A face is read on two axes, trustworthiness across and dominance up, not one dimension.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Question 8.__ Which of the following description(s) is/are correct?
>
> I. Explainable AI is a new concept that only recently emerged due to the advance of deep learning technology.
>
> II. AI in psychology focuses on being a tool for data analysis but not on testing psychological theories.
>
> 1. I only
> 2. II only
> 3. I & II
> 4. None of the above
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{4. None of the above}@}
> - explanation: Neither holds. Explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) is {@{not new}@}: it has had {@{three generations}@}, and {@{expert systems and knowledge-based tutors came before deep learning}@}. Artificial intelligence also {@{tests psychological theory}@}, with {@{generated stimuli standing in for participants}@}.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Question 9.__ Which of the following is NOT an assumption of behaviorism?
>
> 1. All learning is the result of conditioning.
> 2. Conditioning depends on association and reinforcement.
> 3. The human mind can be understood in terms of internal representations.
> 4. Studies should focus on observable behavior.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{3. The human mind can be understood in terms of internal representations.}@}
> - explanation: {@{Behaviourism set internal processes aside and measured only observable behaviour; the cognitive revolution studied internal representations and information processing instead.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> __Question 10.__ The recent advances in AI and Psychology are largely due to ________
>
> 1. Computational power of machines
> 2. Explosion of the internet to provide data
> 3. Both a and b
> 4. none of the above
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{3. Both a and b}@}
> - explanation: Computing power rose exponentially: {@{graphics processing units (GPUs)}@}, built for video games, supplied {@{the parallel arithmetic training demands}@}. The internet supplied {@{the data the networks learn from}@}.
