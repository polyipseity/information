# lecture

- HKUST SOSC 1960 week 7 lecture 1
- parent: [week 7 lecture 1](index.md)

The two hemispheres divide the brain's work, and each controls one side of the body.

The corpus callosum is the thick bundle of nerve fibers that connects them, and cutting it produces the split brain, whose halves answer separately.

Grey matter is mostly neuron cell bodies, and white matter is mostly the fibers that connect them. Imaging methods read the working brain.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- which side of the left hemisphere the left hand is on ::@:: The ipsilateral side.
- the lobe associated with complex problem solving, planning, and organization ::@:: The frontal lobe.
- the pseudoscience that measured the human skull because it assumed certain brain areas have localized, specific functions ::@:: Phrenology.
- the brain signal measurement technique with the highest temporal resolution ::@:: Electroencephalography (EEG).
- the thick bundle of nerve fibers that connects the left and right cerebral hemispheres ::@:: The corpus callosum.
- what a split-brain patient typically reports when Giuseppe Arcimboldo's Vertumnus painting is shown to the left visual field, and when "hat" is shown to the left field and "band" to the right ::@:: A face from the painting, and the word "band" said aloud.
- what diffuse optical imaging (DOI) and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) each read ::@:: DOI reads how infrared light shined on a person's head changes as it penetrates the brain, and fMRI reads changes in the naturally occurring oxygen level in the blood of the brain.
- what white matter in the brain mostly consists of ::@:: Fibers that connect neurons.
