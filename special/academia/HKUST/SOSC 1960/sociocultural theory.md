# sociocultural theory

Sociocultural theory explains cognitive development by the influence of other people and of the surrounding culture. A child's thinking changes with the people around the child, and with that culture's attitudes, values, and beliefs. Lev Vygotsky's theory is one member of the family, alongside [stage theories](developmental%20stage%20theories.md) and [information processing theory](information%20processing%20theory.md).

## zone of proximal development

The zone of proximal development is the space between what a learner can do unsupported and what the learner cannot do even with support. Inside that range the learner performs only with help from a teacher or a peer, the more knowledgeable other. Vygotsky (1896 to 1934) introduced the concept in the last three years of his life. He did not develop it fully.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what the zone of proximal development is ::@:: The space between what a learner can do unsupported and what the learner cannot do even with support.

### more knowledgeable other

The more knowledgeable other is the teacher or the peer who holds more knowledge or expertise about the task than the learner does. The person can be an adult or another child. Vygotsky argued that the child enters a dialogue with that person and gradually develops the ability to solve problems independently.

---

- who the more knowledgeable other is ::@:: The teacher or the peer with more knowledge or expertise about the task than the learner.
- what a child develops through dialogue with the more knowledgeable other ::@:: The ability to solve problems independently.

### limit of guidance

Guidance works only inside the zone. A task beyond the zone fails however much help arrives.

## assessment and support

### assessment of ability

A knowledge-based test records what the child has already learned. Two children who score the same need not sit at the same developmental level. Vygotsky argued against such a test as a measure of intelligence. A test should also ask what the child can do with an adult's help.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- why Lev Vygotsky argued against knowledge-based tests as a measure of intelligence ::@:: Two children with the same score need not be at the same developmental level.
- what a test should also ask, on Lev Vygotsky's argument ::@:: What the child can do with an adult's help.

### scaffolding

Scaffolding is the support an adult gives: focused questions and positive interaction. The term is not Vygotsky's, though the idea is. Jerome Bruner, David Wood, and Gail Ross developed it while applying his concept. The aid tapers off as it becomes unnecessary, like a scaffold off a finished building.

---

- what scaffolding is ::@:: Focused questions and positive interaction from the adult.
- who developed the term scaffolding ::@:: Jerome Bruner, David Wood, and Gail Ross, while applying Lev Vygotsky's concept.
- how scaffolding ends ::@:: The aid tapers off as it becomes unnecessary, like a scaffold off a finished building.

## contrast with stage theories

Stage theories focus on whether children progress through qualitatively different stages of development. Sociocultural theory asks instead how other people and the surrounding culture influence a child's development.
