# health psychology

Health psychology studies the part the mind plays in health, and in preventing and treating illness. Chronic disease has replaced infection as the leading cause of death. The behaviour behind it can be changed, but a germ cannot. Coronary heart disease is the number one cause of death worldwide, according to the World Health Organization in 2013. Smoking, diet, exercise, and sticking to a treatment decide much of how the disease runs.

## biopsychosocial model

The biopsychosocial model holds that biology, psychology, and social circumstances cause disease as much as a pathogen does. The model agrees with the World Health Organization's 1946 definition of health. It replaced the biomedical model, which looked only at the physical factors behind an illness. A heart attack shows all three: a blocked artery is the biology, hostility and chronic stress are the psychology, and isolation and poverty are the social side.

### mind and body

Two fields ask how a mental state reaches the body. Psychosomatic medicine asks whether chronic stress and negative emotion raise the risk of heart disease, and whether treating that distress improves a physical outcome. Psychoneuroimmunology asks how stress and mood change immune function, which shows up as infection risk or slower healing. Wounds healed about 24 percent more slowly in caregivers under long strain.

## health psychologist

A clinical health psychologist treats the disorders tied to poor health and works on the habits behind them. That work happens in a team of physicians, social workers, allied health professionals, and religious leaders. The team works at a hospital, a primary care office, an emergency care centre, a rehabilitation centre, or a chronic illness clinic. It weighs the physical, personal, and environmental factors behind an illness and builds a treatment plan that covers all of them. A research health psychologist finds risk factors and tests interventions at a university, a public health department, a hospital, or a private organisation.

### behavioral medicine

Behavioural medicine is a field close to health psychology. It brings emotion, behaviour, cognition, and social factors into the treatment of disease. The field is applied. Its clinical areas include occupational therapy, hypnosis, rehabilitation, medicine, and preventive medicine.

- what behavioural medicine is ::@:: A field close to health psychology that treats disease using psychological factors.

## prevention and technology

Health care has mostly been illness care: it manages symptoms once they appear. Prevention finds the risk factors behind a preventable illness and develops interventions that lower its prevalence, the share of a population that has it.

Resilience works from the other side, on the positive factors that prevent disease and protect a person from it ([five protective factors](resilience%20(psychology).md)). That direction is positive health, Martin Seligman's name for a shift from risk towards what protects. Positive health studies people whose health is above average.

Mobile health (m-health) apps send medication reminders by email or text message and track exercise and food intake. They raise health awareness, support treatment and [adherence](medical%20care.md#adherence), and collect outcome data remotely. A device that monitors physiology in real time shows which situations raise blood pressure or heart rate. Health psychologists can target an intervention at a particular population or individual. Related research on aging belongs to the [Center for Aging Science](https://aging.hkust.edu.hk/community) at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST).
