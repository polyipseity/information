# experiment

An experiment is a procedure a researcher runs to test a hypothesis. In psychology, the experimenter manipulates one variable and measures a second. Measuring the manipulated variable is what makes a causal claim possible. Two conditions that differ in one respect, and differ in the outcome, show that the manipulation caused that difference.

Most people expect highlighting to help them remember a textbook. Instinct is no substitute for a controlled comparison, and in this case it is wrong.

## independent and dependent variables

The independent variable is the one the experimenter controls and changes between groups. The dependent variable is the one nobody manipulates. It follows from the independent variable, and the manipulation's effect should show up there.

Ask whether highlighting improves retention of information. Highlighting is the independent variable; retention is the dependent variable. The researcher draws names at random, assigns half to highlight, and leaves the rest without it. The two groups differ in nothing but what they did with the page. Instinct had predicted a gain. Undergraduate students got none; graduate students improved.

## what an experiment has to rule out

A difference between two groups tells you about the independent variable only when nothing else differs.

- A [placebo](placebo.md) effect: a participant who knows they are getting the treatment may report what they expect instead of what happened.
- Participant demand: a participant who guesses the point of the study may act on the guess.
- The experimenter expectation effect: an experimenter who knows each participant's condition may see an improvement nobody experienced.

All three are [confounds](confounding.md) fed by information. Hide the condition from both the participant and the experimenter, and all three go away, leaving a [double-blind](blinded%20experiment.md) study. Another control is [random assignment](random%20assignment.md). It assigns participants at random before the manipulation starts, which keeps the two conditions comparable.
