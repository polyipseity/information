# human eye

The eye is an optical instrument. It bends light into a small image on the retina, the sheet of light-sensitive cells lining the back of the eyeball, and the retina turns that image into nerve signals. Everything the [visual cortex](visual%20cortex.md) reconstructs starts from that image, so its sharpness and its position decide what the brain has to work with.

Light crosses the eye in a fixed order: the cornea, the clear front surface; the aqueous humour, the fluid behind it; the pupil, the hole in the coloured iris; the lens; and the vitreous humour, the jelly filling the eyeball. It ends on the retina, where the optic nerve leaves at the back to carry the signal onward.

## refracting media

The cornea does most of the bending. It is transparent and curved, and its shape is fixed, so what it contributes never changes. Behind it the aqueous humour keeps the front of the eye inflated.

The lens supplies the rest of the bending. It is a clear flexible disc, and it is the one part of the optical system that can change its own power. Behind it, the vitreous humour keeps the eyeball round and holds the retina against the back wall.

### accommodation

Light from a near object arrives diverging and needs more bending than light from a distant object, which arrives almost parallel. The cornea cannot supply the difference, so the lens changes shape. A ring of ciliary muscle around the lens contracts, the suspensory ligaments holding the lens go slack, and the lens rounds, adding power for near vision. Into the distance, the muscle relaxes, the ligaments pull taut, and the lens flattens again.

## pupil and iris

The pupil is an opening rather than a structure: the hole through the iris, the ring of muscle that gives the eye its colour. Its width sets how much light enters. In bright light the pupil narrows and in dim light it widens, and the iris carries one set of muscle that shrinks the pupil and another that enlarges it, with light level and arousal deciding which wins.

## image on the retina

Cornea and lens together act as one convex lens, so the image they form is real and inverted: upside down, and reversed left to right. A point of light above the axis of the eye lands below that axis on the retina. Nobody sees the world that way, because the reconstruction in the [visual cortex](visual%20cortex.md) is what reaches awareness.

![A horizontal section of the human eye with light entering from the left, labelled cornea, aqueous humour, pupil, iris, lens, vitreous humour, retina, fovea, blind spot, and optic nerve, with two rays bending at the lens to meet on the retina below the axis they came from](attachments/eye_section.svg)

The sharpest part of the image lands on the fovea, the small pit at the centre of the retina directly behind the pupil. Looking at something swings the eye until the object's image sits there. The rest of the field falls on [photoreceptors](photoreceptor.md) that resolve less detail, so only one small part of the scene is sharp at a time.

Where the optic nerve leaves the retina there are no photoreceptors, and that patch is the blind spot. It sits away from the point of gaze, so it never covers what is being looked at, and the visual system fills the gap it leaves in the field.
