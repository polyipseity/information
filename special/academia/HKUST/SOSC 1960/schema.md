# schema

A schema is a mental model of a person, object, situation, or event: an organized body of general beliefs built from direct encounters and from secondhand sources. A person meeting a dog for the first time assumes it will bark and fetch without working out what a dog is. The stored model lets the perceiver go beyond the information given, as Bruner (1957) put it.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what a schema is ::@:: A mental model: an organized body of general beliefs about a person, object, situation, or event.
- what a schema saves ::@:: Cognitive work: a new encounter meets a stored model instead of being worked out from scratch.
- what a schema lets a perceiver do (Bruner, 1957) ::@:: Go beyond the information given, filling in what the encounter probably involves.

## types

Schemas cover individual people, the self, and recurring events. An event schema, or script, for dining out gives the order of the meal: wait to be seated, order from a menu, pay at the end. Most restaurants follow that format, so the diner runs the script.

## stereotype

A stereotype is a general belief about a group of people, and once activated it can guide judgments outside conscious awareness. It resembles a schema: a representation of how one expects a person to think and behave. A schema for women as caring becomes the stereotype that all women are caring. In 1989, Patricia Devine primed participants with words associated with Black people, such as blues and basketball. Those participants read a target's ambiguous behaviors as more hostile than unprimed ones did.
