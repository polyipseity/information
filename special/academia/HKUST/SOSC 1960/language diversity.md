# language diversity

Human language is one faculty the whole species shares. It comes in more than 7,000 languages. The 2023 Cognitive Science Society Mind Challenge asked why the world holds so many. The speech each community carries is what varies.

## one faculty

Language is the species-specific capacity to map inner thoughts onto external symbols, signs and sounds. Some cognitive linguists hold that capacity to be a separate module, a language organ. The organ is hardwired into the brain and evolved for parsing and producing linguistic structure.

## diversification

### divergence

Communities that live together work out a communication system of their own. Mountains, oceans and dense forests separate them, and social divisions do the same, so each community's speech drifts into a separate lineage. The ecological-risk hypothesis puts the fragmentation in temperate, resource-rich regions that need little migration. Small isolated communities persist there and split further.

### change over time

Speech changes between generations: syllables get elided where they are hard to say, and slang from the young later enters the standard. Mishearing alters words too: listeners took "an ewte" to be "a newte", and the mistaken division became "newt". Contact brings borrowing and mixed vocabulary, and the mix can stabilise into a new native language.

### language or dialect

Whether a speech form is a language or a dialect follows national identity and political history more often than structure. Linguists count by mutual intelligibility instead, the point at which two speakers decode each other despite differences in sound and vocabulary. Serbian and Bosnian are mutually intelligible and still count as separate languages.

## documenting endangered languages

A language behaves like an evolving organism, not a blueprint engineered from above. Each language carries its speakers' worldview, ecological knowledge and history. Documenting a rare grammar or phonology gives an empirical window on the mind, so the work ranks with conservation.
