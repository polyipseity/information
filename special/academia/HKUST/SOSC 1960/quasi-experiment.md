# quasi-experiment

A quasi-experiment is a study that compares groups nobody was assigned to. It is an [experiment](experiment.md) with the random assignment left out. The researcher still has an independent variable, a dependent variable, and two groups to compare. Such a study supports a weaker causal claim than an experiment.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- overview ::@:: A study that compares groups not assigned by chance supports a weaker causal claim than an experiment.
- what a quasi-experiment has that an experiment has ::@:: An independent variable, a dependent variable, and two groups to compare.
- the one feature a quasi-experiment drops ::@:: Random assignment to the conditions of the independent variable.
- what takes the place of the assigned conditions ::@:: The groups' existing membership, which becomes the variable.
- how a quasi-experiment differs from a [correlational design](correlation.md) ::@:: A quasi-experiment compares two groups on an independent variable. A correlational design measures two variables as they occur and never groups anyone.

## conditions that cannot be assigned

Nobody can be assigned to marry, to become a professional musician, to suffer a brain injury, or to grow up in a war. When the independent variable is one of those, the researcher takes the groups as they are. Married couples are compared with unmarried couples, and professional musicians with non-musicians.

Such a variable is a fixed variable: an independent variable the researcher can observe but never change. The study treats it like a manipulated variable.

A researcher settles for a quasi-experiment when the alternative is not asking the question. [The constraints that choose a design](research%20design.md#the%20constraints%20that%20choose%20a%20design) sets out the choice.

## when the groups differ on more than the variable

A quasi-experiment can show that two groups differ, not that one group produced the difference. Nothing balanced the groups, so they differ on more than the variable under study.

Married people may differ from unmarried people on age, income, health, or how long they have been together. Musicians may differ from non-musicians on training, hours of practice, and motivation. A married group that reports more happiness has not shown that marriage produces it.

Students pick which of two professors teaches their course. Nobody chose to be smart, so the comparison looks fair. It is not. The students who picked the professor with low expectations may prefer an easy course. Those who picked the demanding one may already be stronger. Student intelligence explains the course choice and predicts the final grades, whatever the teaching. That is [confounding](confounding.md). [Random assignment](random%20assignment.md) would remove it, and this design has none.
