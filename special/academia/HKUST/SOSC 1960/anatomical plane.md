# anatomical plane

A brain is three-dimensional and every image of one is two-dimensional, so a location has to be reported as a position inside a slice. Three cutting planes are standard. A direction is then named against the body's own axes, so two people describing the same region agree on it.

---

- why a brain location is reported against a cut ::@:: The brain is three-dimensional and every image of it is two-dimensional, so a location is reported as a position inside a slice.
- how many cutting planes are standard for viewing the brain and body ::@:: Three.

## the three planes

The sagittal plane divides the brain into left and right parts. A sagittal cut along the body's own midline, the line down its centre, is called mid-sagittal, and the slice looks like a baked potato cut lengthwise. The coronal plane, also called the frontal plane, divides it into front and back parts. Its slice looks like a cut through a loaf of bread, seen from the front with the eyes at the front of the loaf. The transverse plane, also called the horizontal or axial plane, divides it into top and bottom parts, the way a bun is sliced.

![Three planes drawn through a brain at left, each with the section it produces beside it: a face-on cross-section labelled frontal plane, a side view through the midline labelled sagittal plane, and a top-down cross-section labelled transverse plane](attachments/Blausen%200104%20Brain%20x-secs%20SectionalPlanes.png)

A scan is reported in whichever of the three it was cut in. Which name is written for a plane follows the field reporting it.

## directional terminology

Medial means toward the middle of the body and lateral means toward the side, away from the midline. Both are relative: a structure near the midline is medial to one further from it. Anterior means the head end and posterior the tail end, which on a human body standing upright means front and back. Superior is closer to the head and inferior closer to the feet.

Dorsal means the back and ventral the belly of a vertebrate body. In the human cerebrum those two names follow the top of the head instead: dorsal is the top and ventral the underside. From the midbrain down the mapping reverses, so through the brain stem and spinal cord dorsal is the back and ventral the front.

Two terms are not about position at all but about pairing. Ipsilateral names two things on the same side of the body, contralateral on opposite sides. Almost every pathway into the brain crosses the midline on the way, so what a person sees in the left half of the visual field is handled by the right hemisphere.
