# stimulus control

A pigeon trained to tell two signals apart behaves differently at each. Food pays for the right response. Behaviour that changes with the signal is under stimulus control.

A signal that says whether food is coming is a __discriminative stimulus__, $S^D$. It announces a reward instead of causing the response. The pigeon watches for the reward, not its own behaviour.

The [operant conditioning chamber](operant%20conditioning.md) puts the stimuli, the response, and the two outcomes in one box. The outcomes are food and shock, and the experimenter decides where each one goes before watching which consequence follows which behaviour. Its lights and loudspeaker are two of the signals.

## stimulus generalization

A pigeon taught to pick a Monet over a Picasso goes on to choose the Impressionists over the Cubists. Monet was an Impressionist and Picasso a Cubist, and the choice carried over to the other painters in each movement. Nobody learns a discrimination exactly. The response generalizes to anything resembling the trained stimulus, and that is stimulus generalization.

A habit is the opposite case, answering to no stimulus at all.

The same generalization turns one conditioned fear into a family of [phobias](phobia.md).

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- overview ::@:: Nobody learns a discrimination exactly, and the response generalizes to stimuli that resemble the trained one.
- pigeons taught to select a Monet over a Picasso ::@:: They went on to choose the Impressionists over the Cubists.
- what paid for the correct choice in the Monet-versus-Picasso task ::@:: Food, as a [positive reinforcer](operant%20conditioning.md#reinforcement%20and%20punishment).
- what the choice in that task carried over to ::@:: The other painters in each movement, since Monet was an Impressionist and Picasso a Cubist.
- contrast between generalization and a habit ::@:: Generalization is a response that answers to too many stimuli. A habit is a response that has lost its stimulus altogether.
- how generalization connects a conditioned fear to a phobia ::@:: The same transfer turns one trained cue into a family of feared cues.
