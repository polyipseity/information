# planning fallacy

The planning fallacy is the underestimate of how long one's own task will take: the paper or the bridge. It leaves students working through the night on an assignment that looked manageable.

A person who knew how much work a project would take might never begin it. The optimism that ruins a schedule also starts an ambitious project.

## causes

A person pictures the best case and ignores how long the same kind of task took last time. The record of the last attempt is available to correct that.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what makes a task estimate optimistic ::@:: Picturing the best case and ignoring how long similar tasks took.
- which cause of the planning fallacy has a remedy ::@:: Ignoring how long the last attempt took, which is on record.

## overconfidence in self prediction

A person holds more information about themselves than any peer does. Their own prediction of their academic performance beats a peer's prediction of it. A self-expressed interest predicts the occupation a person enters better than a career inventory does (Shrauger & Osberg, 1981). A person's own assessment of their personality predicts some behavioral tendencies better than a peer's assessment does. For other behaviors, the peer is the more accurate judge (Kolar, Funder, & Colvin, 1996; Vazire, 2010).
