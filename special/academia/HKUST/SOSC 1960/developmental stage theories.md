# developmental stage theories

A developmental stage theory divides development into distinct stages marked by qualitative differences in behaviour.

The theory answers whether development is continuous or discontinuous. Continuous development is the accumulation of small quantitative gains within the same abilities. Discontinuous development is a series of relatively sudden qualitative changes. [Piaget's theory of cognitive development](Piaget's%20theory%20of%20cognitive%20development.md) is the standard discontinuous account.

Stage theories are one of the main families of theory of cognitive development. The other two trace development differently: [sociocultural theory](sociocultural%20theory.md) to other people and the surrounding culture, [information processing theory](information%20processing%20theory.md) to the mental processes behind thinking.
