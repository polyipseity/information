# random assignment

A coin flip or a roll of the die decides who is in which group. With enough people, people with happy childhoods and people who dropped their toast land in both groups. Random assignment makes the two groups match on average before the experimenter changes anything. It leaves one systematic difference: the independent variable. Any difference in the outcome has to come from it.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- how a participant ends up in a group ::@:: A coin flip or a roll of the die decides which group each participant joins, and nothing about the participants enters the draw.
- what random assignment guarantees ::@:: The two groups match on average on everything, before the experimenter changes anything.
- the one systematic difference left between them ::@:: The independent variable.
- why that one difference matters ::@:: Any difference in the outcome has to come from the independent variable.
- what chance leaves unbalanced once there are enough people ::@:: Nothing systematic: every other influence comes out about the same on both sides.

## the two basketball teams

A captain who picks first takes the tallest and the most athletic. His team beats the rest. The gap came from his choice, not from chance.

Drawing names puts the tall and the short on both teams. The two averages come out about the same, and neither team gets better. The even split is what random assignment gives. Letting people pick their own condition loses it before the study starts.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the contrast a random split makes with a captain picking players ::@:: A random split gives both teams about the same average height. A captain picking players cannot do that.
- what a captain who picks first produces ::@:: A taller, more athletic team, so the gap in the result comes from his choice and not from chance.
- what drawing names produces instead ::@:: Tall and short players on both teams, so the two averages come out about the same.
- what drawing names does not improve ::@:: Neither team; it only makes the comparison fair.
- what letting people choose their own condition costs ::@:: The even split between the conditions, before the study starts.

## when chance assignment is impossible

Some conditions cannot be handed out. Nobody can be assigned to marry, become a professional musician, suffer a brain injury, or grow up in a war. A study that pretends otherwise is not an experiment. Researchers use the groups that already exist, treating their membership as the variable. A [quasi-experiment](quasi-experiment.md) supports a causal claim only as strong as its comparison allows.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what a study is when its conditions cannot be assigned ::@:: Conditions nobody can be assigned to leave a study without random assignment. Pretending otherwise does not make it an experiment.
- conditions that cannot be assigned ::@:: Marrying, becoming a professional musician, suffering a brain injury, growing up in a war.
- what a study uses when the conditions already exist ::@:: The existing group membership, treated as the variable.
- what that design is called ::@:: A quasi-experiment.
- how strong a causal claim a quasi-experiment supports ::@:: Only as strong as its comparison allows.
