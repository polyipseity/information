# research ethics

A study can ask a "teacher" to deliver a supposedly painful electric shock to a "learner". Would a review board approve? Scientific psychologists answer with a code of ethics.

Unlike chemistry, psychology studies human participants, who need protection from psychological harm. Knowing how people handle ridicule is no reason to ridicule them.

## ethical guidelines

Informed consent means two things: people know they are in a study and what it will do to them, and they are free to join or refuse.

Privacy and confidentiality are separate duties. Researchers do not watch people in private places such as their bedrooms without their knowledge and consent. Confidentiality keeps what they learn about one participant private unless that participant consents. They also need the participant's or guardian's consent before asking a school authority for confidential information.

Researchers weigh a study's benefits against the risks to participants. They expose anyone to risk only when the risks are fully understood and the likely benefits clearly outweigh them.

A study with human participants goes to a review board. The board weighs the risks against the expected knowledge, checks the consent documents, and may require a less harmful design. It reads the proposal before anyone collects data, not after the results justify it. Neither the [1920 conditioning study](phobia.md#how%20a%20phobia%20forms) nor the [Bobo doll experiment](Bobo%20doll%20experiment.md#the%20design) had that review. A study that fails review never reaches the field, and good data cannot rescue it.

Some researchers deceive participants to hide the study's true nature, usually so participants behave normally. Researchers must debrief everyone afterwards, explaining the true nature.

After a study, debriefing and monitoring are owed whether or not anyone was deceived. A study that deceived nobody needs the same debriefing session: what happened, what was recorded, and what will be done with it. Monitoring is the easiest part to leave out, since the study is already over. Someone has to watch participants who were distressed during the study, or whose risk was never assessed. A study that cannot name that person leaves a gap in its own design.

### participants who cannot consent for themselves

A child or anyone who cannot give informed consent needs a parent or guardian's permission. Researchers then ask the participant for assent, in terms they can understand. Assent sets out the procedures, the risks, and the rights, including the right to stop at any time. Assent is agreement, not the absence of an objection: a participant who does not assent stays out, however willing the parent is.
