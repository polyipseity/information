# human brain

Three names cover the brain at the top level: forebrain, midbrain, and hindbrain. A second set of names, telencephalon through myelencephalon, covers those same structures under the names they carry as they form from the embryonic neural tube. Two schemes over one body is where the complexity of brain structure starts. A third grouping, the brain stem, takes structures out of both of the others.

## the three major parts

The forebrain carries cognitive and motor processing. It holds the cerebral hemispheres and, tucked underneath them, the thalamus and hypothalamus. The midbrain sits between the forebrain and the hindbrain and serves vision, hearing, and the control of eye and body movement. The hindbrain carries the automatic work of keeping the body running.

![The mammalian brain named three ways: each structure box carries its embryological name, the forebrain bracket spans cerebral hemispheres with thalamus and hypothalamus, the midbrain bracket spans only the midbrain, and the hindbrain bracket spans pons, cerebellum and medulla, with a dashed outline over the thalamus, hypothalamus, midbrain, pons and medulla marked brain stem](attachments/brain_division_naming.svg)

Each structure box carries its embryological name. Telencephalon gives the cerebral hemispheres, diencephalon gives the thalamus and hypothalamus, mesencephalon gives the midbrain, metencephalon gives the pons and the cerebellum together, and myelencephalon gives the medulla. The functional brackets group the same structures by job rather than by origin. The dashed brain-stem outline is a subset of neither scheme: it takes the thalamus and hypothalamus from the forebrain group along with the midbrain, and the pons and medulla from the hindbrain group, while leaving out the cerebellum.

## brain stem

The brain stem runs from the midbrain at the top, down through the pons, to the medulla at the bottom where it becomes the spinal cord. The medulla sets heart rate, breathing, and digestion, all of it without any instruction from us. The pons is a major relay between the cortex and the cerebellum, and it is also involved in sleep and dreaming. The midbrain takes visual and auditory information in.

The group's upper boundary is disputed. One definition lists the diencephalon along with the midbrain, pons, and medulla, so the thalamus and hypothalamus count toward the trunk as well. The embryological scheme leaves them with the forebrain instead.

Damage here shows up as a function that stops rather than one that goes missing. A stroke that reaches the medulla stops breathing. A tumour pressing on the pons closes off consciousness while the patient is otherwise intact.

## cerebellum

The cerebellum sits underneath the back of the cerebral hemispheres and is called the little brain because it looks like a smaller copy of the cerebrum. It coordinates fine movement, posture, and balance, estimates time, and keeps speech smooth. It holds about seventy percent of the brain's neurons, far more than its size would suggest.

People with cerebellar damage have trouble with language and with problems that call for planning. Parkinson's disease and multiple sclerosis are also linked to the cerebellum, and both bring cognitive symptoms along with the motor ones.

## gray and white matter

Grey matter contains the cell bodies and dendrites, the parts of a neuron that receive and integrate. White matter consists mostly of axons, and it is white because those axons are wrapped in myelin, the fatty sheath that speeds a signal along. The two interleave through the whole brain: the cortex is grey, the white matter tracts running under it are white, and wherever a tract ends it turns grey again.

## subcortical structures

Subcortical structures lie beneath the cerebral cortex and above the brain stem. They handle work the cortex does not do itself. The thalamus is the switching station between sensory input and cortex: every sense but smell passes through it on the way. The hippocampus takes new memories in and transfers them to long-term storage. Damage to it leaves old memory standing and new memory unrecoverable. The amygdala processes and recognises emotion, fear first among them.

The basal ganglia carry voluntary motor control, habit formation, emotion, and reward processing. The cerebellum is counted here too, as well as in the hindbrain, because it lies underneath the cortex whichever scheme you use.

## cerebral hemispheres

The two cerebral hemispheres are separated by a deep groove, the longitudinal fissure, and joined at the bottom of that groove by the corpus callosum, a band of white matter tracts carrying signals between them. The connections between them are white matter, so the corpus callosum reads as white wherever it is cut. Each hemisphere is layered from the outside in: grey matter on the surface, white matter under it, and the subcortical structures under that.

The hemispheres are not mirror images. The left is bigger toward the front and the right toward the back. Their work is unevenly divided as well: language generally sits in the left, and the right reads a face and its surrounding context better. [Split-brain](split-brain.md) covers what neither can do once the corpus callosum is cut.
