# operant conditioning

An act is followed by a consequence. The consequence decides whether the act happens again, but says nothing about what the act is for. Learning of that kind is operant conditioning, also called instrumental conditioning.

A behaviour followed by a consequence that matters, such as a reward or a punishment, changes how often it happens. Nothing has to be paired with the behaviour, warned about in advance, or signalled.

## reinforcement and punishment

A consequence can be described two ways. What it does to the behaviour that came before it decides whether it is a __reinforcer__ or a __punisher__. Whether a stimulus is added or taken away makes it __positive__ or __negative__.

- toys put away, then a cookie: a stimulus is added and the behaviour increases, positive reinforcement;
- no snack unless the toys are put away: the unpleasant condition ends once the child complies, so the behaviour increases, negative reinforcement;
- mean to a sibling, then be made to do extra housework: a stimulus is added and the behaviour decreases, positive punishment;
- the same child loses a favourite toy for the same act: a stimulus is taken away and the behaviour decreases, negative punishment.

A positive reinforcer and a negative reinforcer differ only in whether a stimulus is added or taken away. Both of them increase the behaviour.

None of the four words says whether a consequence was good or bad. The same object can be a positive reinforcer and a positive punisher at different times.

## choice between behaviours

Given a choice between two behaviours, the one with the better consequences is the one that happens. The comparison is with the other behaviour, not with how good a consequence is on its own.

So a single reward is worth only what you would otherwise have got. A coin is worth a lot to someone who has no other way of getting one, and very little to someone with a machine on every street. [Reward devaluation](reward%20devaluation.md) measures this.

If comparing the consequences already explains the choice, there is less left for a free act of will to explain. The feeling of having chosen freely is what that explanation looks like from the inside. It is not a separate reason the behaviour happened.

## schedules of reinforcement

A __schedule of reinforcement__ is how often reinforcement is offered: after every act, after every tenth act, once a minute. Each schedule has its own effect.

What a behaviour becomes depends on the pattern of consequences, not the size of the reward.

Gambling is the clearest case. A gambling device pays out on some plays and not others, so a player cannot predict which. That is a variable ratio schedule, and it is enough to make a pigeon a pathological gambler. Remove or change the schedule and the behaviour goes. Nothing here needs an urge a person has to suppress.

## classical and operant conditioning

Classical conditioning depends on involuntary behaviour. Operant conditioning is how consequences influence voluntary behaviour. The division is a convenience, not a boundary. Both kinds of conditioning use the same three terms: a stimulus, a response, an outcome.

The stimulus sets the occasion for the response. The response acts on the outcome. The stimulus also reaches the response directly, by __stimulus control__. In the figure the direct route is solid and occasion setting dashed. Both terms are set out in [stimulus control](stimulus%20control.md) and [classical conditioning](classical%20conditioning.md).

![a stimulus, a response and an outcome joined by four arrows: the stimulus sets the occasion for the response and reaches it directly by stimulus control, which a habit does without, the response acts on the outcome as instrumental conditioning, and the stimulus acts on the outcome as classical conditioning](attachments/conditioning_relations.svg)

A habit is the one behaviour that runs without a cue. It carries on when [a reinforcer's worth changes](reward%20devaluation.md).
