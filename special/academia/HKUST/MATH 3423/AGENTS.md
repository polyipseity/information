# MATH 3423 agent instructions

- The instructor tests definitions, so every concept a note defines carries a matched pair of concrete cases: one that satisfies the definition and one that does not. A definition with no example on either side is unfinished.
- The `##` section owning the definition hosts a `### examples and counterexamples` section holding the pair. When a definition lives in the unheaded intro of its note, give it a `##` section first, since a `###` cannot sit above the first `##`.
- The heading trips the `section_example_heading` rule, so each one carries a suppression comment on the line above it, using the same reason as the other sections in this course.
- The prose gives each case and says why it falls on its side; the cards ask the case and answer yes or no with the reason.
- The non-example is the half worth writing. Make it a near miss failing on one specific ground: a quantity that looks free of the unknown parameter but still depends on it, a pair that is independent but not identically distributed, a gamma member with the rate wrong. Say which ground fails.
- Apply the pair to the concept the note defines, not to every formula in it. The notes carrying one are `confidence interval.md` (pivotal quantity), `statistic.md` (statistic), `random sample.md` (random sample), `parametric model.md` (parametric model), `estimator.md` (estimand, estimator, estimate), `chi-squared distribution.md` ($\chi^2(r)$), `Student's t-distribution.md` (the construction of $t(r)$), `gamma distribution.md` (the family and its $\chi^2$ member), `multivariate normal distribution.md` (independence of components), `sample mean.md` (the sample mean), `sample variance.md` (the two sample variances), and `statistical inference.md` (data and modes of inference).
- Keep each case exact. Name the parameter a quantity still depends on, the covariance that is non-zero, or the condition that is violated, rather than asserting that a case fails.
