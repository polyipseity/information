An exercise in sorting value cards into the values a person keeps, then joining them with arrows.

## value cards

Each card carries one value in English with its Chinese name beside it, as authenticity (真我) or community (社群), so the deck works in either language. It carries no definition, rank or category: one word means different things to different people, so the exercise sorts the words without settling them.

- what each card in the value card deck carries ::@:: One value in English with its Chinese name beside it, as authenticity (真我) or community (社群).

- why a value card carries no definition, rank or category ::@:: The same word means different things to different people, so the exercise sorts the words without settling them.

## value map

An arrow from one value to another says the first is held for the sake of the second. Arrows running into a card make it an end, and arrows running out make it a means. One card can hold opposite roles in two maps: happiness (幸福) at the top is what everything else is for, while at the bottom it is one more thing success (成功) is expected to bring.

A completed map claims dependence, not a list of preferences: innovation (創新) at the top means the other values are held for its sake. That belief decides what a trade-off costs a person, so a moral compass rests on it.
