# lecture

- HKUST MGMT 2010 week 7 lecture 1
- parent: [week 7 lecture 1](index.md)

Every case sets a claim made in a relationship against a duty owed in a role.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the tension each of the four cases sets up between a relationship and a role ::@:: A claim made in a relationship against a duty owed in a role.
- the five lines each scenario is answered on ::@:: Benevolence and care, righteousness and justice, propriety and roles, filial duty and trust, then the harmonious resolution.
- why one virtue cannot answer a Confucian case alone ::@:: Each virtue names a different relation in the same situation, so the answer holds them together.

## scenarios

> __Prompt.__ Guanxi against fair procurement, Hong Kong. An assistant procurement manager at a retail group is evaluating vendor bids for a major regional logistics contract. Her maternal uncle, who helped finance her university education, runs a mid-sized trucking company and urges her to award him the contract, insisting that family members must always practice reciprocal loyalty, guanxi, to ensure collective family prosperity.

- __Benevolence (ren, 仁) and care.__ Care reaches the uncle and the drivers and staff whose work depends on a sound bid.
- __Righteousness (yi, 義) and justice.__ Righteousness judges the bid by what it is, not by whose it is, so awarding on kinship fails.
- __Propriety (li, 禮) and roles.__ Her role is procurement officer, and the uncle's appeal to guanxi (關係) asks her to act outside it.
- __Filial duty (xiao, 孝) and trust (xin, 信).__ Gratitude for the education he paid for is a filial claim, and the post is a promise to her employer.
- __Harmonious resolution.__ Decline the award, explain it to the uncle, and help him strengthen the next bid, which honours care and gratitude without falsifying the tender.

> __Prompt.__ Filial piety against whistleblowing in a family enterprise, Taipei. An internal audit trainee at a family-owned electronics firm discovers that his father, the vice president of finance, has created offshore accounts to under-report corporate earnings and evade business taxes. When gently confronted, the father reminds him that filial piety, xiao, requires children to protect and defer to their parents rather than expose them to public scandal and legal punishment.

- __Benevolence and care.__ Care for the father includes wanting him out of a position that ends in prosecution, so silence is not care.
- __Righteousness and justice.__ The accounts are a wrong whoever holds them, and the father's seniority changes the consequences, not the judgement.
- __Propriety and roles.__ The trainee's role is audit, and concealment contradicts it from the first day.
- __Filial duty and trust.__ Filial piety is not obedience: the classical requirement is remonstrance with a parent doing wrong, which makes the harder act the filial one.
- __Harmonious resolution.__ Refuse to conceal, and press the father to correct the filings himself; escalate only if he will not, which keeps the son a child for as long as that role can hold.

> __Prompt.__ Saving face against calling out a defective design, Shenzhen. A junior quality assurance engineer at a consumer technology firm discovers a critical battery overheating flaw two days before the mass release of a flagship smartphone. The battery module was designed by the department's chief engineer, a highly respected industry veteran who personally mentored the junior. Raising the defect publicly during the launch readiness meeting will cost the veteran his face, mianzi, and his annual executive bonus.

- __Benevolence and care.__ Care runs to the people who would carry an overheating phone, which outweighs the discomfort of the meeting.
- __Righteousness and justice.__ Selling a known defect wrongs the buyer, and righteousness does not shrink because the engineer is respected.
- __Propriety and roles.__ Propriety governs how the defect is raised rather than whether, so the chief engineer hears it first, in private, and his face (mianzi, 面子) survives.
- __Filial duty and trust.__ Trust between mentor and junior runs both ways, and the junior keeps it by telling the mentor before the room does.
- __Harmonious resolution.__ Take the evidence to the chief engineer privately, and bring it to the meeting only if nothing changes, which leaves him a route to fix it himself.

> __Prompt.__ Rectification of names in the gig economy, Singapore. A ride-hailing and food-delivery platform headquartered in Singapore classifies its tens of thousands of delivery riders as independent business partners rather than employees. The designation lets the corporation avoid paying mandatory central provident fund contributions, annual leave, and injury insurance, even though automated dispatch algorithms strictly dictate the riders' routes, working hours, and delivery fees.

- __Benevolence and care.__ Care runs to riders injured with no insurance, an exposure the arrangement creates rather than the riders choose.
- __Righteousness and justice.__ The label is false: work governed in every detail is not independent business, and righteousness refuses a false name.
- __Propriety and roles.__ An employer's role carries duties, and calling itself a platform empties none of them.
- __Filial duty and trust.__ Trust breaks between the firm and the people who work for it: the announced partnership and the exercised control cannot both be true.
- __Harmonious resolution.__ Rectification of names (zhengming, 正名) settles it: either the riders are partners, and the algorithm stops dictating routes, hours, and fees, or they are employees, and contributions and insurance follow.
