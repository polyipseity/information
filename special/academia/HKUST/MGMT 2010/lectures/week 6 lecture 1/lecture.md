Eight exercises apply virtue ethics and care ethics to ordinary university situations.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- the eight exercises that apply virtue ethics and care ethics to student situations ::@:: Ordinary university situations, where a virtue or a relation answers more than a rule does.
- what each of the eight exercises asks for in place of a verdict ::@:: A practice: a rule, a habit, a script, or a rewrite.

## virtue ethics exercises

A virtue ethicist asks what a person of good character would do here, and the mean locates the virtue between a deficiency and an excess.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what a virtue ethicist asks about an act rather than what a rule allows ::@:: What a person of good character would do here.
- what a virtue ethics exercise asks the reader to produce ::@:: The habit a good character would build, not the act that settles the case.
- where the doctrine of the mean puts every virtue on its spectrum ::@:: Between a deficiency and an excess.
- cowardice and recklessness beside courage, as the doctrine of the mean reads them ::@:: Cowardice is the deficiency, recklessness the excess, courage the mean.
- where the doctrine of the mean finds the mean in a particular case ::@:: In the case itself, not in a rule that fixes it in advance.

### golden mean of digital friendship

> __Prompt.__ A close friend constantly posts their emotional struggles on Instagram and Facebook, clearly crying out for attention. Some friends over-invest and burn out; others scroll past and ignore it. Draw the spectrum: the deficiency on the left is the cold, distant friend, and the excess on the right is the over-entangled, boundaries-violating friend. Define the virtue in the middle, the golden mean of digital presence, and give two concrete rules for how that friend responds.

The mean is presence that answers what was asked and stops at what one can hold. Answer once, in the channel the friend used, and ask whether the friend wants to be heard or helped. Name what one can offer before offering it, because a limit stated up front costs less than one reached by exhaustion.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- a friend who posts their distress publicly, between the friends who scroll past and the ones who burn out ::@:: The mean answers what was asked and stops at what the responder can hold.
- the deficiency and the excess that a friend's public distress draws out ::@:: Scrolling past is the cold deficiency; over-investing until burnout is the entangled excess.
- how a responder answers a friend's public distress out of the mean ::@:: Once, in the channel the friend used.
- what a responder asks the friend before answering a public distress post ::@:: Whether the friend wants to be heard or helped.
- what a responder names before offering help to a friend posting distress ::@:: The help one can give, named before giving it.
- why a responder names the limit before making an offer ::@:: A limit reached by exhaustion costs more than one stated first.

### contract of care

> __Prompt.__ Group project contracts usually carry penalties, as in "if you miss a meeting, you lose 5% of your grade", which ignores the vulnerabilities students actually face: sickness, family stress, and mental health. Draft a three-point contract of care that covers how the group will check in on each member's well-being, how a member can safely communicate a personal crisis, and how the group will redistribute tasks fairly to support a struggling member without harbouring resentment.

Three points keep the work moving and the person visible: a standing check-in on the member as well as the task, a way to declare a crisis without justifying it, and a recorded reallocation, so the help is a decision the group made rather than a favour owed.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what a group project contract built on grade penalties leaves out ::@:: The vulnerabilities students face: sickness, family stress, and mental health.
- the contract of care a group project sets beside its meeting penalties ::@:: An agreement that keeps the work moving and the person visible.
- what a contract of care checks on beside the group's task ::@:: The member's well-being, at a standing check-in.
- how a member declares a crisis under a contract of care ::@:: Without having to justify it.
- what a contract of care records when the group moves tasks ::@:: A reallocation of the work.
- why the reallocated task is written down in a contract of care ::@:: So the help reads as a decision the group made, not a favour owed.

### virtuous failure resume

> __Prompt.__ In a competitive academic culture, failure reads as a permanent mark on character. Choose a common university failure, such as failing a prerequisite midterm, being rejected from a prestigious student club, or freezing during a presentation. Write a virtuous failure resume entry for it: the initial emotional reaction and whether it was an excess or a deficiency, the virtue required to process the setback, such as humility, resilience, or magnanimity, and the concrete habit that will cultivate that virtue from now on.

Freezing during a presentation draws self-castigation, the excess, or indifference, the deficiency. The virtue between them is proper self-respect, which lets the failure count without becoming the whole account. The habit is rehearsing to one person before the room, so the first attempt happens somewhere safe.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what competitive academic culture makes a failure look like to the person who fails ::@:: A permanent mark on their character.
- freezing during a presentation, as the doctrine of the mean reads the reaction ::@:: Self-castigation is the excess, indifference the deficiency.
- the virtue that sits between self-castigation and indifference after a failure ::@:: Proper self-respect.
- what proper self-respect lets a failure do in one's own account ::@:: Count, without becoming the whole account.
- the habit that cultivates self-respect after freezing in a presentation ::@:: Rehearse to one person before the room, so the first attempt is safe.

### good student by function

> __Prompt.__ First-year students often define a good student by GPA, credentials, and internship offers, which are external instrumental goods. What is the unique ergon, the function, of a university student? From that function, name the top three internal virtues it takes to perform it excellently.

The function is to learn to judge within a field, not to collect scores about it. Three virtues follow: curiosity, which starts the inquiry; integrity, which keeps the conclusion honest; and open-mindedness, which lets a better argument change it. A perfect GPA gained by cheating passes the measure and fails the function, so the external good cannot define the role.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what the function argument asks about a thing before it names its virtues ::@:: What its characteristic activity is, since its good lies in doing that activity well.
- the ergon, or function, of a university student once scores are set aside ::@:: To learn to judge within a field, not to collect scores about it.
- what curiosity does in a student's function of learning to judge ::@:: It starts the inquiry.
- what integrity does in a student's function of learning to judge ::@:: It keeps the conclusion honest.
- what open-mindedness does in a student's function of learning to judge ::@:: It lets a better argument change the conclusion.
- why a perfect GPA gained by cheating fails the function argument ::@:: The external good is met while the function, learning to judge, is not, so the score cannot define the role.

### moral courage script

> __Prompt.__ During a student society meeting, a charismatic senior publicly mocks a junior classmate's ideas, and the room goes silent and awkward. Write three one-sentence responses: the cowardly one, which stays silent and looks at a phone; the reckless one, which yells at the senior and starts an unproductive shouting match; and the courteous one, which asserts a boundary while preserving the junior's dignity.

Silence records the mockery as agreement, and a louder insult changes the target without changing the pattern. Courteous courage is one sentence with a boundary in it, such as calling the shot cheap and asking to hear the rest of the idea; it moves the room back to the idea instead of starting a second fight.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- courteous courage in a meeting where a senior mocks a junior's idea ::@:: One sentence that holds a boundary and keeps the junior's dignity.
- why staying silent while a senior mocks a junior's idea reads as agreement ::@:: The room records no objection, so the mockery stands as its verdict.
- why yelling back at a senior who mocked a junior's idea is the reckless response ::@:: It answers one mockery with a bigger one, so the target changes and the pattern stays.
- what courteous courage does with the room's attention after a senior mocks a junior ::@:: Moves it back to the idea instead of starting a second fight.
- the sentence a courteous response to a senior's public mockery can run ::@:: Calling the shot cheap and asking to hear the rest of the idea.

### humble brag filter

> __Prompt.__ A classmate posts a polished LinkedIn update announcing that out of 5,000 applicants they were selected as the sole elite summer intern at a firm. Aristotle contrasted the boaster, who exaggerates, with the mock-modest person, who downplays reality falsely, and set truthfulness between them. Rewrite the post twice: once as plain truthfulness that owns the accomplishment without arrogance, and once as social grace that uplifts the people who were not selected.

Truthfulness states the fact and its size: the internship was won, 5,000 applied, and being pleased about it is no fault. Grace keeps those facts and moves the subject from the self to the work, naming what it took and what the classmates who missed out can still do.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- truthfulness about one's own achievement, between the boaster and the mock-modest ::@:: Stating the accomplishment and its size plainly, with neither exaggeration nor false downplaying.
- the boaster and the mock-modest person, as Aristotle set truthfulness between them ::@:: One exaggerates the self, the other downplays it falsely.
- what a truthful version of the 5,000-applicant internship post states ::@:: The internship was won, 5,000 applied, and being pleased is no fault.
- what social grace moves a post about the won internship toward ::@:: The work, naming what it took and what classmates who missed out can still do.
- how mock modesty differs from social grace in the internship post ::@:: Mock modesty drops the accomplishment; grace keeps the facts and looks outward.

## care ethics exercises

Care ethics starts from a concrete person one is responsible for, not from a rule that applies to everyone. Its hard cases set that person's claim against a rule or a group, and the response that counts is the one that keeps the relation.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what care ethics starts from, where a rule would apply to everyone ::@:: A concrete person one is responsible for.
- what a hard case in care ethics puts a person's claim against ::@:: A rule or a group.
- what counts as the right response in a care ethics hard case ::@:: The one that keeps the relation.

### particular care and impartial rules

> __Prompt.__ You are a student hall tutor. The hall rule is strict: no guests after 11:00 PM, and violators face immediate eviction. At 11:30 PM you find a resident's friend crying in the hallway. They fled an abusive domestic situation at home and have nowhere else to go tonight. One half of the room argues justice, that the rule prevents favouritism and maintains institutional order; the other argues care, that the immediate vulnerability of the friend comes first. Find a resolution that honours both.

The rule governs whether a guest stays in a room, not whether a person is left in a corridor at midnight. The friend is kept safe tonight in a staff space, the warden is told, and the exception is written down with its reason for review in the morning.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what the hall's 11:00 PM no-guest rule governs, once a resident's friend is in the corridor ::@:: Whether a guest stays in a room, not whether a person is left there at midnight.
- how a hall tutor keeps a friend who fled an abusive home safe at 11:30 PM ::@:: In a staff space, with the warden told.
- why the tutor writes the exception down with its reason ::@:: A recorded decision is one the institution can review, so care stays apart from favouritism.

### relational repair

> __Prompt.__ You accidentally leaked a close friend's private relationship secret during a casual lunch conversation, causing them public embarrassment and loss of face. In care ethics the goal is not to prove who was right or wrong under a rule but to restore the ruptured relationship. Draft a three-sentence verbal apology that acknowledges the specific relational harm and pain caused, takes direct and unqualified responsibility, and offers a concrete action to rebuild trust. It must avoid defensive excuses such as "I didn't mean to" and "you're overreacting".

Name the harm and how it travelled: what was told, to whom, and in front of whom. Take responsibility without a clause that shares it. Offer an action the friend can accept or refuse, such as telling the room where the story came from and keeping the friendship's business out of later lunches.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what care ethics aims at after a leaked secret, instead of who was right ::@:: A restored relationship, since the goal is repair rather than a verdict.
- the first of the three parts of the apology for a leaked secret ::@:: Naming the harm: what was told, to whom, and in front of whom.
- the second of the three parts of the apology for a leaked secret ::@:: Responsibility taken without a clause that shares it.
- the third of the three parts of the apology for a leaked secret ::@:: A concrete action the friend can accept or refuse.
- the action the apology offers the friend whose secret leaked ::@:: Telling the room where the story came from, and keeping the friendship's business out of later lunches.
- why "I didn't mean to" weakens the apology for a leaked secret ::@:: It asks the injured friend to manage the apologiser's intentions instead of the harm.
