# Regulation NMS

_Regulation NMS_, usually written Reg NMS, is the SEC rule adopted in June 2005 that turns the aim of the [national market system](national%20market%20system.md) into an enforceable rulebook. It is mostly about trade execution: at which price a trade may execute, and how fast it may happen.

| Rule | Number | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order Protection Rule | Rule 611 | No trade-through |
| Access Rule | Rule 610 | No locked or crossed market |
| Sub-Penny Rule | Rule 612 | Minimum tick size |
| Market Data Rule | Rules 601, 603 | Direct feed |

The first two protect the price, the third fixes the grid the price sits on, and the fourth governs how the market's data reaches its users. The order protection rule is described in [trade through](trade%20through.md), the access rule in [bid-ask spread](bid-ask%20spread.md), the sub-penny rule in [tick size](tick%20size.md), and the market data rule in [securities information processor](securities%20information%20processor.md).

## flickering quotes

_Flickering quotes_ are quotes submitted and cancelled repeatedly within a very short interval. They are exempt for up to one second.

The exemption exists because the data a venue reads is older than the market it describes. An exchange that executed an order at 10:00:00.000 on its own data has not traded through. Another exchange held a better price, and that price was reflected only at 10:00:00.050. A venue is held to the prices it could see, not to the prices that existed.

Two exemptions are order types rather than conditions on the data: an [intermarket sweep order](intermarket%20sweep%20order.md) and a [do-not-ship order](do-not-ship%20order.md). Each removes the obligation to route and trades the protection away for speed.
