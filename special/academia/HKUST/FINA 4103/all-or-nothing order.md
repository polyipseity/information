# all-or-nothing order

An _all-or-nothing (AON)_ order cannot be partially filled. A "limit sell for 100 units with AON" cannot trade against a "market buy of 50 units"; the broker holds it until enough liquidity appears to fill it entirely.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what an AON order requires ::@:: Full execution: it cannot be partially filled.
- what happens when an AON order encounters insufficient liquidity ::@:: It is held by the broker until enough liquidity appears to fill it entirely.
- an example of when AON prevents execution ::@:: A limit sell for 100 units with AON cannot trade against a market buy of 50 units.

## fill-or-kill order

A _fill-or-kill (FOK)_ order combines AON and IOC: it must fill entirely and immediately, or be cancelled.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what an FOK order combines ::@:: AON (must be fully filled) and IOC (must be filled immediately or cancelled).
- how an FOK order differs from a plain AON order ::@:: AON waits with the broker until the liquidity appears; FOK cancels the moment it does not.
