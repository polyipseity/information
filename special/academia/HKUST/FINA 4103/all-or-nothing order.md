# all-or-nothing order

An _all-or-nothing (AON)_ order cannot be partially filled. A limit sell for 100 units with AON cannot trade against a market buy of 50 units. The broker holds the order until enough liquidity appears to fill it entirely, so the visible book never shows it.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what an all-or-nothing (AON) order requires ::@:: Full execution: it cannot be partially filled.
- what happens when an all-or-nothing (AON) order encounters insufficient liquidity ::@:: It is held by the broker until enough liquidity appears to fill it entirely.
- an example of when all-or-nothing (AON) prevents execution ::@:: A limit sell for 100 units with AON cannot trade against a market buy of 50 units.
- whether an all-or-nothing (AON) order that cannot fill completely is displayed ::@:: No; the visible book leaves it out until enough liquidity is on hand to fill it entirely.

## fill-or-kill order

A _fill-or-kill (FOK)_ order combines AON with immediate-or-cancel (IOC): it must fill entirely and immediately, or be cancelled.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- what a fill-or-kill (FOK) order combines ::@:: All-or-nothing (AON), which must be fully filled, and immediate-or-cancel (IOC), which must be filled immediately or cancelled.
- how a fill-or-kill (FOK) order differs from an all-or-nothing (AON) order ::@:: AON rests with the broker until liquidity appears; FOK cancels at once if it does not.
