# market liquidity

Liquidity is the ability to execute a trade at a low cost and with a small price impact. In a liquid market a trade does not move the price, and in an infinitely liquid market every trading need would be filled with no additional cost.

---

- overview ::@:: The ability to execute a trade at a low cost and with a small price impact.
- what happens to the price when a trade is executed in a liquid market ::@:: Nothing: the trading does not move the price.
- what an infinitely liquid market does with trading needs ::@:: It fills all of them without additional costs.

## market and funding liquidity

_Liquidity_ is used in two senses. _Market_ liquidity is a property of a market, how cheaply an asset can be traded; _funding_ liquidity is about the financing a trader needs rather than about the market.

## trading cost and price impact

For a large order, the cost of trading is the bid–ask spread plus the price impact. The spread is the gap between the best ask and the best bid, and it is positive in general. What sets its size, and whether it measures trading cost at all, remain open questions. Price impact is the part of the cost that comes from the trade itself moving the price. It is what makes order size matter: one unit of a stock trades at the spread, while a large order pays much more. Because these costs fall on everyone who trades, liquidity matters for welfare.

The trade-off shows up in a choice between two limit-order books, for a purchase of 100 units of AAPL; prices are in dollars. Exchange A offers 100 units at 101, then 100 at 100 and 100 at 99, and bids 100 at 98 and 100 at 97. Exchange B quotes the same five prices but with different depth: 100 at 101, 80 at 100, and 20 at 99, against the same two bids. Both books show a best ask of 99 and a best bid of 98, so the quoted spread is the same. What sits at the best ask is not: 100 units on Exchange A, 20 on Exchange B. Which exchange is more liquid for the order? What changes if only one unit is needed? What changes under Regulation National Market System?
