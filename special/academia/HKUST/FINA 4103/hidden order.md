# hidden order

A _hidden order_ (or reserve order) is sent with a display instruction that hides part of the trading amount.

A hidden limit sell for 100 shares at 100 dollars with 50% display shows only 50 shares on the book; the rest are hidden.

Limit orders and quotes show a trader's intensity, and can reveal private, material information. An order that commits to trade visibly is easier for others to read than a hidden one.

## execution priority

Hidden orders have lower execution priority than displayed orders at the same price, so displayed orders standing at the same price execute first. Why they have lower priority is an open question. One account is that a displayed order commits to trade visibly, so it is the firmer commitment. Another is that the venue withholds priority to offset the information advantage that hiding confers.

## prevalence and market effects

Hidden orders make up more than 18% of trading on NASDAQ, 20% on Frankfurt, and 22% on Euronext-Paris (2019 data). They harm price discovery by hiding liquidity, but they promote competition and reduce trading costs for others. Boulatov and George (2013, _Review of Financial Studies_) find that hidden orders benefit the overall market.
