# order (exchange)

An _order_ is an instruction to buy or sell on a trading venue such as a stock exchange, bond market, or cryptocurrency exchange. It can be sent to a broker or directly to the venue through direct market access. An order is how a trading need is expressed and executed.

Trading needs come from different motives. A trader may hold a private value for an asset, such as a preference for a company's products; may trade on information or belief, such as good news about a company; or may trade for non-fundamental reasons, such as needing cash.

## what an order specifies

Every order carries a price, a quantity, and a direction, buy or sell. Beyond that, three factors decide how well it serves its purpose: priority and speed, execution quality and execution risk, and transparency.

Priority and speed decide whether an order is filled at the best available price. Execution quality covers the price obtained, and execution risk the chance the order does not execute at all. Transparency decides whether the order is publicly displayed.

## classes of instructions

Beyond price, quantity, and direction, order instructions fall into four classes. A time instruction sets how long an order stays active, an execution instruction governs whatever does not fill immediately, a price instruction moves the limit price around a reference point, and a display instruction sets how much of the order is visible. Immediate-or-cancel, all-or-nothing, and fill-or-kill are execution instructions; pegged and stop orders are price instructions; hidden and reserve orders are display instructions.

## time in force

A _day order_ is cancelled automatically at the end of the trading session, the default instruction for limit orders. A _good-till-cancel (GTC)_ order stays active until the trader or broker places a cancellation request. Extended-hours instructions name a finer interval: the pre-market runs from 8:00 to 9:15, and the after-market from 16:15 to 18:30.

A market or limit on open order activates at the opening auction, and a market or limit on close order at the closing auction. How time priority is computed for these orders is an open question.
