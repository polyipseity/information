# midterm examination

- HKUST MATH 2431

This page summarizes the written questions from the official midterm examination materials in paraphrased study-note form.

## multiple choice (questions 1–5)

> _Question 1._ How many probability measures exist on $\Omega=\{0,1\}$ with $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal P(\{0,1\})$?
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{uncountably many}@}
> - explanation: {@{For each $p\in[0,1]$, $\mathrm{Ber}(p)$ gives a different measure.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> _Question 2._ If $P[C\mid D]>P[C]$ with $P[C],P[D]>0$, which of the following must hold?
>
> - (A) $P[D\mid C]>P[D]$.
> - (B) $P[C]=P[D]$.
> - (C) $P[C\mid D]=P[D\mid C]$.
> - (D) $P[C]>P[D]$.
> - (E) $P[D\mid C]<P[D]$.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{(A) $P[D\mid C]>P[D]$.}@}
> - explanation: (A) is true, {@{the equivalence $P[C\mid D]>P[C]\iff P[C\cap D]>P[C]P[D]\iff P[D\mid C]>P[D]$}@}. <br/>
>     (B) is false, {@{taking $\Omega=\{1,2,3,4\}$, $P=U(\{1,2,3,4\})$, $C=\{1\}$, $D=\{1,2\}$ gives $P[C\mid D]=1/2>1/4=P[C]$ but $P[D]=1/2\neq1/4=P[C]$}@}. <br/>
>     (C) is false, {@{the same example gives $P[C\mid D]=1/2\neq1=P[D\mid C]$}@}. <br/>
>     (D) is false, {@{the same example gives $P[C]=1/4<1/2=P[D]$}@}. <br/>
>     (E) is false, {@{the opposite of (A)}@}.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> _Question 3._ Two groups of $4$ people each sit on opposite sides of a rectangular table. How many seating arrangements are possible?
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$2\cdot(4!)^2$}@}
> - explanation: {@{$4!$ per side, times $2$ for which side each group takes.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> _Question 4._ For a set $\Omega$ with $|\Omega|=n$, how many ways are there to choose a collection $E\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega)$?
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$2^{2^n}$}@}
> - explanation: {@{This counts $\mathcal P(\mathcal P(\Omega))$.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> _Question 5._ Let $F(x)=0$ for $x<0$, $F(0)=1/2$, $F(x)=1$ for $x>0$. Is $F$ a cumulative distribution function?
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{No, because $F$ is not right-continuous at $0$}@}
> - explanation: {@{$F(0)=1/2$ but $\lim_{x\downarrow0}F(x)=1$.}@}

## true/false (questions 6–10)

> _Question 6._ For a discrete probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal P(\Omega),P)$ and an event $B\in\mathcal P(\Omega)$, the set function $Q[A]=P[A\cap B]$ defines a probability measure on $(B,\mathcal P(B))$.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{False}@}
> - explanation: {@{Take $\Omega=\{0,1\}$, $P=\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)$, $B=\{0\}$; then $Q[B]=1/2\neq1$, so normalization fails.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> _Question 7._ For independent events $A,B$, $P[A\cup B]=P[A]+P[B]P[A^c]$.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{True}@}
> - explanation: {@{$P[A\cup B]=P[A]+P[B]-P[A\cap B]=P[A]+P[B]-P[A]P[B]=P[A]+P[B](1-P[A])=P[A]+P[B]P[A^c]$.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> _Question 8._ If $P[(a,a+1]]=Q[(a,a+1]]$ for all $a\in\mathbb R$, then $P=Q$.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{True}@}
> - explanation: {@{For any $x$, $F_P(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty P[(x-n,x-n+1]]=\sum_{n=1}^\infty Q[(x-n,x-n+1]]=F_Q(x)$, so the CDFs agree.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> _Question 9._ For a finite $\Omega$, if $|\delta(\mathcal E)|=|\sigma(\mathcal E)|$, then $\delta(\mathcal E)$ is a $\sigma$-algebra.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{True}@}
> - explanation: {@{Since $\delta(\mathcal E)\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal E)$ and both are finite with equal cardinality, $\delta(\mathcal E)=\sigma(\mathcal E)$.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> _Question 10._ The pointwise limit of a sequence of CDFs is always a CDF.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{False}@}
> - explanation: {@{$F_n(x)=\mathbf 1_{[1/n,\infty)}(x)\to\mathbf 1_{(0,\infty)}(x)$, which is not right-continuous at $0$.}@}

## long-form (questions 11–12)

> 11.a. Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ be a probability space and $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal F$. Define what it means for $\mathcal E$ to be $\cap$-stable.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$\mathcal E$ is $\cap$-stable if for all $A,B\in\mathcal E$, $A\cap B\in\mathcal E$}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> 11.b. Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ be a probability space and $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal F$. Define $\sigma(\mathcal E)$, the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal E$.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$\sigma(\mathcal E)=\bigcap\{\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega):\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal G,\ \mathcal G\text{ is a }\sigma\text{-algebra}\}$}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> 11.c. State Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{If $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega)$ is $\cap$-stable and $\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega)$ is a Dynkin system with $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal D$, then $\sigma(\mathcal E)\subseteq\mathcal D$}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> 11.d. Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ be a probability space and $\mathcal E_1,\mathcal E_2\subseteq\mathcal F$ be $\cap$-stable. Suppose $P[A\cap B]=P[A]P[B]$ for all $A\in\mathcal E_1$, $B\in\mathcal E_2$. Show this holds for all $A\in\sigma(\mathcal E_1)$, $B\in\sigma(\mathcal E_2)$.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{It holds for all $A\in\sigma(\mathcal E_1)$, $B\in\sigma(\mathcal E_2)$}@}
> - explanation: {@{Fix $A\in\mathcal E_1$}@} and {@{define $\mathcal D_A=\{B\in\mathcal F:P[A\cap B]=P[A]P[B]\}$}@}, then {@{show $\mathcal D_A$ is a Dynkin system containing $\mathcal E_2$}@}. <br/>
>     By {@{Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem}@}, {@{which gives $\sigma(\mathcal E_2)\subseteq\mathcal D_A$}@}. <br/>
>     Then {@{fix $B\in\sigma(\mathcal E_2)$}@} and {@{repeat}@}.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> 11.e. Let $\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega)$ be a Dynkin system that is also closed under finite unions. Show $\mathcal D$ is a $\sigma$-algebra.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$\mathcal D$ is a $\sigma$-algebra}@}
> - explanation: {@{$\mathcal D$ is already closed under complements}@}, and {@{for $A,B\in\mathcal D$ the intersection $A\cap B=(A^c\cup B^c)^c\in\mathcal D$}@} uses finite unions. <br/>
>     So {@{$\mathcal D$ is $\cap$-stable}@}, hence {@{a $\sigma$-algebra}@}.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> 12.a. Let $f(x)=c$ for $x\in\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty[n,n+2^{-n})$ and $f(x)=0$ elsewhere. Determine $c$ so that $f$ is a probability density.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$c=1$}@}
> - explanation: Normalization {@{requires $1=c\sum_{n=1}^\infty2^{-n}$}@}. <br/>
>     The geometric series {@{sums to $1$}@}, so {@{the constant is $c=1$}@}.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> 12.b. Show that a $2026$-periodic continuous nonnegative function $g\colon\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ cannot be a probability density.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{It cannot be a probability density}@}
> - explanation: If $g$ is not identically $0$, {@{continuity gives a positive integral over one period}@}, and {@{periodicity makes $\int_{-\infty}^\infty g(x)\,dx=\infty$}@}. <br/>
>     If $g$ is identically $0$, {@{normalization fails}@}.

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> 12.c. Let $p$ be a probability mass function on a countably infinite $\Omega$. Can $q(\omega)=2\sqrt{p(\omega)}$ be a probability mass function?
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{No}@}
> - explanation: {@{For $x\in[0,1]$, $\sqrt x\ge x$, so $\sum_\omega q(\omega)\ge2\sum_\omega p(\omega)=2$.}@}

<!-- markdownlint MD028 -->

> 12.d. The Rayleigh distribution $\mathrm{Ra}(\theta)$ has density $h(x)=\frac{x}{\theta^2}e^{-x^2/(2\theta^2)}\mathbf 1_{[0,\infty)}(x)$. Find its median $m$.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$m=\theta\sqrt{2\log2}$}@}
> - explanation: The distribution function {@{comes from integrating the density, $F(x)=\int_0^x\frac{t}{\theta^2}e^{-t^2/(2\theta^2)}\,dt$}@}. <br/>
>     Substituting {@{$u=t^2/(2\theta^2)$ with $du=t/\theta^2\,dt$ leaves $F(x)=1-e^{-x^2/(2\theta^2)}$ on $[0,\infty)$}@}. <br/>
>     Setting {@{$F(m)=1/2$ gives $m=\theta\sqrt{2\log2}$}@}.
