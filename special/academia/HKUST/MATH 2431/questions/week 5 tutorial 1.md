> In a 52-card deck, an opponent draws 5 cards. Given that the hand contains at least one queen, find the probability that it contains at least two queens.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$P(A_{\ge2}\mid A_{\ge1})=\frac{1-P(A_0)-P(A_1)}{1-P(A_0)}\approx0.1222$}@}
> - explanation: Defining {@{$A_j$ to mean exactly $j$ queens in 5 cards gives $P(A_j)=\dfrac{\binom4j\binom{48}{5-j}}{\binom{52}5}$}@}. Conditioning on {@{at least one queen gives $P(A_{\ge2}\mid A_{\ge1})=\frac{1-P(A_0)-P(A_1)}{1-P(A_0)}\approx0.1222$}@}, where {@{the numerator removes the 0-queen and 1-queen cases from the conditioned sample space}@}.

> A simple random walk on $\mathbb{Z}^2$ starts at $(0,0)$ and moves to a nearest neighbor uniformly at each step. Give an expression for the probability of returning to the origin after $2n$ steps.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$\frac1{4^{2n}}\sum_{k=0}^n\binom{2n}{k,k,n-k,n-k}$}@}
> - explanation: A return to the origin after $2n$ steps requires {@{vertical and horizontal cancellations}@}: if there are {@{$k$ up-steps then there must also be $k$ down-steps}@}, leaving {@{$2n-2k$ steps split as $n-k$ right and $n-k$ left}@}. >
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$\mathcal{B}_1=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$}@}
> - explanation: We prove {@{both inclusions}@}. {@{First, $\mathcal{B}_1\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$}@}: since $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})=\sigma(\{(a,b):a<b\})$, {@{it is enough to build each generator $(a,b]$ from open intervals}@}. > Derive the Poisson distribution from the usual small-time assumptions of a Poisson process.
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{$N_t\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda t)$, that is $P_n(t)=e^{-\lambda t}(\lambda t)^n/n!$}@}
> - explanation: Write $P_n(t)=P(N_t=n)$. The small-time assumptions say {@{the count behaves like a continuous-time state process}@} that {@{either stays put or jumps up by 1}@}: {@{with probability about $1-\lambda h$ nothing happens}@}, {@{with probability about $\lambda h$ exactly one jump occurs}@}, and {@{the chance of two or more jumps is only $o(h)$}@}. {@{For state 0 this gives $P_0(t+h)=P_0(t)(1-\lambda h)+o(h)$}@}, so {@{the solution is $P_0(t)=e^{-\lambda t}$}@}. For $n\ge1$, {@{the only first-order ways to be at $n$ at time $t+h$ are}@}: {@{already at $n$ and no jump, or at $n-1$ and one jump}@}, so $P_n(t+h)=P_n(t)(1-\lambda h)+P_{n-1}(t)(\lambda h)+o(h)$. Solving {@{the resulting recursion gives $P_n(t)=e^{-\lambda t}(\lambda t)^n/n!$}@}. Therefore {@{the count is Poisson, $N_t\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda t)$}@}. <!--SR:!fsrs,2027-04-25T00:00:00.000Z,291,291.42724554,1,2,7,0,0,2026-07-08T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2027-04-20T00:00:00.000Z,287,287.11697064,1,2,7,0,0,2026-07-07T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2027-04-20T00:00:00.000Z,287,287.11697064,1,2,7,0,0,2026-07-07T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2027-05-17T00:00:00.000Z,309,308.57643926,1,2,7,0,0,2026-07-12T00:00:00.000Z-->

> A television show contains the word "Smurf" according to a Poisson model with mean 7 in 60 minutes. What is the probability of hearing it at least 3 times in the first 26 minutes?
>
> ---
>
> - solution: {@{the probability is $P(X\ge3)=1-\sum_{k=0}^2e^{-91/30}(91/30)^k/k!\approx0.5842$}@}
> - explanation: Use {@{Poisson rate scaling}@}. <br/>
>     For 26 minutes the mean is $\lambda_{26}=7\cdot26/60=91/30$, so {@{the probability is $P(X\ge3)=1-\sum_{k=0}^2e^{-91/30}(91/30)^k/k!\approx0.5842$}@}, i.e. one minus {@{the probabilities of 0, 1, or 2 occurrences}@}.
