# index

- HKUST ELEC 2400
- name: Electronic Circuits
- credits: 4

---

ELEC 2400 is an undergraduate course in electronic circuits at HKUST covering fundamental electronic concepts for DC and AC circuits: Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws, Thevenin's and Norton's theorems, linearity and superposition, and nodal analysis. Later material covers sinusoidal steady state and phasor representation, frequency response, transfer functions and Bode plots, operational amplifiers, first-order transient analysis, and diode circuits. The course treats simple electronic circuits as functional building blocks that can be understood and used as tools.

## overview

- official course outline
    - fundamental concepts: charge, current, voltage, circuit modeling, lumped parameter model, Ohm's law, two-terminal element, reference direction, electric power, voltage and current sources, dependent sources, active and passive elements
    - DC analysis: circuit terminology, KCL, KVL, series and parallel connections, voltage and current dividers, nodal and mesh analyses, linearity, superposition, Thevenin's and Norton's theorems, source transformation
    - operational amplifiers: ideal op amp, voltage buffer, non-inverting amplifier, inverting amplifier, adder, difference amplifier, instrumentation amplifier, current source, negative impedance converter, V-to-I converter, ADC, DAC, differentiator, integrator
    - AC analysis: capacitor and inductor, sinusoidal excitation, steady-state and transient responses, complex numbers, phasor representation, magnitude and phase of the steady-state response, impedance, AC power
    - frequency response: transfer function, poles and zeros, Bode plots, low-pass, band-pass, and high-pass filters, first and second order systems
    - transient analysis: transient circuits, switch operations, RC and RL circuits, first-order transient response
    - diode circuits: diode models, clipping and clamping circuits, half-wave and full-wave rectifiers, Zener diode, regulator, charge pump

- intended learning outcomes
    - apply the fundamental circuit concepts to compute the output of basic electronic circuits in response to a DC input signal
    - recognize sinusoidal steady state characteristics with phasors and compute the output of basic electronic circuits in response to an AC input
    - compute the transient responses of basic electronic circuits consisting of capacitors and inductors
    - compute the characteristics of basic electronic circuits consisting of operational amplifiers and diodes
    - employ electronic instruments and perform experiments
    - apply CAD tools to simulate and analyze electronic circuits
- notes
    - tests and examinations are based mainly on the lecture and tutorial notes
