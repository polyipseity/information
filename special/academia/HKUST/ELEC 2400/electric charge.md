# electric charge

Charge, written $q$ or $Q$, and $q(t)$ when it varies with time, is the basic quantity of electrical circuits. It exists in two signs: like charges repel each other, and unlike charges attract. Every circuit operation, including energy transfer and information processing, is a movement of charge.

The coulomb, abbreviated C, is named for Charles-Augustin de Coulomb (1736-1806). Charge comes in whole units of the elementary charge. It is conserved.

## elementary charge

Charge is not continuous. The smallest indivisible amount is the elementary charge, exactly $e = 1.602176634\times10^{-19}\text{ C}$.

One coulomb is the charge of $1/(1.602176634\times10^{-19}) \approx 6.24\times10^{18}$ elementary charges. A proton carries $+e$ and an electron carries $-e$, so the charge of any body is a whole-number multiple of $e$, positive or negative.

## conservation of charge

Charge cannot be created or destroyed. In an isolated system, the total positive charge and the total negative charge are each constant. The constancy of the net charge alone is weaker.

A system is neutral when its total net charge is zero. Neutrality is a balance of the two signs, not the absence of charge. A circuit redistributes charge among its elements instead of consuming it.
