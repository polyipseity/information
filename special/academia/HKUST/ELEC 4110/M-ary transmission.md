# _M_-ary transmission

- see: [general/_M_-ary transmission](../../../../general/M-ary%20transmission.md)

{@{___M_-ary transmission__}@} is {@{a digital modulation method}@} where {@{each transmitted _symbol_ carries $\log_2 M$ _bits_}@} by selecting {@{one waveform from $M$ signal shapes}@}. Increasing {@{the _modulation order_ $M$}@} yields {@{higher spectral efficiency}@}, but {@{the signal becomes more sensitive to noise}@} and detection {@{grows more complex}@}. <!--SR:!2026-10-24,215,329!2026-11-06,238,330!2026-11-17,249,330!2027-04-22,381,370!2026-11-26,255,330!2027-03-05,342,361!2026-11-23,252,330!2026-11-25,254,330-->

Unlike {@{[binary modulation](binary%20modulation.md)}@} where {@{a _bit_ and a _symbol_ are the same}@}, here {@{a _symbol_ represents more than one _bit_}@}. <!--SR:!2027-01-08,293,349!2027-01-21,304,349!2026-11-09,241,330-->

## background

{@{Digital communication systems}@} consist of {@{three components}@}: {@{a _transmitter_ (often called a digital modulator)}@}, {@{a _receiver_ (or demodulator)}@}, and {@{the _physical channel_ that carries the signal between them}@}. <!--SR:!2027-04-11,371,361!2027-01-30,311,349!2027-02-26,335,361!2027-03-22,356,370!2027-04-30,390,370-->

{@{Performance}@} is measured by {@{the bit error rate (BER) and the bit rate}@}: {@{BER}@} is {@{the fraction of incorrectly decoded bits}@}; {@{the bit rate}@} is {@{bits transmitted per second}@}. <!--SR:!2026-11-25,254,330!2027-04-20,377,361!2026-11-18,248,330!2027-03-09,341,349!2027-05-29,396,361!2027-03-27,357,361-->

{@{Two fundamental resources}@} {@{limit digital communication}@}: {@{bandwidth and power}@}. {@{Bandwidth}@} is {@{the frequency range available for transmission}@}, and {@{power}@} sets {@{the achievable signal-to-noise ratio}@}. {@{The trade-off between bandwidth and power}@} shapes {@{modulation and coding}@} choices. <!--SR:!2027-04-28,386,361!2027-06-11,409,370!2027-03-24,355,361!2027-02-22,330,349!2027-02-23,332,361!2027-04-18,376,361!2027-04-10,370,361!2027-03-20,354,370!2027-04-13,371,361-->

### digital modulation

{@{A digital modulator}@} maps {@{discrete _data symbols_}@} to {@{continuous-time waveforms}@} called {@{_modulation symbols_}@}. Each symbol is {@{a finite-duration pulse shaped to limit spectral leakage}@}. For instance, {@{a binary modulator}@} takes {@{a single bit $\{0,1\}$}@} and {@{outputs one of two pulses, say $\{s_0(t),\, s_1(t)\}$}@}. {@{Pulse trains}@} may be {@{rectangular or smoothly shaped}@} depending on {@{the pulse shaping filter}@}. <!--SR:!2027-04-21,379,361!2027-04-26,385,370!2027-04-27,384,361!2027-03-05,339,349!2027-03-29,361,361!2027-04-29,387,361!2027-01-16,300,349!2027-01-28,311,349!2027-01-07,292,349!2027-03-19,350,349!2027-03-17,348,349-->

{@{The rate at which _symbols_ are transmitted}@} is {@{the _baud rate_ (or _symbol rate_)}@}, {@{the reciprocal of the _symbol duration_ $T_s$}@}: {@{$$R_{\text{baud} }=\frac{1}{T_s} \,.$$}@} Since {@{each symbol may carry multiple bits}@}, the bit rate can exceed {@{the baud rate}@}. <!--SR:!2027-02-20,331,361!2027-01-29,310,349!2026-11-09,241,330!2027-05-07,394,361!2027-03-17,348,349!2027-01-28,309,349-->

{@{An $M$-ary modulator}@} extends this idea by allowing {@{one of $M$ distinct symbols}@} to {@{represent $\log_2 M$ bits}@}. {@{The _modulation alphabet_}@} is then {@{$\mathcal{S}=\{s_0(t),\, s_1(t),\,\dots,\, s_{M-1}(t)\}$}@}. <!--SR:!2027-04-14,373,361!2026-11-14,246,330!2027-01-02,288,349!2026-11-20,250,330!2027-03-15,351,370-->

### channel

{@{A common model for the _channel_}@} is {@{_additive white Gaussian noise_ (AWGN)}@}. In this model {@{the received signal $y(t)$}@} equals {@{the transmitted waveform $x(t)$ plus a zero-mean Gaussian noise process $n(t)$}@}: {@{$$y(t)=x(t)+n(t) \,.$$}@} {@{The AWGN channel}@} introduces {@{only random fluctuations}@} without {@{altering the waveform shape}@}. <!--SR:!2026-11-25,254,330!2027-01-17,300,349!2027-03-15,349,370!2026-11-18,248,330!2026-11-20,250,330!2027-03-13,348,361!2027-03-10,345,361!2026-11-20,251,330-->

### energy and power

Assuming {@{_equiprobable_ symbols}@}, {@{the _average energy per symbol_ $E_s$ and _transmit power_ $P_\text{tx}$}@} are {@{$$E_s=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^ME_{\!s,i},\qquad P_\text{tx}=E_s/T_s \,,$$}@} where {@{$E_{s,i}$ are the _symbol energies_ and $T_s$ is the _symbol duration_}@}. For a binary modulator {@{the average energy per _bit_ $E_b$ equals $E_s$}@}. <!--SR:!2027-03-09,344,361!2027-04-11,371,361!2027-03-25,355,361!2026-11-18,250,330!2027-02-28,338,361-->

### bandwidth

{@{The transmission bandwidth $W$}@} is {@{the occupied frequency range}@} of {@{a pulse-shaped digital signal}@}. <!--SR:!2027-02-04,315,349!2027-05-03,391,370!2027-02-10,320,349-->

{@{The _modulation efficiency_ \(_spectral efficiency_\)}@} of {@{a digital link}@} is {@{bounded by the Nyquist–Hartley limit}@}: {@{an alphabet with $M$ symbols}@} can carry {@{$N=\log_{2}M$ bits per symbol (bpcu)}@}. <!--SR:!2027-01-01,287,349!2027-01-24,305,349!2027-05-30,397,361!2027-02-25,331,349!2027-04-01,362,361-->

For {@{a baseband system}@} whose {@{pulse shaping}@} limits {@{the bandwidth to $B$}@} Hz, {@{the maximum symbol rate without intersymbol interference}@} is {@{$2B$ symbols/s}@}. Consequently {@{the spectral efficiency}@} cannot exceed {@{$$\eta_{\max}= 2N\;\text{bits/s/Hz} \,,$$}@} where _N_ is {@{the modulation efficiency (bpcu)}@}. <!--SR:!2026-11-24,255,330!2027-02-03,316,349!fsrs,2029-01-20T00:00:00.000Z,885,885.29104986,1,2,9,0,0,2026-08-19T00:00:00.000Z!2027-02-25,335,361!2027-03-19,353,370!2026-11-17,249,330!2027-02-24,330,349!2027-06-24,422,392-->

In {@{a passband link}@} of {@{total bandwidth $W$ Hz}@}, {@{the signal}@} can be {@{down-converted to baseband with cutoff $W/2$}@}. With a {@{conventional double-sideband scheme (QAM, ASK, PSK, OFDM)}@}, the symbol rate is limited to {@{$W$ symbols/s}@} and {@{$$\eta_{\max}=N \;\text{bits/s/Hz} \,,$$}@} where _N_ is {@{the modulation efficiency (bpcu)}@}. With {@{digital single-sideband modulation}@}, the passband bandwidth {@{$W$}@} maps directly to {@{baseband width $W$}@}, allowing {@{a symbol rate up to $2W$}@} and {@{$$\eta_{\max}=2N \;\text{bits/s/Hz} \,.$$}@} <!--SR:!2027-04-02,364,370!2027-04-17,374,361!2027-03-08,340,349!2027-03-02,338,361!2027-03-07,341,349!2027-02-01,314,349!2027-04-30,388,361!2027-03-26,358,361!2026-11-18,250,330!2027-04-01,362,361!2027-04-10,370,361!fsrs,2029-02-20T00:00:00.000Z,909,908.9860397,1,2,9,0,0,2026-08-26T00:00:00.000Z!2027-03-31,362,361-->

With {@{a raised-cosine or similar filter}@} of {@{roll-off factor $\alpha$}@}, the bandwidth {@{widens by $1+\alpha$}@}. <!--SR:!2026-11-21,251,330!2026-11-14,245,330!2027-03-23,354,361-->

### signal space

{@{Each signal $s_i(t)$}@} can be projected onto {@{an _orthonormal_ basis $\{\phi_k(t)\}_{k=1}^N$}@} to yield the coordinate vector {@{$$\mathbf{s}_i=[a_{i1},\dots,a_{iN}]^\top$$}@} in {@{an $N$-dimensional Euclidean space}@}. {@{The set of all such vectors}@} forms {@{the _constellation_}@}. <!--SR:!2027-02-03,316,349!2027-05-26,393,361!2027-03-06,338,349!2027-02-18,327,361!2027-04-09,369,361!2027-01-16,299,349-->

{@{The Gram–Schmidt process}@} converts {@{any _linearly independent_ set of waveforms}@} into {@{an _orthonormal_ basis}@}. For {@{many standard modulations (e.g., PSK, QAM)}@} {@{the basis is already known}@}: {@{$$\begin{aligned} \phi_1(t) & = +\sqrt{2/T}\cos(2\pi ft) \\ \phi_2(t) & = -\sqrt{2/T}\sin(2\pi ft) \,, \end{aligned}$$}@} where {@{$f$ is a multiple of $1 / T$}@} and {@{$\sqrt{2 / T}$ _normalizes_ the basis functions}@}. Using {@{trigonometric identities}@}, {@{any real sinusoid of the same frequency $f$}@} can be {@{written as a linear combination of these two basis functions}@}. <!--SR:!2027-03-04,341,370!2027-06-18,416,370!2027-02-18,325,349!2027-05-30,397,361!2027-04-28,385,361!2027-01-15,298,349!2027-04-05,366,361!2027-02-22,329,349!2027-03-04,340,361!2027-01-21,304,349!2027-05-03,391,361-->

### constellation diagram

{@{A _constellation diagram_}@} is {@{a 2D plot of the possible symbols (waveforms)}@} in {@{a digital modulation scheme}@}. Each point corresponds to {@{one symbol}@}, with coordinates given by {@{the projection onto an orthogonal basis}@}—typically {@{the in-phase and quadrature components}@}. Larger {@{minimum Euclidean separations between points}@} yield {@{lower symbol-error rates at a given SNR}@}. <!--SR:!2026-11-25,254,330!2026-11-25,254,330!2027-04-07,367,361!2027-01-15,298,349!2027-02-21,331,361!2027-05-25,392,361!2027-01-07,292,349!2027-02-12,324,361-->

{@{The real-valued I (in-phase) and Q (quadrature) components}@} of {@{a baseband signal}@} combine into {@{a single complex number $s(t)=I(t)+jQ(t)$}@}, exploiting {@{the isomorphism between $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the complex plane}@}. {@{This complex representation}@} simplifies {@{analysis of modulation schemes}@} like {@{PSK and QAM}@}. <!--SR:!2027-03-28,358,361!2026-11-26,255,330!2027-05-21,388,361!2027-04-23,380,361!2027-03-24,357,361!2027-04-27,385,361!2027-02-02,315,349-->

## modulation families

When comparing {@{modulator designs}@}, designers weigh {@{_performance_ against _resource_ constraints}@}. {@{Amplitude shift keying (ASK)}@} varies {@{the carrier amplitude}@}; it is {@{simple to generate}@} but {@{highly vulnerable to fading and additive noise}@}. {@{Frequency shift keying (FSK), particularly its binary form}@}, switches {@{between two distinct frequencies}@}; {@{the orthogonal tones}@} yield {@{larger minimum-distance separation for a given power}@}, making FSK {@{more robust}@} at the expense of {@{bandwidth}@}. So for channels where {@{signal integrity matters more than spectral efficiency}@}, FSK is {@{preferred over ASK}@}. <!--SR:!2027-04-04,365,361!2026-11-21,251,330!2026-11-17,247,330!2026-11-09,241,330!2027-04-12,372,361!2026-12-27,283,349!2027-03-21,354,361!2026-11-24,253,330!2027-03-08,341,349!2027-04-25,383,361!2027-02-26,332,349!2027-03-04,337,349!2026-11-23,254,330!2027-01-19,303,349-->

For {@{higher data rates within fixed bandwidth}@}, {@{M-ary PSK or QAM}@} is preferred. These {@{constellations}@} keep {@{the signal space two-dimensional regardless of $M$}@}, so bandwidth {@{does not grow with symbol density}@}; however, {@{increasing $M$ condenses constellation points}@} and requires {@{higher transmit power to maintain a target error probability}@}. For example, {@{a terrestrial wireless link}@} with {@{limited spectrum and high throughput demands}@} would use {@{M-ary QAM (e.g., 64-QAM, 256-QAM, 1024-QAM, 4096-QAM)}@} for {@{the highest spectral efficiency}@}. <!--SR:!2027-03-27,361,370!2027-01-13,297,349!2026-11-16,248,330!2027-02-05,317,349!2027-01-14,297,349!2027-04-17,375,361!2027-04-06,368,370!2027-03-30,362,361!2027-02-12,321,349!2027-03-09,344,361!2027-01-31,313,349-->

Conversely, for {@{higher data rates within fixed transmit power}@}, {@{M-ary FSK}@} preserves {@{orthogonality}@} at the cost of {@{expanding bandwidth proportionally to $M$}@}, achieving {@{high power efficiency but low spectral efficiency}@}. For example, {@{a space mission}@} with {@{severe path loss and limited transmitter power}@} favors FSK for {@{satellite-to-earth links}@}. <!--SR:!2026-11-26,255,330!2027-06-18,416,370!2027-01-29,310,349!2026-11-19,249,330!2027-03-03,339,361!2027-05-07,394,361!2027-04-02,364,361!2027-01-13,297,349-->

### M-PSK

- see: [M-PSK](M-PSK.md)

{@{_M-ary phase shift keying_ (M-PSK)}@} maps {@{$\log_2M$ bits onto one of $M$ equally spaced points}@} on {@{a unit circle in the constellation diagram}@}, forming {@{a regular polygon constellation}@}. The modulation is {@{purely phase-based}@}; {@{amplitude remains constant}@}, making M-PSK {@{immune to linear amplitude distortion}@} but {@{susceptible to phase noise and nonlinearity}@}. $M$ can be {@{increased without increasing bandwidth}@}, raising {@{spectral efficiency}@} at the expense of {@{higher bit error rates}@}. <!--SR:!2027-01-12,296,349!2027-02-19,328,349!2026-11-23,254,330!2027-03-10,345,361!2026-11-25,254,330!2027-02-13,321,349!2026-11-15,247,330!2027-02-21,330,349!2027-03-28,358,361!2027-03-13,347,361!2027-05-24,391,361-->

For {@{an $M$-ary PSK system}@} {@{the transmitted waveform corresponding to symbol index $m\in\{0,\dots ,M-1\}$}@} is {@{$$s_m(t)=A\,\cos \!\bigl(2\pi f_c t+\varphi_m\bigr),\qquad 0\le t<T_s \,,$$}@} where {@{$f_c$ is the carrier frequency, $T_s$ is the symbol duration}@}, and {@{$\varphi_m=\frac{2\pi m}{M}$}@}. Typically, {@{$f_c$ is a multiple of $1 / T_s$}@}. {@{The constant amplitude $A=\sqrt{\dfrac{2E}{T_s} }$}@} ensures {@{each symbol has average energy $E$}@}. <!--SR:!2027-05-20,387,361!2027-03-01,337,361!2026-11-18,248,330!2026-11-25,254,330!2027-02-22,333,361!fsrs,2028-05-14T00:00:00.000Z,681,680.57191957,1,2,8,0,0,2026-07-03T00:00:00.000Z!2027-06-08,406,370!2027-03-18,352,361-->

{@{The $M$-ary PSK constellation}@} has {@{$M$ equally spaced points on a unit circle}@}, each distinguished by {@{phase angle alone with identical amplitude}@}, forming {@{a regular polygon when plotted with orthogonal basis functions}@}. <!--SR:!2027-06-07,405,370!2027-06-18,416,370!2027-02-19,326,349!2027-04-23,381,361-->

{@{_Binary PSK_ \(BPSK\), _quadrature PSK_ \(QPSK\), and _8-PSK_}@} are {@{the most common special cases}@}: {@{BPSK}@} uses {@{two antipodal points ($M=2$)}@} and conveys {@{one bit per symbol}@}; {@{QPSK}@} employs {@{four points arranged in a square grid ($M=4$)}@}, delivering {@{two bits per symbol}@}; {@{8-PSK}@} uses {@{eight equally spaced points on the unit circle}@} and conveys {@{three bits per symbol}@}. All preserve {@{the fixed-tone bandwidth characteristic of PSK systems}@}; only {@{two orthogonal carrier tones (in-phase and quadrature)}@} are {@{used regardless of $M$}@}. <!--SR:!2027-03-30,360,361!2027-03-13,346,361!2027-04-12,371,361!2027-06-09,407,370!2027-03-12,345,349!2027-02-10,320,349!2027-03-31,361,361!2027-01-08,293,349!2027-03-09,344,361!2026-11-08,240,330!2027-02-09,320,349!2027-03-04,340,361!2027-03-18,351,361!2027-01-13,297,349-->

Under {@{equal symbol energies and two-dimensional signal space}@}, {@{QSK is optimal for quartic modulation}@} as {@{any alternative quartic scheme}@} must {@{also use four symbols}@}; {@{the only way to improve performance}@} would be to {@{increase minimum Euclidean distance}@}, which is {@{already maximal for QPSK}@}. <!--SR:!2027-01-14,297,349!2027-03-16,351,361!2026-11-12,243,330!2027-05-21,388,361!2027-02-19,328,361!2027-04-01,363,361!2027-03-30,362,361-->

### M-QAM

- see: [M-QAM](M-QAM.md)

{@{_Quadrature amplitude modulation_ (M-QAM)}@} extends {@{PSK}@} by {@{varying both phase and amplitude}@}, arranging {@{$\sqrt{M}\times\sqrt{M}$ points}@} on {@{a rectangular grid in the complex plane}@}. Each symbol carries {@{$\log_2M$ bits}@}. Like PSK, $M$ can be {@{increased without increasing bandwidth}@}; QAM achieves {@{equal spectral efficiency to PSK for comparable error performance}@} at {@{higher signal-to-noise ratios}@}. M-QAM requires {@{two orthogonal carrier tones}@} regardless of {@{$M$}@}. <!--SR:!2027-05-09,396,361!2027-02-21,332,361!2027-01-02,288,349!2027-02-21,328,349!2027-03-07,343,361!2026-11-21,251,330!2027-02-17,327,361!2026-11-26,255,330!2026-11-12,243,330!2027-06-03,401,370!2027-04-12,373,370-->

For {@{symbol index $m = 0, \ldots, M - 1$}@} {@{the waveform}@} is {@{$$s_m(t)=I_m\,\sqrt{\frac{2E}{T_s} }\;\cos(2\pi f_c t)-Q_m\,\sqrt{\frac{2E}{T_s} }\;\sin(2\pi f_c t), \qquad 0\le t<T_s\,,$$}@} where {@{$I_m$ and $Q_m$ are the in-phase and quadrature amplitudes}@} chosen from {@{a rectangular grid}@} (e.g., {@{$\{-3,-1,1,3\}$ for 16-QAM}@}). {@{The scaling factor $\sqrt{2E/T_s}$}@} ensures {@{each symbol has unit average energy $E$}@}. <!--SR:!2027-02-09,319,349!2027-05-23,390,361!2026-11-18,248,330!2027-01-27,308,349!2027-03-29,359,361!2027-01-23,306,349!2027-02-14,325,361!2027-02-18,329,361-->

{@{An $M$-ary QAM constellation}@} forms {@{a $\sqrt{M}\times\sqrt{M}$ rectangular grid}@} with {@{the horizontal axis as the in-phase component and the vertical axis as the quadrature component}@}. This {@{maximizes Euclidean distance between adjacent symbols}@} while keeping {@{average power bounded}@}. <!--SR:!2027-05-19,406,370!2027-02-02,313,349!2027-02-06,318,349!2027-03-23,357,361!2027-04-03,365,361-->

In practice, {@{16-QAM}@} outperforms {@{16-PSK}@}. Both use {@{sixteen symbols}@}, but QAM distributes {@{points on a rectangular grid rather than confining them to a circle}@}, allowing {@{larger symbol separations at lower energy per bit}@}. 16-QAM thus delivers {@{better error performance at moderate SNRs}@}. <!--SR:!2027-03-18,349,349!2027-03-29,359,361!2027-01-28,309,349!2027-03-24,354,361!2027-03-16,349,361!2027-05-28,395,361-->

### M-FSK

- see: [M-FSK](M-FSK.md)

{@{_Frequency shift keying_ (M-FSK)}@} assigns {@{$\log_2M$ bits to one of $M$ orthogonal carrier frequencies}@}. Each symbol is {@{a single tone at a different frequency}@}, making FSK {@{resistant to amplitude fading and nonlinearity}@}—useful in {@{power-limited or distorted channels}@}. {@{The bandwidth expands linearly with $M$}@}, though, since {@{each additional frequency}@} must {@{fit in the channel spectrum}@}. <!--SR:!2026-11-25,254,330!2026-11-14,246,341!2027-02-25,334,361!2027-03-07,342,361!2027-03-16,350,370!2027-05-05,392,361!2026-11-26,255,330!2027-02-28,336,349-->

{@{The transmitted waveform for symbol index $m = 0, \ldots, M - 1$}@} is {@{a single tone at one of $M$ distinct frequencies}@}: {@{$$s_m(t)=A\,\cos \!\bigl(2\pi(f_c+\Delta f_m)t\bigr), \qquad 0\le t<T_s \,,$$}@} where {@{$f_c = n / T_s$ for some positive integer $n$ and $\Delta f_m$ denotes the frequency offset for symbol $m$}@}. The offsets are chosen so {@{the tones are orthogonal over $T_s$}@} (e.g., {@{$\Delta f_m=m\,\Delta f_{\min}$ where $\Delta f_{\min} = 1 / T_s$}@}). With {@{$A=\sqrt{2E/T_s}$}@}, each tone has {@{average energy $E$}@}. <!--SR:!2027-05-18,405,370!2027-03-05,337,349!2026-11-23,252,330!2027-03-26,356,361!2027-04-19,376,361!2027-04-04,364,361!2027-05-24,391,361!2026-11-26,255,330-->

{@{The constellation for $M$-ary FSK}@} consists of {@{$M$ distinct tones}@}, each at a different frequency (equivalently, {@{unit vectors in $M$-dimensional space}@}). <!--SR:!2027-04-19,377,361!2027-04-10,370,361!2027-05-27,394,361-->

## optimal receiver

{@{The receiver}@} must decide {@{which symbol was transmitted}@} from {@{the noisy observation $\mathbf{y}$}@}. {@{_Optimality_}@} means {@{minimizing the _bit error rate_ (BER)}@}. <!--SR:!2026-11-15,246,330!2027-05-10,397,361!2027-05-30,397,361!2026-11-23,254,330!2027-01-22,303,349-->

### noise vector

{@{The channel}@} adds {@{noise $n(t)$ with power spectral density $N_0/2$}@}. In {@{vector form}@}, {@{$\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{s}_i+\mathbf{n}$}@}, where {@{$\mathbf{n}$ is a zero-mean Gaussian random vector}@} with {@{covariance matrix $(N_0/2)\mathbf{I}$}@}. ### minimum distance

{@{The _minimum distance_ (MD) rule}@} is {@{optimal detection for _equiprobable_ symbols in an AWGN channel}@}. After {@{demodulating and digitizing the input waveform $y(t)$}@}, the receiver computes its {@{projection onto the $D$-dimensional basis $\{\phi_i(t)\}$}@} to form {@{a signal space vector $$\mathbf{y}=\bigl[\langle y,\phi_1\rangle,\dots ,\langle y,\phi_D\rangle\bigr]^T \,.$$}@} It then evaluates {@{the Euclidean distance to every candidate point $s_i$}@}: {@{$$d(\mathbf{y},s_i)=\|\mathbf{y}-s_i\|_2, \quad i=1,\dots ,\mu \,.$$}@} The output is {@{the index of the closest point}@}: {@{$$s^*=\arg\min_{i}\,d(\mathbf{y},s_i) \,.$$}@} <!--SR:!2027-05-06,393,361!fsrs,2029-07-15T00:00:00.000Z,999,999.02799132,1,2,9,0,0,2026-10-20T00:00:00.000Z!2027-04-26,384,370!2027-05-26,393,361!2027-04-05,365,361!2026-12-25,281,349!2027-03-08,343,349-->

### complexity

### peak-to-average power ratio

In {@{real-world radio front ends}@}, {@{power amplifiers (PAs)}@} exhibit {@{nonlinear behavior}@}: {@{signals whose instantaneous amplitude}@} {@{exceeds the linear region}@} are {@{clipped}@}, producing {@{spectral regrowth \(unwanted bandwidth broadening\)}@} that violates {@{regulatory emission masks}@}. ## fundamental limits

In {@{digital communication theory}@}, {@{the achievable data rate of a channel}@} is {@{bounded by Shannon's capacity theorem}@}. For {@{an AWGN link}@}, {@{the maximum reliable rate}@} is {@{$$C = B \log_2(1 + \tfrac{S}{N})$$}@}, where {@{$B$ is the bandwidth and $S/N$ is the signal-to-noise ratio at the receiver}@}. Only two resources—{@{transmit power (which determines $S/N$)}@} and {@{bandwidth}@}—can {@{increase this limit}@}. <!--SR:!2027-07-22,445,392!2027-06-28,426,392!2027-07-09,432,392!2027-08-27,476,392!2027-08-10,464,392!2027-06-16,414,392!2027-08-17,466,392!2027-07-05,433,392!2027-07-07,435,392!2027-06-29,427,392-->

{@{A convenient comparison metric}@} is {@{_spectral efficiency_ $\eta = R/B$ (bits/s/Hz)}@}. {@{The required $E_b/N_0$}@} can then be expressed as {@{a function of $\eta$}@}: {@{$$\frac{E_b}{N_0} = \frac{2^{\,\eta}-1}{\eta}$$}@}. This defines {@{a boundary in the $(\eta,E_b/N_0)$ plane}@}: {@{points above the curve}@} are {@{infeasible}@}; {@{points below}@} are {@{theoretically attainable with sufficiently long block codes}@}. <!--SR:!2027-08-18,467,392!2027-07-10,433,392!2027-07-01,429,392!2027-08-28,477,392!2027-07-02,430,392!2027-08-17,466,392!2027-07-05,428,392!2027-08-30,479,392!2027-07-26,449,392!2027-09-01,481,392-->

### fundamental limits vs. modulation families

{@{M‑ary PSK (MPSK) and M‑QAM (MQAM)}@} occupy {@{higher spectral efficiencies ($\eta \ge 1$)}@}. BPSK achieves {@{$\eta = 1$}@}, QPSK reaches {@{$\eta = 2$}@}, and {@{16‑QAM}@} attains {@{$\eta = 4$}@}. In practice, {@{uncoded MPSK and MQAM}@} require {@{much more $E_b/N_0$ than the Shannon boundary}@}: BPSK needs about {@{10&nbsp;dB for low BER}@}, while {@{the capacity limit at $\eta=1$ is around 0&nbsp;dB}@}. {@{The gap}@} widens with {@{constellation size}@}, which is why {@{channel coding is needed to approach fundamental limits}@}. <!--SR:!2027-08-09,463,392!2027-06-28,426,392!2027-07-09,432,392!2027-08-21,470,392!2027-08-25,474,392!2027-09-12,489,392!2027-09-08,485,392!2027-07-27,450,392!2027-07-20,443,392!2027-06-26,424,392!2027-07-01,429,392!2027-07-30,453,392!2027-08-31,480,392-->
