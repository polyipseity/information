# bandwidth (computing)

Bandwidth is the number of bits a link carries per unit time, in bits per second. It is the "width" of the link in the [pipe picture](network%20delay.md#pipe%20model) whose length is the propagation delay. Transmission delay falls as the bandwidth rises, and a link is quoted by both numbers at once, as `1 Gbps x 10 ms`.

---

Flashcards for this section are as follows:

- overview ::@:: Bandwidth is the number of bits a link sends or receives per unit time, measured in bits per second.
- what does "width of the link" name? ::@:: Its bandwidth, as against the length that names the propagation delay.

## units

Bandwidth is measured in bits per second, abbreviated bps. Bytes per second, Bps, is used elsewhere but not in networking: a rate in Bps is eight times the same rate in bps. The prefixes step by powers of 1000, not by 1024: 1 Kbps is 1000 bps, 1 Mbps is 1000 Kbps, and 1 Gbps is 1000 Mbps.
