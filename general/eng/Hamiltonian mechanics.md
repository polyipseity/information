## overview

### phase space coordinates \(_p_, _q_\) and Hamiltonian _H_

> {@{__Proof__ (annotation: of Hamilton's equations from the Euler–Lagrange equation)}@}
>
> {@{The Hamiltonian ${\mathcal {H} }({\boldsymbol {p} },{\boldsymbol {q} })$}@} is {@{the [Legendre transform](Legendre%20transform.md) of the Lagrangian ${\mathcal {L} }({\boldsymbol {q} },{\dot {\boldsymbol {q} } })$}@}, thus one has
>
> |                                                                                                                                                                              |                               |
> | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------: | :---------------------------: |
> | {@{$${\mathcal {L} }({\boldsymbol {q} },{\dot {\boldsymbol {q} } })+{\mathcal {H} }({\boldsymbol {p} },{\boldsymbol {q} })={\boldsymbol {p} }{\dot {\boldsymbol {q} } }$$}@} | <a id="math_1"></a> __\(1\)__ |
>
> where {@{${\boldsymbol {p} }=\partial {\mathcal {L} }/\partial {\dot {\boldsymbol {q} } }$}@}.
>
> By rearranging {@{the equation ${\boldsymbol {p} }=\partial {\mathcal {L} }/\partial {\dot {\boldsymbol {q} } }$}@}, we can write {@{${\dot {\boldsymbol {q} } }$ in terms of ${\boldsymbol {q} }$ and ${\boldsymbol {p} }$ as ${\dot {\boldsymbol {q} } }({\boldsymbol {q} },{\boldsymbol {p} })$}@}. So __[1](#math%201)__ becomes {@{an equation in two variables}@}: {@{${\boldsymbol {q} }$ and ${\boldsymbol {p} }$}@}.
>

### generalization to quantum mechanics through Poisson bracket

{@{Hamilton's equations}@} above work {@{well for [classical mechanics](classical%20mechanics.md)}@}, but not {@{for [quantum mechanics](quantum%20mechanics.md)}@}, since {@{the differential equations discussed}@} assume that one can specify {@{the exact position and momentum of the particle simultaneously at any point in time}@}. However, {@{the equations can be further generalized}@} to then be extended to {@{apply to quantum mechanics as well as to classical mechanics}@}, through {@{the deformation of the [Poisson algebra](Poisson%20algebra.md) over _p_ and _q_}@} to {@{the algebra of [Moyal brackets](Moyal%20bracket.md)}@}. <!--SR:!fsrs,2026-08-25T19:01:50.244Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T19:01:50.244Z!fsrs,2026-08-25T21:18:59.630Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T21:18:59.630Z!fsrs,2026-08-25T20:24:37.845Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T20:24:37.845Z!fsrs,2026-08-25T19:09:33.456Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T19:09:33.456Z!fsrs,2026-08-25T20:32:23.587Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T20:32:23.587Z!fsrs,2026-08-25T19:39:26.540Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T19:39:26.540Z!fsrs,2026-08-25T09:12:15.392Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T09:12:15.392Z!fsrs,2026-08-25T21:20:53.583Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T21:20:53.583Z!fsrs,2026-08-25T21:09:13.586Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-08-17T21:09:13.586Z-->

Specifically, {@{the more general form of the Hamilton's equation}@} reads {@{$${\frac {\mathrm {d} f}{\mathrm {d} t} }=\left\{f,{\mathcal {H} }\right\}+{\frac {\partial f}{\partial t} },$$}@} where {@{_f_ is some function of _p_ and _q_, and H is the Hamiltonian}@}. To find out {@{the rules for evaluating a [Poisson bracket](Poisson%20bracket.md)}@} without {@{resorting to differential equations}@}, see {@{_[Lie algebra](Lie%20algebra.md)_}@}; {@{a Poisson bracket}@} is the name for {@{the Lie bracket in a [Poisson algebra](Poisson%20algebra.md)}@}. {@{These Poisson brackets}@} can then be extended to {@{[Moyal brackets](Moyal%20bracket.md)}@} comporting to {@{an inequivalent Lie algebra, as proven by [Hilbrand J. Groenewold](Hilbrand%20J.%20Groenewold.md)}@}, and thereby describe {@{quantum mechanical diffusion in phase space}@} \(See {@{_[Phase space formulation](phase%20space%20formulation.md)_ and _[Wigner–Weyl transform](Wigner–Weyl%20transform.md)_}@}\). {@{This more algebraic approach}@} not only permits ultimately extending {@{[probability distributions](probability%20distribution.md) in [phase space](phase%20space.md)}@} to {@{[Wigner quasi-probability distributions](Wigner%20quasi-probability%20distribution.md)}@}, but, at {@{the mere Poisson bracket classical setting}@}, also provides more power in helping analyze {@{the relevant [conserved quantities](conserved%20quantity.md) in a system}@}.
