# anti-symmetric operator

(annotation: This article is {@{incoherent}@} and describes {@{raising and lowering operators instead}@}. There is no such thing as {@{an "anti-symmetric operator" in quantum mechanics}@}. {@{The incoherent parts}@} have been {@{commented out}@}.)

In {@{[quantum mechanics](quantum%20mechanics.md)}@}, {@{a __raising__ or __lowering operator__ \(collectively known as [ladder operators](ladder%20operators.md)\)}@} is {@{an [operator](operator%20(mathematics).md) that increases or decreases the [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) of another operator}@}. In {@{quantum mechanics}@}, {@{the raising operator}@} is sometimes called {@{the [creation operator](creation%20operator.md)}@}, and {@{the lowering operator the [annihilation operator](annihilation%20operator.md)}@}. {@{Well-known applications of ladder operators}@} in quantum mechanics are in {@{the formalisms of the [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum%20harmonic%20oscillator.md) and [angular momentum](angular%20momentum.md)}@}.

## introduction

This operator, similar to spin in non-relativistic [quantum mechanics](quantum%20mechanics.md) is a __ladder operator__ that can create two [fermions](fermions.md) of opposite spin out of a [boson](boson.md) or a [boson](boson.md) from two [fermions](fermions.md). --> {@{A [Fermion](fermion.md), named after Enrico Fermi}@}, is {@{a particle with a [half-integer](half-integer.md) spin, such as electrons and protons}@}. This is {@{a matter particle}@}. {@{A [boson](boson.md), named after [S. N. Bose](S.%20N.%20Bose.md)}@}, is {@{a particle with full integer spin, such as photons and W's}@}. This is {@{a force carrying particle}@}.

## spin

First, we will review {@{spin for non-relativistic quantum mechanics}@}. {@{Spin, an intrinsic property similar to angular momentum}@}, is defined by {@{a spin operator __S__}@} that plays {@{a role on a system similar to the operator __L__ for orbital angular momentum}@}. {@{The operators $S^{2}$ and $S_{z}$ (annotation: $S^2$ measures total spin magnitude squared, while $S_z$ measures its projection along the $z$-axis.)}@} whose eigenvalues are {@{$S^{2}|s,m\rangle =s(s+1)\hbar ^{2}|s,m\rangle$}@} and {@{$S_{z}|s,m\rangle =m\hbar |s,m\rangle$}@} respectively. These formalisms also obey {@{the usual commutation relations for angular momentum}@} {@{$[S_{x},S_{y}]=i\hbar S_{z}$, $[S_{y},S_{z}]=i\hbar S_{x}$, and $[S_{z},S_{x}]=i\hbar S_{y}$}@}. {@{The raising and lowering operators, $S_{+}$ and $S_{-}$}@}, are defined as {@{$S_{+}=S_{x}+i\cdot S_{y}$ and $S_{-}=S_{x}-i\cdot S_{y}$}@} respectively. {@{These ladder operators act on the state}@} in the following {@{$S_{+}|s,m\rangle =\hbar {\sqrt {s(s+1)-m(m+1)} }|s,m+1\rangle$}@} and {@{$S_{-}|s,m\rangle =\hbar {\sqrt {s(s+1)-m(m-1)} }|s,m-1\rangle$}@} respectively. (annotation: Expanding {@{$S_\mp S_\pm = (S_x \mp i S_y)(S_x \pm i S_y) = S_x^2 + S_y^2 \pm i [S_x, S_y]$}@} using {@{$[S_x, S_y] = i\hbar S_z$ and $S^2 = S_x^2 + S_y^2 + S_z^2$}@} gives {@{$S_\mp S_\pm = S^2 - S_z^2 \mp \hbar S_z$}@}. Setting {@{$S_\pm \vert{}s,m\rangle = C_\pm \vert{}s, m \pm 1\rangle$}@}, {@{the norm squared}@} is {@{$\vert{}C_\pm\vert{}^2 = \langle s,m\vert{} S_\mp S_\pm \vert{}s,m\rangle = \langle s,m\vert{} (S^2 - S_z^2 \mp \hbar S_z) \vert{}s,m\rangle$}@}. Evaluating {@{the operators on the eigenstate $\vert{}s,m\rangle$}@} yields {@{$\vert{}C_\pm\vert{}^2 = \hbar^2 s(s+1) - \hbar^2 m^2 \mp \hbar^2 m = \hbar^2 [s(s+1) - m(m \pm 1)]$}@}. Taking {@{the standard positive square root}@} yields {@{$C_\pm = \hbar \sqrt{s(s+1) - m(m \pm 1)}$}@}, establishing {@{$S_\pm \vert{}s,m\rangle = \hbar \sqrt{s(s+1) - m(m \pm 1)} \vert{}s, m \pm 1\rangle$}@}.)

{@{The operators S\_x and S\_y}@} can be {@{determined using the ladder method}@}. In {@{the case of the spin 1/2 case \(fermion\)}@}, {@{the operator $S_{+}$ acting on a state}@} produces {@{$S_{+}|+\rangle =0$ and $S_{+}|-\rangle =\hbar |+\rangle$}@}. Likewise, {@{the operator $S_{-}$ acting on a state}@} produces {@{$S_{-}|-\rangle =0$ and $S_{-}|+\rangle =\hbar |-\rangle$}@}. {@{The matrix representations of these operators}@} are constructed as follows: {@{$$[S_{+}]={\begin{bmatrix}\langle +|S_{+}|+\rangle &\langle +|S_{+}|-\rangle \\\langle -|S_{+}|+\rangle &\langle -|S_{+}|-\rangle \end{bmatrix} }=\hbar \cdot {\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix} }$$}@} <br/> {@{$$[S_{-}]={\begin{bmatrix}\langle +|S_{-}|+\rangle &\langle +|S_{-}|-\rangle \\\langle -|S_{-}|+\rangle &\langle -|S_{-}|-\rangle \end{bmatrix} }=\hbar \cdot {\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix} }$$}@} Therefore, {@{$S_{x}$ and $S_{y}$}@} can be {@{represented by the matrix representations}@}: (annotation: Note that {@{$S_+ = S_x + i \cdot S_y$ and $S_- = S_x - i \cdot S_y$}@} gives {@{$S_x = \frac {S_+ + S_-} 2$ and $S_y = \frac {S_+ - S_-} {2i}$}@}.) {@{$$[S_{x}]={\frac {\hbar }{2} }\cdot {\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix} }$$}@} <br/> {@{$$[S_{y}]={\frac {\hbar }{2} }\cdot {\begin{bmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{bmatrix} }$$}@}

Recalling {@{the generalized uncertainty relation for two operators A and B}@}, {@{$\Delta _{\psi }A\,\Delta _{\psi }B\geq {\frac {1}{2} }\left|\left\langle \left[{A},{B}\right]\right\rangle _{\psi }\right|$}@}, we can immediately see that {@{the uncertainty relation of the operators $S_{x}$ and $S_{y}$}@} are as follows: {@{$$\Delta _{\psi }S_{x}\,\Delta _{\psi }S_{y}\geq {\frac {1}{2} }\left|\left\langle \left[{S_{x} },{S_{y} }\right]\right\rangle _{\psi }\right|={\frac {1}{2} }(i\hbar S_{z})={\frac {\hbar }{2} }S_{z}$$}@} (annotation: Directly evaluating {@{the commutator using the given matrix representations}@} yields {@{$[S_x][S_y] - [S_y][S_x] = \frac{\hbar^2}{4} \begin{bmatrix} i & 0 \\ 0 & -i \end{bmatrix} - \frac{\hbar^2}{4} \begin{bmatrix} -i & 0 \\ 0 & i \end{bmatrix} = i\hbar \cdot \frac{\hbar}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = i\hbar S_z$}@}. {@{This explicit matrix calculation}@} establishes {@{the operator identity $[S_x, S_y] = i\hbar S_z$}@}.) Therefore, like {@{orbital angular momentum}@}, we can {@{only specify one coordinate at a time}@}. We specify {@{the operators $S^{2}$ and $S_{z}$}@}. (annotation: Because {@{no two spatial components commute}@}, thus they {@{do not share a common set of eigenvectors}@}, we can pick {@{at most one projection (conventionally $S_z$) to define our measurement axis}@}. To completely {@{describe the spin state}@}, quantum mechanics requires {@{a complete set of commuting observables}@}: {@{a maximal group of mutually commuting operators}@} whose {@{eigenvalues uniquely identify every quantum state}@}. We add {@{$S^2$ because it commutes with every component ($[S^2, S_z] = 0$)}@} and fixes {@{the total spin magnitude}@}. The set is {@{strictly limited to exactly _two operators_}@} because {@{$S^2$ and $S_z$ together fully distinguish every basis state}@} in {@{the $(2s+1)$-dimensional spin space using quantum numbers $s$ and $m_s$}@}. {@{Any other candidate operator in this space}@} is either {@{non-commuting with $S_z$ or mathematically redundant as a function of $S^2$ and $S_z$}@}.)

### bosons

Characterized by {@{their spin, a [bosonic field](bosonic%20field.md)}@} can be {@{scalar fields, vector fields and even tensor fields}@}. To illustrate, {@{the electromagnetic field quantized}@} is {@{the photon field}@}, which can be {@{quantized using conventional methods of canonical or path integral quantization}@}. This has led to the theory of {@{[quantum electrodynamics](quantum%20electrodynamics.md), arguably the most successful theory in physics}@}. {@{The graviton field}@} is {@{the quantized gravitational field}@}. There is yet to be {@{a theory that quantizes the gravitational field}@}, but theories such as {@{string theory can be thought of the gravitational field quantized}@}. {@{An example of a non-relativistic [bosonic field](bosonic%20field.md)}@} is that describing {@{cold bosonic atoms, such as Helium-4}@}. {@{Free bosonic fields}@} obey {@{commutation relations}@}: <p> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; {@{$$[a_{i},a_{j}]=[a_{i}^{\dagger },a_{j}^{\dagger }]=0$$}@} <br/> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; {@{$$[a_{i},a_{i}^{\dagger }]=\langle f|g\rangle\,,$$}@} (annotation: Here, {@{$a_i$ and $a_j^\dagger$}@} are {@{annihilation and creation operators for bosons in single-particle wavefunctions (mode states) $f$ and $g$}@}, where {@{$\langle f \vert{} g \rangle$ is their spatial overlap (inner product)}@}. {@{$[a_i, a_j] = [a_i^\dagger, a_j^\dagger] = 0$}@} shows that {@{same-type operations commute due to bosonic exchange symmetry}@}, while {@{$[a_i, a_j^\dagger] = \langle f \vert{} g \rangle$}@} equates {@{the non-zero commutator directly to the overlap of modes $f$ and $g$}@}.)

To illustrate, suppose we have {@{a system of N bosons that occupy mutually orthogonal single-particle states}@} {@{$|\phi _{1}\rangle ,|\phi _{2}\rangle ,|\phi _{3}\rangle$, etc.}@} Using {@{the usual representation}@}, we demonstrate the system by assigning {@{a state to each particle and then imposing exchange symmetry}@}. {@{$${\frac {1}{\sqrt {3} } }\left[|\phi _{1}\rangle |\phi _{2}\rangle |\phi _{3}\rangle +|\phi _{2}\rangle |\phi _{1}\rangle |\phi _{3}\rangle +|\phi _{3}\rangle |\phi _{2}\rangle |\phi _{1}\rangle \right].$$}@} {@{This wave equation}@} can be represented using {@{a second quantized approach, known as [second quantization](second%20quantization.md)}@}. {@{The number of particles in each single-particle state}@} is listed. {@{$$|1,2,0,0,0,\cdots \rangle ,$$}@}

{@{The [creation and annihilation operators](creation%20and%20annihilation%20operators.md)}@}, which {@{add and subtract particles from multi-particle states}@}. {@{These creation and annihilation operators}@} are very {@{similar to those defined for the [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum%20harmonic%20oscillator.md)}@}, which {@{added and subtracted energy quanta}@}. However, these operators {@{literally create and annihilate particles with a given quantum state}@}. {@{The bosonic annihilation operator $a_{2}$ and creation operator $a_{2}^{\dagger }$}@} have the following effects: <p> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; {@{$$a_{2}|N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},\cdots \rangle ={\sqrt {N_{2} } }\mid N_{1},(N_{2}-1),N_{3},\cdots \rangle ,$$}@} <br/> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; {@{$$a_{2}^{\dagger }|N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},\cdots \rangle ={\sqrt {N_{2}+1} }\mid N_{1},(N_{2}+1),N_{3},\cdots \rangle .$$}@}
