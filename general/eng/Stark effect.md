{@{The __Stark effect__}@} is {@{the shifting and splitting of [spectral lines](spectral%20line.md) of atoms and molecules due to the presence of an external [electric field](electric%20field.md)}@}. It is {@{the electric-field analogue of the [Zeeman effect](Zeeman%20effect.md)}@}, where {@{a spectral line is split into several components due to the presence of the [magnetic field](magnetic%20field.md)}@}. Although {@{initially coined for the static case}@}, it is also used in {@{the wider context to describe the effect of time-dependent electric fields}@}. In particular, {@{the Stark effect}@} is responsible for {@{the [pressure broadening](spectral%20line.md#pressure%20broadening) \(Stark broadening\) of spectral lines}@} by {@{charged particles in [plasmas](plasma%20(physics).md)}@}. ## mechanism

### overview

Imagine {@{an atom with occupied 2s and 2p [electron states](electron%20configuration.md)}@}. In {@{the [Bohr model](Bohr%20model.md)}@}, these states are {@{[degenerate](degenerate%20energy%20levels.md)}@}. However, in {@{the presence of an external electric field}@}, {@{these electron orbitals}@} will {@{[hybridize](orbital%20hybridisation.md) into eigenstates of the [perturbed Hamiltonian](perturbation%20theory%20(quantum%20mechanics).md)}@} \(where {@{each perturbed hybrid state}@} can be written as {@{a superposition of unperturbed states}@}\). Since {@{the 2s and 2p states have opposite [parity](parity%20(physics).md)}@}, {@{these hybrid states}@} will {@{lack inversion symmetry and will possess a time-averaged electric dipole moment}@}. If this {@{dipole moment is aligned with the electric field}@}, {@{the energy of the state will shift down}@}; if this {@{dipole moment is anti-aligned with the electric field}@}, {@{the energy of the state will shift up}@}. Thus, {@{the Stark effect}@} causes {@{a splitting of the original degeneracy}@}. <!--SR:!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z-->

### multipole expansion

- Main article: ::@:: [Multipole expansion](multipole%20expansion.md) <!--SR:!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z-->

{@{The Stark effect originates}@} from {@{the interaction between a [charge](electric%20charge.md) distribution \(atom or molecule\) and an external [electric field](electric%20field.md)}@}. {@{The interaction energy $V_{\mathrm {int} }$}@} of {@{a continuous charge distribution $\rho (\mathbf {r} )$, confined within a finite volume ${\mathcal {V} }$}@}, with {@{an external [electrostatic potential](electrostatic.md#electrostatic%20potential) $\phi (\mathbf {r} )$}@} is {@{$$V_{\mathrm {int} }=\int \limits _{\mathcal {V} }\phi (\mathbf {r} )\rho (\mathbf {r} )\;\mathrm {d} ^{3}\mathbf {r}$$}@} This expression is {@{valid [classically](classical%20physics.md) and quantum-mechanically alike}@}. If {@{the potential varies weakly over the charge distribution}@}, {@{the [multipole expansion](multipole%20expansion.md) converges fast}@}, so only {@{a few first terms give an accurate approximation}@}. Namely, keeping {@{only the zeroth- and first-order terms}@}, {@{$$\phi (\mathbf {r} )\approx \phi (\mathbf {0} )-\sum _{i=1}^{3}F_{i}\,r_{i}$$}@} where we introduced {@{the electric field $F_{i}\equiv -\left.\left({\frac {\partial \phi }{\partial r_{i} } }\right)\right|_{\mathbf {r=0} }$}@} and assumed {@{the origin $\mathbf {0}$ to be somewhere within ${\mathcal {V} }$}@}. As stated, {@{the quadratic Stark effect}@} is described by {@{second-order perturbation theory}@}. {@{The zeroth-order [eigenproblem](eigenvalues%20and%20eigenvectors.md)}@} {@{$$H^{(0)}\psi _{k}^{0}=E_{k}^{(0)}\psi _{k}^{0},\quad k=0,1,\ldots ,\quad E_{0}^{(0)}<E_{1}^{(0)}\leq E_{2}^{(0)},\dots$$}@} is {@{assumed to be solved}@}. {@{The perturbation theory}@} gives {@{$$E_{k}^{(2)}=\sum _{k'\neq k}{\frac {\langle \psi _{k}^{0}|V_{\mathrm {int} }|\psi _{k^{\prime } }^{0}\rangle \langle \psi _{k'}^{0}|V_{\mathrm {int} }|\psi _{k}^{0}\rangle }{E_{k}^{(0)}-E_{k'}^{(0)} } }\equiv -{\frac {1}{2} }\sum _{i,j=1}^{3}\alpha _{ij}F_{i}F_{j}$$}@} (annotation: {@{Perturbation theory}@} treats {@{perturbed states as superpositions of unperturbed states}@}, where {@{the interaction potential $V_{\text{int} }$}@} mixes {@{unperturbed reference states $\psi_k^0$ with all other unperturbed states $\psi_{k'}^0$}@} via {@{first-order mixing coefficients given by $c_{k'}^{(1)} = \frac{\langle\psi_{k'}^0\vert{}V_{\text{int} }\vert{}\psi_k^0\rangle}{E_k^{(0)} - E_{k'}^{(0)} }$}@}. Substituting {@{this state superposition back into the energy expectation}@} yields {@{the second-order energy shift $E_k^{(2)} = \sum_{k' \neq k} \frac{\langle\psi_k^0\vert{}V_{\text{int} }\vert{}\psi_{k'}^0\rangle\langle\psi_{k'}^0\vert{}V_{\text{int} }\vert{}\psi_k^0\rangle}{E_k^{(0)} - E_{k'}^{(0)} }$}@}, where {@{the numerator's product of transition matrix elements}@} quantifies {@{the squared coupling strength between states}@}, and {@{the energy denominator ($E_k^{(0)} - E_{k'}^{(0)}$)}@} inversely scales {@{the mixing effect based on unperturbed level spacing}@}.) with {@{the components of the [polarizability tensor](polarizability.md) α}@} defined by {@{$$\alpha _{ij}=-2\sum _{k'\neq k}{\frac {\langle \psi _{k}^{0}|\mu _{i}|\psi _{k'}^{0}\rangle \langle \psi _{k'}^{0}|\mu _{j}|\psi _{k}^{0}\rangle }{E_{k}^{(0)}-E_{k'}^{(0)} } }.$$}@} (annotation: {@{This tensor definition}@} arises directly from expanding {@{the dot product in the interaction potential $V_{\text{int} } = -\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{F} = -\sum_{i} \mu_i F_i$}@}.) {@{The energy _E_<sup>\(2\)</sup>}@} gives {@{the quadratic Stark effect}@}. #### problems

{@{The perturbative treatment of the Stark effect}@} has {@{some problems}@}. In {@{the presence of an electric field}@}, {@{states of atoms and molecules that were previously bound \([square-integrable](square-integrable.md)\)}@}, become {@{formally \(non-square-integrable\) [resonances](resonance.md) of finite width}@}. {@{These resonances}@} may {@{decay in finite time via field ionization}@}. For {@{low lying states and not too strong fields}@} the decay times are {@{so long, however, that for all practical purposes the system can be regarded as bound}@}. For {@{highly excited states and/or very strong fields}@} {@{ionization may have to be accounted for}@}. \(See also {@{the article on the [Rydberg atom](Rydberg%20atom.md)}@}\).<sup>\[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation%20needed)_\]</sup> (annotation: {@{The unperturbed Coulomb potential}@} confines {@{electrons in an infinitely deep well}@} because it {@{approaches zero asymptotically only as $r \to \infty$}@}, keeping all {@{bound states at strictly negative energies ($E < 0$) with an infinite barrier preventing escape}@}. When {@{an external electric field introduces a linear potential term $-eFz$}@}, it tilts {@{the total potential profile downward at large distances}@}, turning {@{the infinite confining wall into a finite potential barrier}@}. {@{This finite barrier}@} enables {@{the electron to escape via quantum mechanical tunneling (field ionization)}@}. Consequently, {@{true square-integrable bound states ($\psi \in L^2$)}@} transform into {@{non-square-integrable quasi-bound resonances}@} characterized by {@{a complex energy $E = E_r - i\Gamma/2$}@}, where {@{the imaginary width $\Gamma$}@} dictates {@{the finite decay lifetime $\tau = \hbar/\Gamma$}@}.)
