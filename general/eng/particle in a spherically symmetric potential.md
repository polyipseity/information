In {@{[quantum mechanics](quantum%20mechanics.md)}@}, {@{a __particle in a spherically symmetric potential__}@} is {@{a system where a particle's [potential energy](potential%20energy.md) depends only on its distance from a central point, not on the direction}@}. This model is {@{fundamental to physics}@} because it can be used to describe {@{a wide range of real-world phenomena}@}, from {@{the behavior of a single electron in a [hydrogen atom](hydrogen%20atom.md) to the approximate structure of [atomic nuclei](atomic%20nucleus.md)}@}.<sup>[\[1\]](#^ref-1)</sup> <!--SR:!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z-->

{@{The particle's behavior}@} is described by {@{the [time-independent Schrödinger equation](Schrödinger%20equation.md)}@}. Because of {@{the spherical symmetry}@}, {@{the problem can be greatly simplified}@} by using {@{[spherical coordinates](spherical%20coordinate%20system.md) \($r$, $\theta$ and $\phi$\)}@} and {@{a mathematical technique}@} called {@{[separation of variables](separation%20of%20variables.md)}@}. ## structure of the eigenfunctions

If solved by {@{[separation of variables](separation%20of%20variables.md)}@}, {@{the eigenstates of the [system](system.md)}@} will have the form: {@{$$\psi (r,\theta ,\phi )=R(r)\Theta (\theta )\Phi (\phi )$$}@} in which {@{the [spherical angles](spherical%20angle.md#angle) $\theta$ and $\phi$}@} represent {@{the [polar](colatitude.md) and [azimuthal](azimuth.md) angle}@}, respectively. {@{Those two factors of $\psi$}@} are often {@{grouped together as [spherical harmonics](spherical%20harmonics.md)}@}, so that {@{the [eigenfunctions](eigenfunction.md) take the form}@}: {@{$$\psi (r,\theta ,\phi )=R(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta ,\phi ).$$}@} {@{The differential equation which characterises the function $R(r)$}@} is called {@{the __radial equation__}@}.<sup>[\[2\]](#^ref-2)</sup> <!--SR:!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z!fsrs,2026-11-18T00:00:00.000Z,8,8.2956,1,2,1,0,0,2026-11-10T00:00:00.000Z-->

It's defined {@{\(non-dimensionally\) in spherical coordinates}@} as {@{$${\hat {L} }^{2}\equiv -\left({\frac {1}{\sin \theta } }{\frac {\partial }{\partial \theta } }\left(\sin \theta {\frac {@{$${\hat {L} }^{2}Y_{\ell m}(\theta ,\phi )=\ell (\ell +1)Y_{\ell m}(\theta ,\phi ).$$}@} {@{Substituting this into the [Schrödinger equation](Schrödinger%20equation.md)}@} we get {@{a ## spherically symmetric Hamiltonians

Since {@{the Hamiltonian is spherically symmetric}@}, it is said to be {@{invariant under rotation}@}, i.e.: {@{${U(R)}^{\dagger }{\hat {H} }U(R)={\hat {H} }$}@} (annotation: Interpreted, this means {@{the unrotated Hamiltonian}@} is {@{the same as applying a rotation $R$ to the system, then applying the Hamiltonian, and then rotating back by $R^{-1}$}@}. {@{$U(R)$}@} is the {@{unitary operator corresponding to the rotation $R$}@}.) Since {@{[angular momentum](angular%20momentum.md) operators are generators of rotation}@}, (annotation: That means {@{$U(R)$}@} can be expressed as {@{$U(R)=e^{-i{\hat {\mathbf {n} } }\cdot \mathbf 1. The wavefunction must be regular at the origin.
2. The wavefunction and its derivative must be continuous at the potential discontinuity.
3. The wavefunction must converge at infinity.
